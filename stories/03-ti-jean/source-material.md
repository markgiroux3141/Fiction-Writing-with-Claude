## M1 — The parish, and the boy

**The hat.** His aunt made them. She took a child's hat, got the band out of it, cut it down
and worked it round again; an evening's work, and she never made anything of it. His shoes were
a child's shoes and had to be sent for. His coat was a man's coat, because a coat does not have
to fit. Everybody in the parish had said something nice about that hat at one time or another.
It was a good place to put your eyes and it was always there.

**Ask his aunt.** Ovide leaned over the counter and asked his aunt whether the boy would want a
peppermint. Ti-Jean was at her elbow and had asked for it himself, out loud, a moment before.

**Léonide.** Elmire's cousin, twenty-two. A long birth and a bad one; the doctor came out on the
second morning to find there was nothing to do that had not been done; she lived four days
after. There is a way of telling it in that family in which the child did it to her. It has
never been said out loud where Elmire could hear. Everybody in that house has heard it. He went
to his aunt because his aunt had the room.

**Come see the lamb.** He went for the figure every time he was taken out there. Not fast. He
would be beside you, and then four steps off and going up the middle of the path, the way a
child goes at a dog. Elmire's hand was on his shoulder before she had thought about it, and she
turned him and said *come on now, Ti-Jean, come see the lamb*, and he came. He was easy and he
always came. He did not look back at it, which she noticed and did not think about until much
later, because a child hauled off a dog looks back. She set his hand on the lamb and he was
pleased with it, and she stood there with her heart going for no reason she would have given,
and got her breath, and went back to the coping.

## M2 — Her own ground

**Where she walks faster.** There is a stretch of the shell path where she walks faster. She
knows where it begins and could put her hand on the piece of shell it begins at. She has never
slowed down through there, and is not going to start, because slowing down would be a thing she
had decided to do, and walking fast is not a thing anybody decides. She has a great deal to get
through in a day. Her mother walked fast.

**Nobody saw it go up.** Nobody could tell you when it came, which is not the same as nobody
knowing. Asked sideways at a wedding and at a burial, people gave the same shape of answer both
times: that it had always been there — and then a pause, and then the weather. Madame Dugas was
ninety-one and could give you the price of hogs in any year you named; she said she believed it
had gone up around the time they lost Léonide, then sat a moment and said she did not know why
she had said that, and asked after Elmire's boy.

**Ovide's route.** He had taken the short way through the cemetery to the store his whole life,
and he went round by the road now. In between there had been a while when he still cut through
and walked it faster than he used to, and if you had asked him he would have said the shell was
bad in there.

**Maman.** He called the figure maman. He called Father Ancelet's housekeeper maman and she was
seventy and she liked it. He called Elmire's mother maman. He called the mare maman, and
Elmire's brother thought that was the best thing he had ever heard and used to get him to do it
in front of company. So it was nothing. It was a word he had, and he put it on things he was
fond of.

**The dream.** *(Mark's idea; converted from first person — the gladness is the whole of it.)*
There was nothing bad in it. She went up the middle of the path at her own pace, not hurrying,
on a bright morning with the shell white and nothing in her hands, and she put her hand on the
face the way he does, and it was easy, and she was glad. That was the part she could not get
round. In the dream it was her own gladness. She woke sitting straight up with her heart going
and her husband asking her what, what, what. She told him a dog. She had never in her life
dreamed about a dog.

## M3 — What she does about it

*Two failed attempts, the mundane explanation killed once, then the drapery. Written fresh; the
only pre-approved beat here is F7.*

**F7 — the one who looked.** Father Ancelet looked at Ti-Jean. Straight at him, the whole time
he was speaking to him, the way you look at anybody at all. Elmire watched him do it at a burial
and found she was angry about it, and got hold of that and turned it over, and could not find
anybody to be angry at.

**The drapery — the story's one impossible thing.** Three dry days, the whole ground drying, the
copings pale from the edges in because the edges are thin. The drapery dark with water from the
knee down, the way a hem goes dark, and hanging. **Stone does not hang.** Two sentences and out.
She says nothing about it to anybody, that day or after.

## M4 — The Saturday before All Saints'

Forty people in that ground by nine, buckets and brushes and their own dead to see to. Elmire
three rows up doing her coping with her back to the middle of the ground, which is how the north
row runs. She straightened to get the ache out and saw him go by on the shell path, going up the
middle of it at his own pace with his hat on.

She had the brush in her hand. Ovide was in there and his aunt was in there and Father Ancelet
was in there, and the girl who was eleven was in there somewhere with the flowers, and any one
of them was nearer to that path than Elmire was.

She turned round and finished the coping. It took her the best part of an hour and she did it
properly, and afterwards she could not have said one thing about doing it.

## M5 — After

**D9 — the hand.** Ovide found him and Théo Landry was with him. Neither man ever said what they
saw, then or after, and both were asked by people with more right to ask than Elmire had. She
stood in Ovide's yard that evening and would not go until he gave her something, and he gave her
one thing, looking at the fence the whole time: *they could not get his hand down.* She never
asked either of them another question about it.

**D1 — no viewing.** The box was closed at the house and it stayed closed at the church. For a
boy of that age, dead out of doors on a clear morning, with no cart and no water and no
machinery anywhere in it, that is a thing a parish notices. Nobody asked. His aunt said, to two
or three people, that he had never liked being looked at.

**The hat on the plinth.** The sexton had seen him get there twice, years before, on days when
nobody was minding, and had come out of the shed and led him off himself. He told Elmire at the
wake, because it was the only thing he had that was worth anything to anybody: the boy went up
and set his hat on the plinth first, the way a man sets his hat down coming into a house, and
then went on. His hat was on the plinth when they found him. So he had not been in a hurry. He
had put his hat down, which takes a moment, and which nobody does if there is anything at all
the matter. That was the part Elmire could not put anywhere. Not what had been wrong with him
afterwards. **That he had been comfortable.**

**The burial.** The aunt has no money and the family ground is full, and the unowned plot is
right there and costs almost nothing, and the parish makes it easy without anybody proposing it.
Nobody objects. To object you would have to say why.

**The ending — door left open.** All Saints', the following year. A small child breaks from a
group and goes up the middle of the path. Elmire's hand is on his shoulder before she has
thought about it, and she turns him, and says come on now. Flat, short, and nothing made of it.

## Approved, but with no home in this skeleton

Left out rather than forced in. Cramming them is the likeliest way this draft goes wrong.

- **The sole** (batch 1 #1) — needs somebody to go close, and nobody in this story does.
- **The rake** (batch 2 #1) — the All Saints' tools beat. Possible in M4 in a clause, but M4 is the shortest movement on purpose and this would slow it.
- **His name / how does anybody know** (batch 2 #4) — a second child asking a second flat question. One child question is the instrument; two is a device.
- **The quiet stretch** (batch 2 #11) — the sexton hearing families go silent. It is a *crowd* consistency, which L-016 basis 4 now bars.
- **Wings forward, the dry ground** (batch 2 #10) — a second physical anomaly. The story has one.
- **The toes, the whitewashed plinth, the monument man, the colour of its hair, the shears** — all good, none of them this story's.
