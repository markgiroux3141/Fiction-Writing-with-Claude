# Ti-Jean *(working)*

## Title candidates — SETTLED

**The Long Way Round**, chosen by Mark 2026-09-10. Superseded list: *Come See the Lamb* · *The Short Way Through* · *A Turn* · *He Had Not Been
in a Hurry* · *The Unowned Plot*. The title should not name the figure.

## The one impossible thing

**The drapery hangs.** One sentence, in movement 3, immediately after Elmire has been told
there is nothing there. Seen by her alone; never mentioned by her to anybody, including
herself, for the rest of the story. That is the entire budget (§2).

Everything else is people, and the mundane explanation — a boy with a weak heart wandered
off and died, in a parish that was already odd about a statue — is deliberately available
and unsatisfying. It is killed **once**, in one exchange in movement 3, and never argued
with again (L-018).

## What must not happen

- The figure never acts, never changes, never responds to anything anybody does. Anything it *does* is a fact about it, and enough facts are a mechanism (L-016).
- **No crowd ever obeys the avoidance at once.** One person at a time, deniable, overridable in principle. A demonstrated consistency is a mechanism — L-016 basis 4, and the reason the earlier graveside scene was scrapped.
- Ti-Jean is never found in the figure's posture, and the body is never described. Story 01 spent arms-out, and a corpse echoing the statue answers *what was it* in one picture.
- No clinical description of his deformity, ever. Rendered by what the world arranges around it and nothing else.
- The family is never cruel. Tired, ordinary, stretched.
- The child in the last beat is not Elmire's own — that converts the story to inheritance, which is a meaning.
- Nobody says *maman* is significant. Nobody dates the figure. Nobody explains the wings.

## Geography — settled at draft, 2026-09-10 (L-009 clause 3)

Draft 01 put the figure on the plot **beside** Elmire's, which made *up the middle of the
path* point at nothing and cost the movement its one violation. Revised, and binding on
movements 2–5:

- The shell path runs from the gate **straight up the middle to the back wall**. It ends
  at the unowned plot, which is at its head, against the wall, with the figure on it. The
  path goes nowhere else, so walking up the middle of it is going to the one place.
- **Elmire's plot is in the north row, four plots down from the top** — near it, not
  adjoining it. She works with her back to the path, which is how the north row runs (M4),
  and she straightens and sees him go by (M4) without being the nearest person to the path.
- **The stretch where she walks faster (M2) is the last stretch**, where the path stops
  being packed shell and goes loose.
- The wound is unaffected: Ti-Jean is buried in the unowned plot at the head of the path,
  which is still the one place in that ground she has spent her life not standing.

## Also settled at draft

- **His slowness is named once, in the parish's word: *simple*.** 1905's clinical register
  (*feeble-minded*, *imbecile*) is barred as clinical and cruel-reading; *dullard* is
  period but a judging, schoolroom word. **Open to Mark:** the Louisiana French *innocent*
  — simple-minded, said without malice — in Azélie's mouth instead of or alongside it.
- **The masonry is her oddity and the story says so.** Plot owners kept their own plots;
  scrubbing, whitewashing and flowers were women's work, brickwork generally was not. She
  does the ordinary work; the one north corner of brick is hers because her father laid it
  and she carried his bucket. Azélie tells her to get a man to do it and she does not
  answer. (D-005: specification as a craft move, earning its place in the scene.)

## The rake beat — corrected 2026-09-10 (L-024, over-application clause)

Mark: *"When I read the line about her putting the rake down and then picking it up, it was
just confusing."* Two faults, one after the other, both mine:

1. **The setup was in M4, a movement after the image.** L-009 clause 2 — the enabling fact
   goes before the image, never after — broken in the same draft that produced the clause. I
   noticed the ordering when I placed the rake and argued past it on the grounds that M4
   would *retro-illuminate* it. Readers do not re-read.
2. **The first fix did not work.** I established only the positive habit (the copings take
   the buckets, the rakes lean on the crosses) because I read L-024's *never by anybody being
   seen to obey* as barring any statement of the avoidance. Mark, correctly: a norm about
   where things go says nothing about where things are never put.

**Now, in M3, immediately before the test:** *She had never seen anybody put anything against
the figure. Not a rake, not a coat, not a bucket. There was no rule about it, and nobody had
ever mentioned it to her, and she had never mentioned it to anybody.*

Safe under L-016 basis 4 because it is not a demonstration — one witness, no scene, no
simultaneity — and because **there was no rule about it** denies the mechanism on the page and
**the rake stands there perfectly well**. The object does nothing; the dread is entirely in her
hand coming back before she has finished thinking. Deniable, overridable in principle, and
overridden.

The M1 scenery clause stays (it is the positive half of the pair and it makes *not a rake, not
a coat, not a bucket* land against something), and M4's fuller catalogue stays as the same
habit at full volume on the one day the ground is crowded. Cut the M1 clause if it reads inert.

## Geography, second pass — settled in movement two, 2026-09-10

Also binding on movements 3–5:

- **Six rows**, running off the path on both sides, numbered from the front gate. Elmire's
  plot is **row five, north side** — one short of the back. The unowned plot is past row six
  at the head of the path, against the wall.
- **The through-route to Hébert's**: in at the front gate, up the path as far as the third
  row, out over the wall by the china tree. A quarter mile saved. This is why the shell is
  packed hard to the third row and loose past it — the trace has a boring cause, which is
  what makes it deniable (L-024), and it is also why **everybody in that parish has feet on
  that path** and therefore an opinion about it.
- **Elmire's walk-faster stretch is between the third row and her own plot** — the length of
  path nobody else's feet are on. It begins where the shell goes loose, so she can put her
  hand on the piece of shell it begins at, and so *the footing was bad in there* is true.
- **Ovide's line is the same line.** *The shell was bad in there* is his excuse and *the
  footing was bad in there* is hers, four paragraphs apart, unremarked. The join attaches to
  two people and not to the figure, so it prices rather than explains (D-004's rhyme clause).

Merged rather than separated because both are the same move — a child contradicting an
adult's benign account of what the figure is doing — so running them apart would read as the
device the discard note warns about. Together they are one exchange with an escalation: the
correction, the mother letting it go, then the consequence the child draws from it. Nothing
in it is a fact about the figure; it is a perception nobody can check, which is why it
answers nothing (D-004).

**Also placed, by omission (L-024 adjacency):** Mark's preferred version of the deference
beat — *"not laying tools on it could imply fear where a hat tip does not"*. M4 states the
general habit only, that by nine a bucket or a basket or a pair of shears is set down on
every coping in the ground because a coping is the only flat thing out there, and never
mentions the plinth again. The exception is the reader's to supply. Barred version: forty
people all declining to use it, which is the crowd.


## The child beat — corrected 2026-09-10, second pass (L-025)

Mark, on the first pass: *"some ideas are getting fragmented... the second instance where the
child simply says she wondered if grandma could see the angel back barely hits."* He was right
and the fault was mine twice over — I merged two beats that perform the same move, then split
the result across M2 and M4, which kept every word and lost the mechanism.

**Now: one unit, one place, M2, unsplit.** Elmire has Numa out there on a Sunday in June and
tells him the angel is watching over his grandmother, because that is what you say to a child
in a burying ground. He says it wasn't watching over her, it was watching **at** her. She lets
it go, the way you let a child's grammar go. He is quiet the whole way down the shell path,
and at the gate he wants to know whether his grandmother could see it back.

Three changes from the first pass, all restoring the original in
`bible/plate-explorations/attitude-of-grief-prose-01.md`:

1. **The walk is back.** *Quiet the whole way down the shell path* is the beat's engine — the
   question is frightening because a child carried it a hundred yards first.
2. **The preposition is back.** *Watching at her*, not *looking at us*. The preposition is the
   correction, and it is what makes *could she see it back* inevitable.
3. **Elmire says the pious line.** The original is first person and the adult is the narrator;
   handing it to a stranger's mother demoted Elmire to overhearing and made the unit look
   divisible. Her own son corrects her, and she has no answer, and none is written.

**Consequence for M3, and it is an improvement the first version could not have had:** Numa
asks in June whether his dead grandmother can see it back, and in September she takes him off
the path and round by the road and says nothing about it at Azélie's. Her own child, moved;
the other one, not mentioned. The order is load-bearing — the question must precede the
rerouting.

**beats.md #6 (*her hair's just in the way*) is retired, not merged.** Same move as #5 and the
weaker of the two; running both is the device the discard note warns about. Available as a
straight swap for #5 if Mark prefers it, but not alongside.

**M4 is now 376 words** and has nothing in it but the crowd, the eight o'clock sighting, the
walk past at ten, the list, and the hour she cannot account for. That is the skeleton's *least
in it* taken literally. Flagged for a decision once M5 exists, not before.
