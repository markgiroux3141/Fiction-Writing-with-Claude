## The one impossible thing

**The drapery hangs.** One sentence, once, in movement 3 — *stone does not hang* — seen by
Elmire alone and never mentioned by her to anybody, including herself. Everything else in the
story is people. That is the whole budget (§2), and it is deliberately spent immediately after
she has been told there is nothing there.

Everything else has a mundane explanation available and unsatisfying: a boy with a weak heart
wandered off and died in a cemetery, and a parish that was already odd about a statue got
odder. The story kills that explanation **once** (L-018) and never argues with it again.

## The movements

### 1 — The parish, and the boy *(~900 w)*
*Job: the mundane buys everything. §6 notice.*

- Open on ordinary work: Elmire at the coping, the shell path, the lamb, the season. Frame first (L-009).
- The figure introduced as **scenery** — the plot next to theirs, unowned, three families said to own it and none of them saying so. No emphasis, no adjectives doing work.
- Ti-Jean, by consequence only: **F2 the hat**, and **F1 ask his aunt** in the store.
- Léonide, briefly, and the version of the story in which the child did it to her, which is never said out loud where Elmire can hear.
- **Come see the lamb.** He goes for the figure; her hand is on his shoulder before she thinks; he does not look back, and a child hauled off a dog looks back. Her heart going at the lamb for no reason she would have given.

### 2 — Her own ground *(~800 w)*
*Job: the thing gets nearer to her without doing anything. §6 intrude.*

- **Where she walks faster** — hers, in third, and she does not slow down because slowing down would be a decision.
- **Nobody saw it go up.** Madame Dugas says it went up around the time they lost Léonide, then does not know why she said that, and asks after Elmire's boy. Ovide's route: through, then through-but-faster, then round by the road, and the shell is bad in there.
- **Maman**, with the mare undercutting it in the same breath.
- **The dream.** She walks up the middle of the path, unhurried, in bright morning, and puts her hand on the face, and it is easy, and she is glad. She wakes sitting up. She tells her husband a dog. Never referred to again by anybody.

### 3 — What she does about it *(~900 w)*
*Job: L-019. She fights, and the ordinary world beats her.*

- **First attempt: the aunt.** Elmire goes at it sideways over something else entirely — the boy wants watching in that ground, there is the road and there is the water. The aunt agrees with every word. Nothing changes, because nothing was actually said.
- **Second attempt: Father Ancelet.** She gets closer and hears herself getting closer and stops early. He is kind, and he answers the question she asked, which was not her question. **F7: he looks straight at Ti-Jean, the whole time, the way you look at anybody** — and she finds she is angry about it and can find nobody to be angry at.
- **The mundane explanation, killed once:** she asks the one person who could have put the figure there — or could have known — and they could not have. One exchange, no survey (L-018).
- **The drapery.** Three dry days. The whole ground drying. Two sentences and out.
- She says nothing about it to anybody. Not that day and not after.

### 4 — The Saturday before All Saints' *(~700 w)*
*Job: §6 implicate. The shortest movement and the one with the least in it.*

- Forty people, buckets and brushes, everyone with their own dead to see to.
- She straightens to get the ache out and sees him go by on the shell path, at his own pace, hat on.
- Ovide is in there. His aunt is in there. Father Ancelet is in there. The girl who is eleven is in there with the flowers. Any one of them is nearer that path than Elmire is.
- She turns round and finishes the coping properly and cannot afterwards account for a minute of it.
- **No scene at the figure.** The movement ends with her at her own plot.

### 5 — After *(~900 w)*
*Job: the aftermath instruments — flatness, cost, duration — fire here and only here.*

- **D9:** Ovide in his own yard that evening, looking at the fence. *They could not get his hand down.* She never asks either man another question.
- **D1:** closed at the house, closed at the church. His aunt says, to two or three people, that he had never liked being looked at.
- **The hat on the plinth**, told at the wake by the sexton because it is the only thing he has that is worth anything to anybody. He had put his hat down first. He had not been in a hurry. **He had been comfortable.** ← the story's centre, and it lands here.
- **The burial.** The aunt has no money and the family ground is full, and the unowned plot is right there and costs almost nothing, and the parish makes it easy without anybody proposing it. Nobody objects. To object you would have to say why.
- **The ending — the door left open.** All Saints', the following year. A small child breaks from a group and goes up the middle of the path. Elmire's hand is on his shoulder before she has thought about it, and she turns him, and says come on now.

## Wound

**Cost, not meaning (D-004).** She keeps his grave now — four times a year, in the one place in
that ground she has spent her life not standing — and she is the person who saw him go up the
path and finished her coping. She never tells anybody either fact. The second one is why she
does the first.

## Rule (off-page) — needs choosing, never written in the prose (L-008)

- It wants to be attended to, and it does not distinguish between the person who owes it and anybody else who turns up.
- It wants somewhere to be, and a plot is somewhere.
- It wants nothing at all, and it is not finished.

## Known risks, before a word is drafted

- **The ending is a repeated gesture**, and repetition at the close can read as a key rather than a continuance. It should be flat and short and nothing should be made of it.
- **Movement 5 carries four separate payloads** and is the likeliest to sprawl past its budget. Story 01 went 143 words over and every cut came out of something working.
- **The child at the end must not be Elmire's own.** That converts the story into inheritance, which is a meaning.
