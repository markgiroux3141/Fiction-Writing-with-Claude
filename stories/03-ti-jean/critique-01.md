## Notes

1. **The age must land before the hat.** *"I think we should mention that Ti-jean is 19
   sooner so that the lines about him having a childs hat hit harder (without the age he
   could just be a child)."* → L-009, second clause. Basis 1 → 2.

2. **Keep the child's shoes.** *"I think we should keep the childs shoes line instead of
   just saying they were sent for which could mean anything."* → L-003, third
   replication. Basis 2 → 3. The source material had *"His shoes were a child's shoes and
   had to be sent for"* and I compressed away the half carrying the fact.

3. **Indicate the mental slowness.** *"I think there should be some indication of his
   mental slowness. Was 'dullard' a word used back then? If not choose something
   different."* → Taken. *Dullard* is period (15th c., common in the 19th) but it is a
   judging, schoolroom word. The parish's word is **simple**; 1905's clinical register —
   *feeble-minded*, *imbecile*, *idiot* — is accurate to the year and barred here as both
   clinical and cruel-reading (`notes.md` What must not happen; L-019's modern-interior
   clause). Used once, plainly, with the rest carried by what the world arranges. The
   Louisiana French **innocent** (simple-minded, said without malice) is offered as an
   alternative in Azélie's mouth and not taken unilaterally.

4. **Question: what is the peppermint paragraph doing?** Answer: the parish transacts
   *over* him to whoever has charge of him while he is standing there having already
   spoken — which is the habit that kills him in M4, where forty people each assume he is
   with somebody. It was also carrying the slowness implicitly, which it no longer needs
   to. Trimmed from six clauses to three; *"and that was the whole of it"* was padding.
   **The question is itself a finding** — a passage a reader has to ask about is not doing
   its job visibly. → `revision-protocol.md` §2 gains the signal.

5. **Question: does the family work in the graveyard, and did women do masonry?** Plot
   owners maintained their own plots; the sexton keeps paths and wall only. That is the
   custom M4 rests on. Scrubbing, whitewashing and flowers were women's work and well
   documented; **masonry was not** — relaying brick would normally be a man or a hired
   brick man. Fixed by making the oddity visible rather than invisible: she does the
   ordinary work, the one north corner of brick is hers because her father laid it and she
   carried his bucket, and Azélie tells her to get a man to do it. D-005: specification
   permitted as a craft move, and it earns its place by doing work in the scene.

6. **Is it clear he is trying to reach the figure?** No, and the cause was geography, not
   diction: the figure stood on the plot beside hers, so *up the middle of the path* was
   not toward anything. The path now runs gate to back wall with the unowned plot at its
   head, and the line names the destination. → L-009, third clause (spatial).

7. **The avoidance is not set up, so the violation does no work.** *"there hasn't been
   much setup of avoidance yet, so will him going towards it do any work? something to
   think about."* → **L-024**, new. L-016 basis 4's fix (establish the rule by breaking
   it) is a rule about how not to *prove* the avoidance, and I read it as permission not
   to establish it at all.
