## A. HEADLINE, AND MARK'S TO RULE ON — the figure is solvable

Asked whether it could answer *what was the figure* in one line, it said:

> **Yes, and easily: it is Léonide, waiting to collect the son the family blamed for
> killing her.**
>
> That is a problem, and the story hands it to me in one sentence: *"she believed it had
> gone up about the time they lost Léonide."* Madame Dugas converts the figure from
> unexplainable to solved. Everything else corroborates — he calls it *maman*, the hair is
> over the face so there is no face, Numa says it is *watching at her*, the drapery is a
> woman's. Then the story protests: *"So it was nothing. It was a word Ti-Jean had."* A
> denial that explicit is a confirmation. I closed the file with the mystery retired.
>
> Worse, the identification crowds out the better engine that is also present. Read
> without the Dugas line, the figure is a thing that takes only what is *brought* to it,
> willingly, by someone nobody will stop — Ti-Jean sets his hat down like a man coming into
> a house, he was *comfortable*, and forty adults each assumed a nearer adult would turn
> him. That version makes the parish the mechanism and is genuinely unanswerable. The
> Léonide reading makes it a revenant with a grudge and a motive, and it wins because it is
> the only reading the story supplies a fact for.

**Its proposed fix:** *"cut the Léonide clause from Madame Dugas. Keep her hands stopping in
the butter beans and her 'she did not know why she had said that' — keep the fact that
something was said and retracted, delete the content. The figure survives; the answer dies."*

**Not actioned. This is Mark's, for three reasons.**

1. **The mother-link is Mark's own conception**, twice over: *"a birth defect that affected
   his mother who died giving birth to him... some extremely careful hinting at a connection
   between the figure and his mother"* (2026-09-09), and *"perhaps the figure went up around
   the time the mother died, but no one can remember actually seeing it go up"* (2026-09-10).
   Removing it is removing the thing he asked for.
2. **This is the exact question the pipeline was built to answer, and it has now been
   answered.** `attitude-of-grief-prose-03.md` says of the two mother-link passages: *"My
   read is that they hold, because 5 destroys its own evidence in its second paragraph and 9
   refuses itself in its third — but this is exactly the call that wants a fresh-context read
   before it wants my opinion (L-020)."* The fresh-context read says they do not hold. That
   is the process working, and it is why the call should not now be made by me.
3. **D-004 is a standing decision of Mark's**, and its only real test is the one that just
   failed: *"could a reader finish this and answer 'what was it' in a line?"*

**My recommendation, for what it is worth:** take the review's fix, which is surgical. Dugas
keeps the stopped hands and *she did not know why she had said that* — the retraction is the
whole beat and it survives intact without the date. Everything else the reviewer lists as
corroboration is Mark's and should stay: *maman* is undercut by the mare, the hair over the
face is the plate, and *watching at her* is the best line in the story. Those three are a
temperature. The Dugas clause is a fact, and the fact is what lets the temperature be read as
an answer.

## B. HEADLINE, ALSO MARK'S — the story protects her

> A woman of enormous practical competence and one unexploded cowardice, and the story likes
> her more than it should. **The story is protecting her, and the protection is visible.**
> One exposure (the Numa line) against four shelters: the alibi in section 4 (*"any one of
> them was nearer"*), the amnesia (*"could not have said one thing about doing it"*), the
> anger with no available object, and the absence of any thought about the Léonide story. She
> is never permitted a single self-serving thought she can remember having.

It named the same sentence I did as the story's centre — *"Elmire had been up in the fifth
row at her own coping the whole of that morning, which was true, and she said so"* — and
called it *"a woman lying by telling the truth and knowing it. More of that; far less 'she
could not have said.'"*

Its one thing it would change if it could change only one:

> **Let her want him gone. Once, on the page, in her own head, unmistakably, and let her
> remember it.** The socket is already built — the family telling in which the child did it
> to Léonide; *"her heart was going for no reason she would have given"*; the half-hour lost;
> the mortar gone off; the dream where the gladness was her own — and the story fills that
> socket with amnesia instead of a sentence. Give me one ugly clear thought in September and
> section 4 becomes unbearable instead of merely quiet.

**Not actioned. It runs straight into L-021's hardest clause** — *never write the sentence in
which she understands what she has done* — and into Mark's *"Do not assume she must remain
sympathetic. Assume she does not."* Those two pull opposite ways here and only Mark can say
which wins. Note that a *wish* (I want him gone) is not the same object as a *recognition*
(I see what I did), so the review's version may be admissible where L-021's forbidden
sentence is not.

**A cheaper alternative that needs no new sentence: remove one shelter instead of adding a
confession.** The most defensible to remove is the M4 alibi, *"and any one of them was nearer
to that path than Elmire was"* — it is the plot handing her an excuse in the same breath as
her failure, which is L-021's textbook tell. Cutting eleven words would leave the list of
people standing as fact rather than as exoneration. The amnesia line and the anger are both
Mark's source material and should stay.

## C. APPLIED — machine-made patterning (L-014, the rubric tell)

This is the review's most useful section and none of it is aesthetic judgment. Verbatim:

> The prose is skilled and it is skilled in a countable way. Four generators run the whole
> story, and once you see them the sentences stop being sentences.

| Generator | Count it gave | Applied |
|---|---|---|
| `X, which is Y` folk-appositive — *"which is how anything in that parish gets said"*, *"which is the part worn smooth"*, *"which is the long way round by the wall"*, *"which is the same work twice over"* | "well over a dozen… roughly one per paragraph… a construction that manufactures the appearance of inherited local knowledge on demand" | 3 cut. Kept the ones doing real work |
| `the way you…` domesticating simile | 9, "arriving at almost even intervals" | 1 cut — *"without heat, the way you report weather"* and *"without heat, the way she said everything"* were the same phrase twice about the same character |
| `before she had thought about it` | 4 — "the signature move is used four times and therefore signifies nothing when it lands last" | 1 cut (mine, in Ovide's yard). **Three remain**: M1, the rake, the ending. M1 and the ending are the refrain and must both stay; the rake's is Mark's own line, so Mark decides whether to thin it |
| `she could not have said` | 3, plus 3 near-relatives. "This formula is the story's *only* mode of interiority — a way of naming a mental state while claiming not to name it. It reads exactly like a rule being satisfied" | 1 cut |
| Section closers | "Four of five close on a short flat sentence recording either a small material cost or an absence of memory. Same cadence, same length, same withholding" | M1's shape broken |
| Verbatim duplication | *"The shell was packed hard as far as the third row"* repeated in M2 | cut (L-005) |
| Witness-introduction template | Madame Dugas and Anatole Broussard: "name + tenure + one credential + the useful fact. Identical construction, identical function, sixty lines apart" | Broussard's rebuilt |

And the note worth keeping whatever happens to the rest:

> **The deepest tell: nothing in this story fails.** There is no ugly sentence, no overreach,
> no digression that goes nowhere, no paragraph that is merely necessary. Every one lands a
> small point cleanly. Real writing has scars — a stretch the author loved too much, a joke
> that doesn't work, a description that goes on because the thing was interesting. This has a
> uniform, risk-adjusted competence across 4,100 words. That evenness is more machine-legible
> than any individual sentence.

That is `ai-tells.md` §D and L-014 arriving from a reader who had never seen either.

## D. APPLIED — where it skimmed, and why

> **Section 2, at the drummer from Franklin.** By then the section had already demonstrated
> its single proposition — *people avoid this and won't admit they avoid it* — four separate
> times, and the drummer is the fifth. Worse, its punchline is the third consecutive use of
> the same figure of speech: Elmire thinks *"The footing was bad in there"*; Ovide *"would
> have said the shell was bad in there"*; then *"the way you go round a hole in a road you
> have known about for years."* Three variations on one joke inside twenty-five lines.
>
> Section 2 contains five interchangeable proofs of one point plus two more in section 3.
> Two would carry it. Seven is an author who does not trust me to have understood the first
> one.

**Partly actioned and partly Mark's.** The Ovide/Elmire echo (*the footing was bad* / *the
shell was bad*) is deliberate and damning and stays. But the reviewer is right that M2 proves
one proposition five times, and **this is the counterweight to L-024's quantity ceiling
arriving from the other side** — I have been told the touches must be deniable and I stacked
seven of them. If a cut is wanted, the candidates in order are: the wedding-and-burial
sideways asking, and then the drummer (F4), which is the newest and the one I argued for
hardest. Not cut unilaterally because F4 is also the only rendering of the deformity that
works.

## E. APPLIED — over-stated lines

Cut: *"She noticed that and did nothing with it"* (the author grading his protagonist);
*"That was what it was"* (the knowing, audibly obeying the rule); *"She had wanted to see
whether she could, and she could"* (three beats for one gesture, now folded into the act);
*"because that was how he had been brought up"* (a moral button).

**Declined**, with reasons: *"Nothing changed, because nothing had been said"* (load-bearing
for L-021 — it makes the failure hers and not Azélie's refusal); the dream's *"In the dream
the gladness was her own"* (Mark's beat, and its whole point); and the three paragraphs after
*"His hat was on the plinth when they found him."* On that last one the review says:

> Cut the whole paragraph. *"His hat was on the plinth when they found him"* is the best image
> in the story and it is then explained across three consecutive paragraphs, the last of which
> (*"That he had been comfortable"*) is the only one needed… The fourth sentence I would have
> noticed missing would have been *"His hat was on the plinth when they found him"* — except
> the story then explains it across three paragraphs and I remember the explanation instead of
> the hat.

That text is Mark's almost verbatim and it is the story's centre, so it is passed up rather
than acted on. **It is a real question though:** does *"So he had not been in a hurry. He had
gone up there and put his hat down, which takes a moment, and which nobody does if there is
anything at all the matter"* earn its place, or does it explain the best image in the story?

## G. What it said the story does best

Three sentences it would have noticed missing:

1. *"His coat was a man's coat, because a coat does not have to fit."* — "Best sentence in the
   story. It renders an entire household's economy, an entire community's improvised kindness,
   and the exact shape of Ti-Jean's dignity, in twelve words, without one adjective of feeling."
2. *"He said it wasn't watching over her. He said it was watching at her."* — "A nine-year-old's
   preposition doing more than the previous 1,200 words. This is the only place in the story
   where the impossible thing gets a grammar."
3. *"Elmire had been up in the fifth row at her own coping the whole of that morning, which was
   true, and she said so."* — "The story's moral centre, and its only unprotected look at her."

On the ending: *"the last line earns itself… the reflex she suppressed once now fires for a
stranger's child, and she has become the person who turns them, every time, for the rest of her
life, for no reason she will ever give."* But: *"I would have noticed the last paragraph
missing. I would not have noticed the two before it."* And a real structural warning — *"the
story has three viable stopping points in nine lines, which suggests it does not know which one
it means."*

## Calibration (L-020's running record)

Fourth run of this stage. **Nineteen changes taken, four declined with reasons, two headline
findings escalated to Mark, one contradiction between the review and a standing decision.**

The pattern from rounds two and three **broke** this time, and that is the notable thing: on
story 01 the declines clustered on the review's most confident idea. Here its most confident
idea — cut the Léonide clause — is the one I think is probably right, and the thing it was
*least* interested in (the countable generators) is where it did the most good. Withholding the
craft folder is what produced both: it counted constructions a rubric-holder would have scored
as compliant, and it answered *what was it* without knowing it was not supposed to be able to.
