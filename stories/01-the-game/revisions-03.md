# Revisions 03 — three passages

## A. Mattie's laying-out — compressed to two sentences

**Replaces**, in full:

> Mrs Rey came up and laid her out and did the jaw and the hair and put her in the grey.
> Alma did not fold the arms across her front. Mrs Rey did not either. Nothing was said
> about it by either of them, and the two of them worked round the bed for the better part
> of an hour in a room where a thing had been settled without being mentioned.

**With:**

Mrs Rey came up and laid her out in the grey. Nobody touched the arms.

## B. Wick's laying-out — lengthened

**Replaces** the passage from *"Mrs Rey came up for the third time"* through *"I've done a
few and I never saw it."*

**With:**

Mrs Rey came up for the third time that summer and she did not fold his arms across. She
had them straight out from the start and worked round them, and when she was finished she
stood back from the bed and looked at him for a while.

"I meant to ask. Is that a way in your people? Laying them out so."

Alma said her mother's people were from Alabama.

"I wondered, was all." Mrs Rey picked up the basin and then set it down again. "I've done
a few and I never saw it."

She had learned the work from a woman called Delphine on the Bertrand place, who did that
place and two others besides, and who would not let a girl put a hand on a body before she
was fourteen. Mrs Rey was eleven when she started watching from the door, and she watched
for three years. Delphine was particular about the hair over everything else. Most people
got the hair wrong, she said, and thought nobody could tell.

Alma asked how many she had done.

Mrs Rey said she did not keep a count. Then she said better than a hundred. Then she said
it was more than that, on account of the year of the water, when she did eleven in one
week, and she could still say the eleven in order.

She took her apron off and folded it.

"I'll come if you send for me again," she said. "I'd rather you didn't send."

She was down off the steps and gone before Alma had the door open for her.

## C. The ending

**Replaces** everything from *"He was blown by then"* to the end. The paragraphs before it
— the sheet, the fight, him giving ground round the north side, her holding it down over
herself — are unchanged.

**With:**

He was blown by then and had to get his hands on his knees. She had not changed her pace
once.

There was a smell down there that he knew and did not have a name for.

Through the holes she had cut wrong he had got a look at one of her eyes, and it was open,
and it was not on him.

He looked up into it.

The sun was over the top of the tree by two and came down through it, and there was a good
deal of light coming through.

Emmy Ledoux was up where the trunk goes into three, about level with the gallery roof. The
figs were all round her and most of them were still green. She was not holding on.

He got off his knees and went as far as the trunk and put his hand on it and left it
there.

In August he had gone round that yard the better part of an hour with a lamp and the gun.
He did not go round it now.

He went back up the steps and sat down on the gallery, because that was as far as he got,
and he did not go out to her again.

She went round, and she stopped, and she stood where she had stopped, and then she went
round again.

The sun came off the yard a little after five. She did not come in with it. The grass was
going down in a ring by then where she had been walking.

He sat there until it was too dark to make her out.

She never hurried. She never put her arms down. She waited to be got, because that was the
rule.

# What each passage is doing, and what to watch

## A — the compression

Two sentences where there were four, and *"Neither of them touched the arms"* keeps
everything the long version had: the silence, the agreement, and the fact that a thing has
been settled without being said. The asymmetry now runs **scene → clause → long scene**,
which is asymmetry of *kind*. Three scenes with one variation each was the pattern the
fresh-context review called sanded to the same grain, and varying it further would not
have answered the note.

## B — the lengthening

**Mrs Rey specifies herself and explains nothing.** Delphine, the Bertrand place, watching
from the door at eleven, not being let to touch a body until fourteen, the year of the
water — that locates her without the prose saying a word about it, which is the craft
exception in D-005 rather than a correction to the record.

**It also does structural work, which is why it earns the length.** Her expertise is what
makes the arms impossible. *"I've done a few and I never saw it"* was doing that job with
the word *few*; now *a few* turns out to be better than a hundred, with eleven nameable in
order from one week. That is L-018's *kill the mundane explanation* arriving through a
character instead of through a survey — and the survey is the thing you cut in round two.

**Watch for:** the knowledgeable local (`ai-tells.md` §C). She offers no belief, no
folklore, and no account of the arms. She asks a question about Alma's people, gets
nothing, tells her own history, and then declines. *"I'll come if you send for me again. I'd
rather you didn't send."* is the closest anyone in the story comes to saying the house is
wrong, and it is a refusal, so Alma is left exactly as alone as she was.

**The one call I made that you may want back:** she leaves fast. The old version had her
working round the bed for an hour alongside Alma, which was warmer. This version has her
off the steps before the door is open.

## C — the ending

**The smell, added on Mark's question** — could Hollis catch the one Alma got in the hall
and could not name. Phrased as a near-verbatim echo on Mark's instruction, with no
reference to the earlier scene: *"there was a smell in the hall that Alma knew and did not
have a name for"* becomes *"There was a smell down there that he knew and did not have a
name for."* **The repetition of the construction is the linkage**; a pointer back to the
hall would have been the story checking the reader kept up.

It relocates a third banked image and hands Alma's private knowledge to her husband. It
supplies the involuntary event the discovery did not have (§E item 9, L-001's order
applied to Hollis: the body finds out before he does). And it moves the signal into the
approach, keeping the contact thin (L-010) — the smell says something, the eye says where,
the sight says who.

*"She was not holding on"* stays. Because the smell is never named it remains deniable, so
the holding-on line is not a restatement of it: a child in a tree holds on.

**The eye beat is now the hinge, unchanged.** *"it was open, and it was not on him"* stops
being atmosphere and becomes a direction, and the next line — *"He looked up into it"* — is
verbatim the July sentence about Alma, which you approved with no notes. The relocation
costs three words.

**Why he can see her, without a sentence arguing for it.** He is on his knees because his
wife just outfought him; nobody in this story has been under that tree at that angle. The
hour is two, so the sun is over the top of the fig and comes down through it rather than
into his eyes — which is the other half of the same July line. Nothing else explains
anything. If you want the miss stated harder, the cheapest addition is one clause about
the ditch being where men look, and it can go in the search paragraph rather than here.

**"There is no stone for Emmy Ledoux" stays where it is**, five paragraphs earlier, and is
now read twice: a failed search on the way past, and on reflection a child nobody ever got
down. Hollis not being able to stand up is what makes it literal, and the story never
connects the two.

**Hollis is stopping, not deciding.** No second attempt at the tree, no reasons, no
obstacles — he had his fight ninety seconds ago and lost it. *"because that was as far as he
got"* is the whole of it. The state is rendered by one earlier action of his own not
recurring: he walked that yard for an hour with a lamp and a gun in August, and he does not
walk it now. No clinical word appears, and none was needed.

**She never learns that he saw.** He says nothing, and her eye is not on him, so *"she never
said the rest of it to anybody as long as she lived"* still holds — and now he has a thing
he cannot say either.

**Deliberately not written: her arms.** He sees the height and the green figs and nothing
else. Putting the arms out along the limbs was available and would have made four
recurrences of the story's one image, which is the point where a pattern becomes a
mechanism the reader can solve. The reader supplies them.

**The two risks I would score this on:**

1. **Three payoffs land inside one page** — looking up into it, the figs still green, and
   her stopping where she had stopped. Two of the three are invisible on a first read,
   which is why I think it holds, but an ending that clicks is the failure mode the
   fresh-context review named and I cannot see it from in here.
2. **Nobody else arrives, and nobody must.** The moment a neighbour comes up the road the
   story owes an aftermath it cannot pay for. It ends with the two of them and the tree.

**Pre-flight note, honestly:** the height stays climbable on purpose — nothing says a child
could not have got up there — so the parish keeps a reading and the story stays at one
impossible thing. If you want it flatly impossible, that is one sentence and it costs
§E item 5.
