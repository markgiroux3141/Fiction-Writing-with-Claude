# Complicity options — Alma / Emmy staying

## What currently protects her

- L174-176: her one active letter argues *for* Emmy staying, and a second party agrees,
  so the reasoning belongs to the parish rather than to her.
- L263-264: a second letter, and "which was the only answer there was" — the sentence
  closes the alternative off in narration rather than letting her close it.
- L318: "Alma said when your mother is up" — an answer that is true when given.
- L386-388: the mother is up, the ask is soft, and "Alma wrote back that she could manage
  it" is one clause long and passes without weight.
- L380-384: Sam goes to Ada's and Emmy is not mentioned by anyone, including the story.
- L417: "Emmy was out of the bed and through the door before Alma was up out of the chair"
  — she is given a head start she cannot beat.

---

## Tier 1 — protections removed, nothing added

Nothing here is an act. A court has nothing.

**1. Strike "and Alma wrote and said so."**
L174-176. The mother's letter still comes and still says stay, but the argument is no
longer in Alma's handwriting and no objection replaces it.
> Emmy Ledoux stayed. Her mother was lying-in with a four-week-old and there was nowhere
> in the parish worse to send a child out of a sick house than into that one, and a
> letter came saying so, and that was the whole of the discussion.

**2. Cut the August letter.**
L263-264. The only follow-up disappears, so Mattie, Wick and Sam's evacuation all pass
without her writing once.
> She thought about writing to the Ledoux woman again and did not.

**3. Kill "the only answer there was."**
L264. Let the letter be received rather than obeyed.
> ...and got back the same answer, and she read it twice and put it in the press.

**4. The refusal was never a refusal.**
Both letters. The mother defers to Alma instead of declining, and the door stays open the
whole summer.
> The letter said she was in no state to have her, and would Alma keep her if she could
> manage it, and if she could not, to say so plainly and they would shift. Alma did not
> say so.

**5. Silence taken as consent.**
L263. She writes, nothing comes back, and she lets the nothing decide.
> No letter came back. She let that be the answer.

**6. The letter that does not go down.**
L263-264. It is written. It is not posted. Posting means walking to the landing.
> The letter to the Ledoux woman was still on the press on the Saturday and still on it in
> September, and she never did anything with it but move it when she wanted the top of
> the press.

**7. There was room in the wagon.**
L380-384. Sam leaves for Ada's and the second seat is empty.
> There was room in the wagon. Nobody said so, and she was one of the ones who did not.

**8. The doctor is not told.**
L168-172. He is in the house an hour and counts the children he was sent for.
> He asked how many were in the house and she said four, which was the four of hers.

**9. Hollis is not corrected.**
L281-287. He says the boys should go up to Ada, and the sentence is allowed to describe
the whole house.
> He said the boys. She let him mean the boys.

## Tier 2 — small acts, every one deniable

Still nothing actionable. Each is a true thing said, or a true thing withheld.

**10. The burials stay out of the letters.**
L386-388, planted from L208. The mother agrees to another few weeks not knowing three
children are in the ground.
> She had not put the burials in any of the letters. There had not been a natural place
> for it, and then there were three of them and no place at all.

**11. The answer stops being true and does not change.**
L318, paid after L386.
> Emmy asked most days when she was going home. Alma said when your mother is up. She
> went on saying it in September.

**12. Give "she could manage it" its weight.**
L386-388. The one word was available and she wrote the other.
> Alma wrote back that she could manage it. It took her no time at all to write.

**13. The letter shaped to get its answer.**
L176. She reports the fever at the Thibaults', the baby wanting quiet, her own house
healthy, and asks the woman what she thinks best.
> She asked the woman what she thought best, and she put in that the fever was at the
> Thibaults', and every word of it was true.

**14. The boots are in the press.**
Between L233-239 and L318. Emmy's road shoes went in with the good linen in July and the
press has been shut since, and that is tidiness.
> Emmy's boots had gone into the press in July with the good linen, and the press had
> been shut since, and Alma did not open it.

**15. Laundered through Mrs Rey.**
L248-250, at the pot. Alma describes the Ledoux house; Mrs Rey supplies the conclusion
unasked, and Alma can repeat a thing she did not say.
> Mrs Rey said the child was better off where she was, and Alma had not had to say
> anything to get it said.

**16. Something comes with the child.**
L174-176. Board, or Ledoux's mule for the cutting, or a debt of Hollis's set against her
keep.
> The arrangement was that Ledoux would send his mule up for the cutting, and Hollis had
> been counting on the mule since June.

**17. Emmy is load-bearing.**
L330-334, Mattie's illness. The house runs on her, and Alma knows what the nights would
be without her.
> Emmy took the nights with Mattie that Alma could not take, and was nine, and was good
> at it.

**18. The brother is turned at the gate.**
Between Mattie and Wick. A Ledoux boy walks up from Franklin and gets two true sentences.
> She met him at the gate and said the child was asleep and there was fever in the house,
> and both of those were true, and he went back down the road.

**19. Emmy is made the one who won't go.**
L318. Alma explains, once and kindly, what the fever would do to a four-week-old, and the
asking stops.
> After that Emmy did not ask again, and Alma let her have the reason and did not take it
> back.

**20. The question asked at the right hour.**
L362-366, the figs. She puts it to the child on the one evening of the month the answer
will be no.
> She asked her that night, with the figs in her, whether she wanted to go home to her
> mother, and the girl said not yet, and Alma never asked her again and never had to.

## Tier 3 — she arranges the exposure

**21. Hers are called in.**
L214. Split the sentence that currently covers four children with one verb.
> She called hers in at four, and Emmy stayed out, because nobody had said her name.

**22. Somebody has to go out.**
L233-239, the shut-up house. The pot and the henhouse are outside, and the quick one goes.
> Somebody had to go out to the pot and the henhouse, and she sent Emmy, because Emmy was
> the quick one.

**23. Inside work and outside work.**
L334, attached to "nobody ever said why."
> Sam had the inside work from the middle of August, because she wanted him where she
> could see him, and Emmy had the yard, because Emmy would not sit still.

**24. The bench is for checking.**
L297-299 and L314-316. The worse place for the wash is the place the pattern can be
confirmed from, over days.
> It went after the Ledoux girl mostly. She watched from the bench a week to be sure of
> it, and it held, and the not-saying of it got easier every day.

**25. She sends her out.**
L390-392. "Emmy went out in the afternoons anyway" becomes an instruction, backed by the
doctor's own advice about air.
> Alma sent her out in the afternoons for the air, because that was what you did with a
> child, and the doctor had said it in front of six people.

**26. She taught her the rule.** — **REJECTED by Mark, 2026-09-09.** Written into draft
02 and cut the same day: *"the kids just came up with it themselves. It doesn't need any
explanation."* Logged as L-016's first replication — the game may not have a provenance,
and a complicity move may not buy its effect out of the haunting's furniture.
Planted in movement one, paid at L425-435. Emmy came from a house that played it
differently, and Alma is the one who told her you keep to the ring and you wait to be
got.
> She had told the girl the rule herself, in June, on the step, because the girl had come
> from a house where they played it another way.

**27. The shutters have two reasons.**
L400. The correct one is available to say out loud and is not the one operating.
> She sat up eight nights with the shutters back, and she could have said it was because
> you kept a sickroom open, and that was true, and it was not why.

**28. No head start.**
L415-419. She is on her feet and nearer the door than the child is.
> She was out of the chair when the girl came off the bed. The door was six feet away and
> she was nearer it.

**29. She gives over first.**
L442-444. The search runs a week and she stops on the Wednesday.
> She went out with them the first two days. On the Wednesday she stayed at the house, and
> when they gave over on the Saturday she had given over already.

**30. The letter's selection acquits her.**
L446-449. Among the true things she includes are the two that make a record.
> She put in that she had written twice to ask whether the girl should come home, which
> she had, and she put in the dates.

## Tier 4 — over the line

**31. A letter that did not come.**
After L263.
> She told the girl her mother had written and said stay, and no letter had come that
> week.

**32. The hook on the outside of the door.**
August, and it is on account of fever, or sleepwalking.
> She put the hook on the outside of the back-room door in August and told Hollis it was
> on account of the girl walking in her sleep.

**33. Invert the amnesia at the tree.**
L226-231. She knew exactly what she shouted on the Sunday, and it was not get away from
them, and the words never reach the page.
> She knew what she had said. She knew it all that week and every week after, and she
> never said it to anybody as long as she lived.

**34. She put the first sheet on her.**
Movement one, L64-66 and the third-sheet beat. The press is opened by her hand and the
child under the sheet is Emmy.
> She had got the sheet out of the press herself and put it over the girl's head and told
> her to hold the front of it up so she could see her feet.

**35. Two in the afternoon.**
The last week, in place of or beside the rally. The hour they had used, the child asleep,
the ring of shade, the steps back up.
> The girl did not wake when she was picked up and she did not wake when she was put
> down, and Alma went back up the steps and got on with the wash.

**36. The name said to the yard.**
Any night after Mattie.
> She said the girl's name to the yard, once, in September, and then she said it again in
> case it had not heard.
