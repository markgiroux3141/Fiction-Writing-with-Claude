## Movement two — the arms beat under-rendered

> *"I think more attention should be paid to the arms being out to the sides and her
> putting them back down. She either needs to be unsettled by this, or it should be
> implied to the reader that she thinks someone else did it. In this case the reader must
> know that there is no way anyone could have done it (no real person)."*

**What the failed version did.** Delivered the worst physical fact in the draft inside a
subordinate clause — *"and when she came back they were out to the sides, and she put
them down again"* — then closed the beat with *"because there was a good deal else."*
Pure behaviour, no body, no search, and the natural explanation left standing.

**Diagnosis.** Two separate faults, both mine, both over-applications of existing rules:

1. **Flatness used as a mid-story instrument.** L-006's *withdraw interiority* earns its
   effect at the **last** beat, where a character who has stopped having a crisis proves
   the haunting is a settled condition. Mid-story there is no settled condition to prove,
   so the same move reads as the event being skipped.
2. **The mundane explanation never died.** An impossible event is only impossible once
   the possible version is gone, and the reader has to watch it go.

**L-001 replicated.** L-001 already says *behaviour alone is under-rendering*, and this
passage was pure behaviour. **First independent replication of an existing rule in the
project** — L-001's basis moves 2 → 3, which matters given that STATUS.md's central
complaint is that almost every rule sits at basis 1.

**Extracted as L-018.** Order is now: involuntary body → the knowing → the misfired
action → the failed search → flat. Boundaries added to L-006 and `craft-principles.md`
§11 the same day.

**Both of Mark's options taken, not one.** He offered *unsettled* **or** *she thinks
someone else did it*. The revision does both, because the second is what makes the first
credible: she is not establishing a haunting, she wants to know who handled her child,
and she stops when she runs out of people rather than when she reaches a conclusion.

**Guard applied — and then broken in the same turn.** I wrote *"keep the elimination
domestic, never forensic, three clauses at most"* into L-018 and then wrote a
three-clause alibi survey: Hollis in the cane, Mrs Rey in sight of the kitchen window,
the children in the yard. See below.

## Movement two, second pass — the survey cut

> *"I think everything that precedes this paragraph does enough to explain the arms by
> the side, so if there's anything in this paragraph or anything after that is still
> trying to explain this, it isn't needed. Standing by the door, asking about it, that's
> good enough."*

**Cut:** the alibi paragraph, and *"The room had one door. She had come through it both
times."* Kept: the doorway, the cool cup, the arms, the misfired action, the question to
Mrs Rey, her answer. Then straight to *"They were out again when she went back in."*

**The finding.** The clause count was never the variable. **A survey is narration; a
question is drama.** Mrs Rey is the only person who could plausibly have done it, so
asking her *is* the exclusion — and her answer is worse than a denial, because she did
fold them, across, the way she always does. Everyone else is the reader's business.

Two further reasons the survey actively hurt, both from existing doctrine: it closed a
gap the reader was enjoying closing (L-006's good-gap boundary), and it carried an
unspoken *I have checked, you may stop wondering*, which is L-004 at scene scale.

**Tell for future drafts:** any sentence after an impossible fact whose job is to make
the fact hold up. If a line exists to defend the event, cut it. The event does not need
a lawyer.

**L-018 amended** the same day rather than a new rule added — the rule was hours old and
needed correcting, not company.

## Movement two, third pass — the orphaned referent

> *"'They were out again when she went back in.' is a bit ambiguous, I know you're
> talking about the other children, but it's been quite some time since you've referenced
> them and now that you removed that other paragraph, it's hard to tell what you're
> talking about"*

**Worse than reported.** The line meant **the arms**. Mark resolved it to the children,
so this is not ambiguity — it is a wrong resolution, and the reader carried it forward.
Clean L-003 failure on its own test: *could a reader name the referent of this sentence
without going back a line?*

**I caused it with the previous cut.** The original read *"They were out again when she
got there"*, anchored by the paragraph above it and by *there* = the room. Trimming to
*"went back in"* removed the anchor and activated the indoor/outdoor frame, where *out*
belongs to children far more strongly than to arms.

**Fixed by naming the noun:** *"When she came back in, the arms were out to the sides
again."* Ends on *again*, which is the frightening word.

**Doctrine.** No new rule — this is L-003 replicating (**basis 1 → 2**) plus a new clause
under L-005 on post-cut orphaning, and a new seam-check appended to `ai-tells.md` §E item
10. Also §E item 12, from the previous pass.

**Second replication in two rounds.** L-001 went 2 → 3 and L-003 went 1 → 2 from this
single draft. Both were rules I had read, written down, and then broken — which is
evidence the doctrine is roughly right and that reading it is not the same as applying
it.

## Movement three — rejected outright: the uncannily calm protagonist

> *"immediately something feels off about this part. This woman already noticed this
> strange figure which initially she thought was her husband but was unsure, she knows
> that it disappears when she pays attention to it, and that her daughter recently died,
> but then she's just casually watching it play with her children? We need a realistic
> scenario because any reasonable person in this scenario would be freaking out,
> screaming at it and chasing it out of the yard. Is she paralyzed with fear? Is she
> depressed and traumatized? How can we pivot here?"*

**The most serious note of the round — a plausibility failure, not a prose one.**

**The rejected version, kept as the record** (CLAUDE.md: *the progression is the
record*). Between Nan's burial and Mattie's illness it had, in full:

> Nan was buried on the Saturday, and by the Wednesday after, Alma had the wash caught
> up. / She did the wash on the bench at the north end of the gallery from then on... /
> She saw it on the Thursday and again on the Saturday. Both times it was in the game...
> and both times she stayed on the bench. She had been round that tree once.

Four sentences of arrangement where a fight should have been. *"She had been round that
tree once"* was carrying the entire motivation for a mother's inaction after the death of
her daughter, and it cannot.

**Why I wrote it.** Every craft doc in this repo praises restraint, flatness and
accommodation — §6 calls adaptation *"the true horror: people adapt"*, and L-006 prices
it. All three are instruments for **after** a character has fought and lost. I used them
as substitutes for the fight. This is the third time this round that a correct rule
misfired by being applied at the wrong beat (see L-018 on flatness).

**Both of Mark's suggested answers declined, with a reason.** *Paralysed with fear*,
*depressed*, *traumatised* — `reference/period/anachronism-traps.md` § Psychological
flags exactly this vocabulary as *"the largest single risk, and exactly the vocabulary an
AI drafts in by default."* A diagnosis is a modern interior. The period-true state is
not a condition, it is a **position**: she has been overruled by everyone who loves her,
on grounds she cannot argue with.

**The pivot.** She fights, and **1878** beats her:

1. She goes at it — down the steps, across the yard, at the top of her voice. Nothing at
   the tree. Her children watch her do it.
2. She keeps them in the house. Days of it, in an August with the doors shut.
3. **A shut hot house is how children take the fever**, and everybody in the parish knows
   it — bad air is the whole explanatory frame before germ theory. Hollis says it, Mrs
   Rey says it unasked, the doctor has said it in front of six people.
4. **Her true argument is unavailable.** She spent it on the Sunday, shouting, in front of
   the children; Hollis walked the yard with a lamp and a gun and then was kind to her
   all week. So she argues the fever, which she does not believe, against three people who
   do, and loses on those grounds.
5. She opens the house and lets them out — the correct, loving, medically endorsed thing
   to do.
6. **Then** the wash bench at the wrong end of the gallery is earned, and reads as a
   position rather than as calm.

**Extracted as L-019.** `craft-principles.md` §6 beat 2 gains the precondition;
`ai-tells.md` §D gains *the uncannily calm protagonist*. The load-bearing clause: **the
ordinary world closes her options, never the thing** — a haunting that forecloses choices
has acquired rules and reach, which is L-016.
