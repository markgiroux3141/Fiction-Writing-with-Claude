# Critique 03 — round three

Raw feedback. Generalised rules go to `craft/lessons-learned.md`; this file is the record
of what Mark actually said. Round three began the same day draft 01 was promoted and
ended with draft 03 promoted over it.

**The character of this round is different from round one.** Round one was eleven notes on
passages. Round three was **seven rulings on the story's decisions**, five of which
corrected my judgment rather than my execution, and three of which reversed a
recommendation I had argued for.

---

## 1. The Emmy thread — the story was protecting her

> *"That is the most damning thing in the story and I think the story then protects her
> from it. As written, Alma repeatedly tries to send Emmy home and is refused, so the
> child staying is nobody's fault."*
>
> *"Give me at least fifteen distinct ways the story could make Alma genuinely complicit
> ... Not a shortlist. I want the whole range, including versions you think go too far ...
> Do not assume she must remain sympathetic. Assume she does not."*

**Identified as a thread, not a passage** — the first note in this project that names a
decision spanning the whole story rather than a line. The July letter was already in
Alma's handwriting; what protected her was that a second party ratified it and that the
narration closed the alternative for her (*"which was the only answer there was"*).

**→ L-021**, and the pool is `complicity-options.md`: 36 candidates sorted by damage, of
which seventeen were taken into draft 02. Also L-013 applied to a **revision** decision
for the first time; the middle of the distribution on a moral question about a character
is *keep her sympathetic*, and it was the default I was writing from without having
chosen it.

## 2. The game may not have an origin

> *"I don't think Alma should teach the rule in the beginning. I want this to be something
> that the kids just came up with themselves. It doesn't need any explanation."*

Draft 02 had *"Alma had taught her the rule herself, on the step, the first week she was
up"* — written to implicate her in the rule that kills the child. Cut the same day.

**→ L-016 basis 1 → 2, new clause: no provenance either.** The rule already banned the
game's interior; this bans its history. And the important half: the sentence existed only
because L-021 wanted the space. **A prohibition does not weaken because a different lesson
is shopping.**

## 3. Historical accuracy is not the standard

> *"this level of historical accuracy is not necessary. We're going to be writing ghost
> stories. If ghosts can be real then being one year off (1877 vs 1878) and having slight
> contradictions that no one would notice is fine. I don't want to lessen the impact of the
> story by trying to fix these issues unless you think there's a way to do it cleanly. For
> example I'm fine explaining that the woman laying out the bodies is a black woman if it
> would add to the story."*

Declines the wider half of `critique-02.md` Tier 3b. **→ D-005.** Same sentence as L-016,
pointed at the reference library instead of the plot. The exception is load-bearing:
specification is permitted as a **craft** move and forbidden as a correction, which is the
route Mrs Rey's history took into draft 03.

## 4. The laying-outs — compress one, lengthen one

> *"for the laying out, I think drastically compressing one of them works because it does
> seem repetitive each time. I'm also ok with lengthening one adding a conversation with
> Ms Rey, perhaps her background and ethnicity could be hinted at during this part."*

Chosen from a 21-item list. The three now run **scene → clause → long scene**, which is
asymmetry of *kind*; three scenes with one variation each was the pattern the fresh-context
review called *sanded to the same grain*, and varying it further would not have answered
the note.

Mrs Rey gained Delphine on the Bertrand place, watching from the door at eleven, not
allowed to touch a body before fourteen, the year of the water, eleven in one week she can
still say in order — and she explains nothing. Her expertise is what makes the arms
impossible, which is L-018's *kill the mundane explanation* arriving through a character
rather than through the survey cut in round one.

## 5. The ending — the discovery, constructed by Mark

> *"perhaps we can save the original last line. A night doesn't pass. Hollis goes out after
> his wife when she puts the sheet on, she walks around and he tries to stop her, then she
> stops and looks up. Perhaps this can tie into the part where he sees her eye but it's not
> on him. Then he makes the discovery ... Then he becomes totally overwhelmed, perhaps
> staggers away, and watches aghast as his wife continues to walk around as day turns to
> night, and the original ending along with the final line survives."*

His second ending for this story, and the second to beat everything I had. The move that
makes it work is his: **the eye beat becomes a direction.** *"it was open, and it was not
on him"* had been a closed door; it is now what points Hollis up. The next line —
*"He looked up into it"* — is verbatim the July sentence about Alma from the movement he
approved with no notes, so the relocation cost three words and nothing had to be planted.

On the earlier, more damning variant in which a night passes and Alma has stood under the
child since the dark: not taken. The refrain was worth more than the complicity.

**→ D-004 gains *a visible consequence is not an explanation*.** The ending shows what the
thing did, once, to one child; three others died of fever in their beds the same summer, so
the reader still cannot answer *what was it*. Mark, on the trade: *"It does point more
towards the thing being real but that's ok."*

## 6. "There is no stone" stays, and gets worse

> *"I think 'no stone' can stay. It opens the possibility that they all went insane and
> never buried her. Perhaps the property was abandoned. The specifics don't matter, but for
> some reason having a body and it never being buried is deeply unsettling."*

**Reverses my recommendation.** I had proposed cutting the line because a recovered body
implies a grave — checking it for consistency instead of asking what the new fact made it
**mean**. Nine unchanged words now say nobody ever got her down.

**→ L-016 basis 2 → 3, the unfollowed consequence**, with the procedure: before cutting a
line because a new event contradicts it, write down what the new event makes the line mean.
And the class it belongs to — **the observance not performed**, which is cheaper than gore
and does not spend D-003.

## 7. Hollis is not deciding, he is stopping

> *"Hollis sitting on the gallery isn't a decision, it's a place he is forced to out of
> mental exhaustion. He is unable to think or act, or do anything but sit and watch in
> horror."*

**Reverses my proposal** of a failed attempt on the tree plus two ordinary reasons for
abandoning it. He had bought the collapse ninety seconds earlier in the same scene.

**→ L-019, the per-character clause: the fight precondition is spent once.** Asking a man
who has just lost to lose again is this rule fired at the wrong beat — the exact mirror of
L-018's error, committed by me while holding the beat index that exists to prevent it.
Rendered by one earlier action of his own not recurring: the lamp and the gun.

## 8. Is the description enough to know she is dead?

> *"do you think the description of her in the tree is enough to know shes dead? if not add
> something subtle, if it's enough, leave it."*

It was not — the placement alone left a beat in which a reader could read her as alive up
there. **→ "She was not holding on."** An absence-signal (L-010) rather than a
description: it carries dead with no word about death or decay and stays out of the
visceral register.

Note the form of the instruction — names the goal, sets the budget, leaves the execution,
and licenses *no change*. That is the shape of a note that costs one message instead of a
round.

## 9. The smell — and how a refrain is linked

> *"could Holis catch a whiff of the scent that Alma got in the hallway that she could not
> name?"*
>
> *"the smell should be described almost exactly as Alma described it ... The repetition of
> the way it was said should be the linkage, not a direct reference to the hallway which
> seems too forced. Perhaps the hallway doesn't even need to be mentioned."*

The best addition in the round, and then a correction to my execution of his own idea. I
wrote *"He had had it in the hall before"* — a pointer back to the earlier scene, which is
the story checking the reader kept up. His version:

| | |
|---|---|
| movement two | *there was a smell in the hall that Alma knew and did not have a name for* |
| the ending | *There was a smell down there that he knew and did not have a name for* |

**A refrain is carried by repetition of form, never by reference to where it came from.**
Worth promoting into L-017 if it replicates. It also supplied the involuntary event the
discovery had been missing — `ai-tells.md` §E item 9, which I had detected and was going to
report rather than solve.

I also cut *"She was not holding on"* on the grounds that the smell made it a third
statement of the same fact, and Mark reinstated it. He was right and the reason is exact:
**because the smell is never named it stays deniable**, so the two beats do different work —
one is what his body knows, the other is what his eyes settle.

## 10. Process — line ranges

> *"is there a way you can point to line numbers of the draft of the changes between draft 2
> and 3 so I don't have to go through the whole thing. Ranges of lines will work"*

Cheap and obviously right. **→ `revision-protocol.md` step 4:** every revision's changelog
carries a table of changed line ranges in the new file. The reader of a 5,000-word draft
should not have to diff it by eye to find 200 changed words.

## The pattern of the round

**Five of seven rulings corrected judgment, not execution, and three reversed a
recommendation I had argued for** — the no-stone cut, Hollis's second fight, and the
holding-on cut. Every one of my proposals ran toward coherence and every one of his
corrections ran away from it: the game's origin, the plot's bookkeeping, and a character
given one obstacle too many.

Round one's finding was *the failure mode is making things add up.* Round three is that
finding replicating three times in a single day, in a context that had the sentence in
front of it the whole time.

**What I caught unaided this round:** two orphaned referents of my own making, a changelog
that overstated what was on the page, and one pre-flight item. Bookkeeping and mechanics.
**Structural or judgment faults caught unaided: none.**
