# Story 01 — Ending candidates

Built to L-017: **bank a plain image of the thing early, then let it come true of a
person in the flattest short sentence in the story.** No new fact arrives. Nobody
explains anything.

**No recommendation section, by rule (L-013).** Selection is Mark's, or `pick.py`'s.

## What is available to bank

Everything the seed vignette already establishes about the thing, without adding
anything. These are the images an ending can cash:

| Image | As laid down in the vignette |
|---|---|
| Height | *"It was taller."* |
| Rate | *"It went the same slow way"* |
| Posture | arms out while playing; **arms down at its sides** when it stopped |
| Heat | *"in the full sun"* — it stood in it and did not mind |
| Silence | the children screamed; it did not |
| Sparing | *"It followed the Ledoux girl twice around and let her get away"* |
| Summons | she called them in and it did not come |
| Number | six in the field, five at the table |

## The constraint that rules one shape out

The **accommodation ending** (L-006 — close on a small permanent habit she now keeps) is
not available to story 01. It has fired in nine of twelve pieces and L-014 rations
ending shapes to one in three. Every candidate below is a **recontextualization**
instead.

## The pool

Eighteen candidates. **Underlying idea** is tagged on each per the L-013 amendment, so
repetition is visible rather than disguised by the headings.

### Rate — the slowness relocated

**R1.** The Ledoux girl gets up from it in September and is well, and she is slow now.
Not weak. Slow. `[idea: rate]`
> *She had all the time in the world to cross a room.*

**R2.** The last one to sicken takes the longest to die, and the household adjusts to
her pace, and nobody remarks on it. `[idea: rate]`
> *It took her from July to the end of August, and she did not hurry any of it.*

**R3.** She watches her daughter come up the steps from the yard and knows how long it
will take before she is halfway. `[idea: rate]`

### Heat — the sun relocated

**H1.** The sick child will not be covered and is not warm to the touch, and lies in the
sun on the gallery boards in the afternoon, comfortable. `[idea: temperature]`
> *She lay in the sun all that August and she never once sweated.*

**H2.** They keep the sickroom shut and hot, the way you do, and the child asks for it
hotter. `[idea: temperature]`

**H3.** At the burying it is ninety-six degrees and the mourners are wrecked by it, and
the girl in the box is the only one in the parish the heat is not touching.
`[idea: temperature]`

### Posture — the arms relocated

**P1.** Not the laid-out body but the living one. The last child sleeps with her arms
straight out to the sides and cannot be folded back. `[idea: posture]`
> *She put the arms back down twice and then she left them.*

**P2.** The neighbour woman's question (C3) is answered by the next thing she sees, not
by a word. `[idea: posture]`

**P3.** The surviving child stands in the yard with her arms down at her sides, in the
full sun, not playing. `[idea: posture + temperature]`

### Silence — the not-screaming relocated

**S1.** The children screamed at it properly all summer. None of them makes a sound
going. `[idea: silence]`
> *Four children died in that house and not one of them cried about it.*

**S2.** The last one stops screaming first, weeks before she is ill, and that is how
she knows. `[idea: silence]`

### Sparing — "let her get away" relocated

**G1.** The Ledoux girl is the one who lives. It followed her twice and let her get
away, and it let her get away again. `[idea: sparing]`
> *It had let her get away twice around the tree, and she thought about that.*

**G2.** She understands that the child who was spared is the one who was chosen, and she
cannot say which of those two things is worse, and she does not try. `[idea: sparing]`

**G3.** Years on, the surviving daughter is the only one of them, and she is well, and
she is not right. `[idea: sparing]`

### Summons — "it did not come in" relocated

**C1.** She calls the last one in at noon and the child does not come, and is not ill,
and is not being disobedient. `[idea: summons]`
> *She called her twice and then she went and got her.*

**C2.** The child comes in when called all her life, and never once at the first call.
`[idea: summons]`

### Height — the tallness relocated

**T1.** The carpenter measures for the box and comes back to measure again, and does
not say why, and charges for the longer one. `[idea: height]`

**T2.** She has to look up at the last of them, and the girl is nine. `[idea: height]`

## L-013 duplicate audit

Eighteen candidates, **seven distinct ideas**: rate (3), temperature (3), posture (3),
silence (2), sparing (3), summons (2), height (2). Ratio is honest — no idea is
masquerading as a category. Compare the event pool, where twenty-four candidates covered
about four ideas.

## Selection — resolved

**None of the eighteen. Mark wrote his own, 2026-09-08, and it is better than the pool:**

> *"The last child, near death, leaps from the bed and runs out the door. The figure
> reappears and chases them around a tree playing the game, and they disappear behind
> the tree and are never seen again."*

**Why it beats the pool.** Every candidate above relocates an image onto a *body* — a
dead child, a sick child, a slow child. All eighteen are therefore variations on one
idea, which the L-013 duplicate audit above failed to catch because I counted the images
rather than the underlying move. Mark's ending relocates nothing onto a body. It puts the
child **into the game**, alive, and takes her out of the story entirely.

**What makes it work:**

- **A dying child who leaps.** The body doing what it cannot do is worse than any
  description of the thing, and it is period-grounded rather than a second impossible
  event — terminal lucidity, the surge before death, is attested and every adult in that
  house has seen it before. It reads as medicine until she reaches the tree.
- **The tree does the impossibility cheaply.** A fig two feet through, full daylight,
  nothing behind it, and she can see all the way round. The **narrowness** is the horror.
  No vanishing effect required.
- **It resolves B4.** All summer it is gone when she looks. At the end it does not
  bother. That is the escalation, and B4 was Mark's own pick.
- **It does not use the accommodation shape** (L-006), which was unavailable.

**The risk, and the guard.** A chase-and-vanish *completes* the story: read once, the
summer becomes a recruitment, and the reader can answer *what was it* in a line — the
mystery-novel failure that got D and E rejected. **The guard is already in the spine:
the other four died and stayed dead.** Four graves, one of them laid out with her arms
out to the sides, and then one nothing. Same summer, same thing, different outcomes,
so there is no rule to extract. Nobody in the story may treat the vanishing as the
game's outcome, and the earlier deaths must not fall into a pattern.

**Execution deviates from L-017 and it is logged as DEV-003.** The chase is a scene where
L-017 asks for flat summary. Resolution: the run and the tree are the **penultimate**
beat; the last beat is cold and after the fact — the empty yard in daylight, no body, one
flat short sentence relocating a banked image. Kinetic ending, freezing last line.

**Still to choose: which image the last line cashes.** The pool above is now a menu of
banked images rather than of endings, and one of them still has to be laid down in the
first third and paid off in that final sentence.

## Drafting consequence of whichever wins

The chosen image must be **banked in the first third**, plainly, where it reads as
weather or furniture, and then left alone. If it appears twice before the last line it
is a plant and the reader will see it coming.
