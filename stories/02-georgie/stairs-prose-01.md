# The Stairs — Twelve Renderings

Story 02. Scene supplied by Mark, 2026-09-10: *he is sitting on the stairs of the cellar,
Georgie is in view, he is paralysed, Georgie is getting in his head, he tries to get out
through the door at the top and cannot.*

Twelve versions of that one moment. No plot, no mechanism, **no flags in the body** — react
to whichever ones do something to you. They are not a set and several contradict each other
about what Georgie is and what the evening is for. Names are placeholders: Ellis, his wife
Verna, a neighbour called Teague Marsh.

Notes on what varied, and the two decisions I made silently, are at the **end of the file**.

---

## 1. The answer

He had come down for a jar of the sweet potatoes and had got as far as the fourth stair. The
jar was beside him on the tread with the paper still tied over the lid. The window at the far
end sat level with the yard and took what light was left, and Georgie was under it, on the
boards, where Georgie had been since the spring.

The trouble was that he had been asked something.

A man can stand up and go up his own stairs whenever he likes. He cannot do it in the part of
an evening where he has been asked a thing and has not yet said anything back — not to a
neighbour, not to a hand he is paying off, not to anybody living. Ellis had let that part run
on longer than he had ever let it run with a person, and it was running now.

He put his hands on his knees twice, which is what a man does before he gets up, and both
times he was still sitting.

The door at the top was shut and had the kitchen behind it. It came to him that if he waited
much longer it would be too late to answer at all, and that he had no idea what a man does
then.

## 2. The face he had on him

The cellar took a little water every March and he had come down to see how much. It was about
an inch, which was nothing, and he had known that from the second stair.

He sat down on the fifth.

Above him the door stood open the width of a hand, the way it was left now, and the kitchen
was on the other side of it with the lamp lit and Verna doing something at the table. Twice
she went across the gap. He could hear the chair legs when she sat back down.

Ellis worked out what would be required. Eleven stairs, then the door, then the kitchen, and
Verna would look up from whatever she had, the way she looked up when anybody came into a
room, and he would be standing in the doorway with the face he had on him now.

That was the whole of it. He could go up any time he liked and he could not go up like this.

Georgie went on. Ellis kept his eyes on the strip of kitchen light and answered where an
answer was wanted, quietly, so it would not carry, and the water came in over the toes of his
boots and he let it.

## 3. The same ground

It went over the same ground it had gone over on the Tuesday, and Ellis said the same things
back, because they had been true on the Tuesday and they were true now.

That is the part he could never have explained to anybody. It was dull. He sat on those
stairs from the end of supper until the strip of light under the kitchen door went out, and
for most of that time he was bored the way a man is bored at a wake in the third hour, with
his hat in his hands and somebody's aunt working through a grievance about land.

He wanted to go up. He had wanted to go up since about nine.

Twice he got as far as standing. Both times he heard himself saying that he would see about
it, or that he did not know about that, and a man who is answering is not a man who is
leaving; the second time he caught himself at it he had already sat back down.

He went up a little after one, and it was not because anything had finished. It was that he
could not hold his eyes open any longer, and he could not be down there asleep.

## 4. Following

He had come down at dark to bring up the crock and he had not brought up the crock. The crock
was at his elbow on the stair. Georgie was on the barrel head, in the last of the light off
the window, and had been talking since before Ellis sat.

What Ellis noticed, somewhere in the middle of it, was that he had been agreeing.

Not to anything. He could not have named one thing he had agreed to. But there is a noise a
man makes with his mouth shut when another man is telling him something and he wants it known
that he is following, and Ellis had been making that noise, in the right places, for a long
time. He made it again while he was thinking about it. He heard himself do it.

He looked at the door. Eleven stairs and shut, and he had shut it himself on the way down,
out of habit, on account of the cold.

He got up. He was standing, and then he was only standing, and then Georgie said a thing that
wanted the noise, and he made the noise, and sat.

## 5. Not to him

He had come down for the lamp oil. The can was on the shelf beside the stair and he had it in
his hand the whole time.

Georgie was talking, and Georgie was not talking to him.

Ellis had sat down to listen, and that is the honest account of it. A man hears his own name
not said, and works out from the turns and the pauses that whatever this is has been going a
while and has somebody else in it, and he sits down where he is on the stairs to find out
who.

It went the way a man talks to somebody he is fond of and in no hurry with. It stopped and
waited. It went on again when it had what it wanted.

Between Ellis and the top there was nothing at all — eleven boards, a rail he had put on
himself, and a door standing open — and he stayed where he was, because getting up meant
being noticed, and he had got this far without being noticed.

## 6. Eleven

He had come down to see to the window, and the window was fine, and he sat on the stairs to
think about that.

There are eleven stairs. He built the cellar steps himself in the second year, he cut eleven
treads, and there are eleven.

Georgie started up, and Ellis started counting, which he could not afterward account for. He
got to eleven and there were two above him. He did it again from the bottom, out loud, the
way you do it for a child, and it came out eleven, and there were two above him. Georgie
waited the whole time, the way you wait for a man to finish a job you have asked him to do,
and then carried on from where he had left off.

A man does not go up steps he cannot count.

Ellis sat where he was and waited for it to come right. The light went off the window at
about seven. It did not come right.

## 7. Again

He had come down with the light to look for the awl he had dropped through the boards, and he
found it, and he was on his way back up when Georgie said something.

Ellis stopped on the stair and said he was sorry, he had not caught that.

Georgie said it again.

He came back down two steps, which is what you do, and set the light on the tread and turned
his ear to it and said he still had not caught it. Georgie said it again. It was not soft. It
had the shape of a sentence — the lift at the front, the drop at the end, the stop in the
middle where a man takes his breath — and Ellis could not get one word out of it, and after a
while he could not get the shape out of it either, and it went on offering.

He said, out of pure habit, that he would come down and see to it in the morning when the
light was better.

He did not move for a long time after that, because the one thing a man cannot do, having
told somebody he will see to him in the morning, is walk off while he is still saying it.

## 8. The mule

He had come down with the light and he had not gone back up with it, and by ten he had told
Georgie about the fence, the price of moss, his brother in Amite, and a thing his father had
said to him once that he had never told Verna.

Georgie had not said one word.

That was the ordinary condition of it and it had never troubled him before. What Ellis wanted
at ten, and could not manage, was to stop. He would finish a thing and hear the finish in his
own voice, and hear how the cellar took it, flat, with nothing coming back, and then he would
start on something else.

The door at the top stood open and the kitchen was dark behind it, which meant Verna had gone
up without calling him, which she had not done before.

He put his hand on the rail. He said he ought to get on up.

Then he told Georgie about the mule.

## 9. Plain about the stairs

I had gone down for the potatoes and I sat on the stairs, and I want to be plain about the
sitting, because it was never that I could not get up.

I could get up. I got up several times.

What I could not do was be the one who went. You will have sat in a room with a man who is
telling you a thing that matters to him, and you will know there is no place in it where you
can stand. There is no such place. You wait for one and it does not come, and the longer you
wait the worse the standing would be, and after an hour it is out of the question altogether,
and you are a grown man on his own cellar stairs at eleven at night with his knees up under
his chin.

Verna asked me the next day whether I had been down there. I said the drain wanted looking
at. She said the drain had wanted looking at for two years, and left it, and that is the
whole of what has ever been said about it in this house.

## 10. Teague Marsh

He had come down to see about the drain and had been on the stairs since the light went.

Georgie said a name.

It was not a name that had anything to do with anything. Teague Marsh farmed the second place
past the church, and Ellis had bought a heifer off him in the spring, and had no quarrel with
him and never had. Ellis said so. He said it twice, and the second time he heard how it
sounded — a man sitting in the dark in his own cellar, explaining that he had no quarrel with
Teague Marsh — and he stopped.

Georgie did not say the name again, or anything else, for a long while.

Ellis stayed where he was. He would have said, if anybody had asked, that he was waiting to
hear whether it came up a second time, and that is true as far as it goes. What is also true
is that going up those stairs and opening that door and walking into his own kitchen would
have been agreeing that the evening was over, and he could not agree to that with the name
lying there.

He got up a little after two. On the Sunday he was at the church before anybody, watching the
road for Teague Marsh's cart.

## 11. A small thing

He had come down for nothing in particular, which he only understood later, and he sat on the
stairs with his hands hanging between his knees.

What Georgie wanted was a small thing. That was the difficulty with it. If it had been a
large thing he could have said no once and been done, but it was the size of thing a man does
for a neighbour without being thanked, and to refuse it he had to go on refusing it, and each
refusal took a little more saying than the one before.

The door at the top was shut. Beyond it the house was doing what a house does at that hour,
which is nothing. Above that his daughter turned over in her bed and the boards said so.

He said no again. It came out thinner than the last one.

He stood up. What he thought about, standing, was the morning — coming down these stairs in
daylight, in his shirt, to a thing that had been left over from the night before. He sat back
down. He said no again, and it came out almost pleasant.

## 12. Directly

He had been down there since the plates were cleared, and then he heard the kitchen door and
Verna's feet on the top stair, and there was nothing to be done about any of it.

She did not come down. She never came down. She put her head into the stairway the way she
did when she was calling the girls, and said the lamp was low, and was he going to be much
longer.

He said he would be up directly.

Then she said the other thing. She said it going away, in the middle of turning round, in the
voice she kept for the ordinary business of the house, and she was across the kitchen and had
the pump going before Ellis understood that it had not been said to him.

He sat with his hands on his knees. Above him the pump went, and stopped, and Verna set
something down on the table and said something else, pleasant, the way you do when a person
has told you he will be up directly.

Ellis did not go up directly. He worked out that he would have to go up eventually, and that
when he did he would have to say something to her, and he sat on the fourth stair with the
light beside him for another two hours, trying to find the thing a man says.

## What varied

**Six answers to *what does Georgie say*, none of them quoted:**

- **Only the timing.** The stops, the waiting, the pace. (5, 6)
- **Only Ellis's half** — and not even that, only the *noise* he makes instead of a half. (4)
- **Reported the way you report a dull conversation.** Same ground, third hour, a grievance
  about land. Domestic register laid over the content. (3)
- **The shape without the words** — the lift at the front, the drop at the end, the stop where
  a man takes his breath. (7)
- **One word only**, a name, with nothing round it. (10)
- **Nothing at all.** Georgie does not speak; Ellis cannot stop. (8)

**Six reasons he does not go up, none of them a hold:**

- He has been asked something and has not answered. (1, 10)
- He would arrive in his own kitchen wearing this. (2)
- Getting up means being noticed, and so far he has not been. (5)
- You cannot walk off mid-sentence, and there is never a place to stand. (3, 9)
- Going up leaves it there to be come down to in daylight. (11)
- He has to find the thing a man says to his wife first. (12)

Registers, kept one to a piece so no two renderings of fear share a kind: motor (1, 4),
spatial (2, 5), cognitive (6, 7), temporal (3), social (9, 11), household (12).

## Two decisions made without asking

1. **Georgie is never described.** Not the face, not the container, not the state of it. Every
   piece says *Georgie*, puts Georgie somewhere plain — the boards, the barrel head, under the
   window — and looks away. That is a real choice and it is reversible: say the word and I
   will write three that look straight at it. D-003 would want that late, once, and vegetal.
2. **Georgie takes the name and never a pronoun.** *Georgie said*, never *it said* or *he
   said*. It keeps the reference clean with two males in a dark room, and the flat repetition
   of the name does more than either pronoun would.

**Not written, and available:** a version where he gets all the way up, opens the door, and
comes back down; a version that is only the two hours with no scene around it; a version where
the door at the top is opened from the other side by one of the children.
