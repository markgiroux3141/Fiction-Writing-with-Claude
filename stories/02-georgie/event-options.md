## Three things in the seed that fail doctrine as stated

Recorded before generating, because each one changes which candidates are even legal.

### 1. "He imagines it is talking" is the thesis D-004 forbids

If the story's position is that the voice is his guilt, the reader can finish it and
write the one-line answer to *what was it* — which is D-004's only real test, and
`craft-principles.md` §4's *a thing that is the shape of grief is an explanation* with
guilt substituted for grief. It is also the single most-written version of this
premise: *The Tell-Tale Heart* and *The Black Cat* are both this exact machine.

**What survives the fix:** madness as *consequence* rather than as *cause*. He may
deteriorate; the story may never say the deterioration is doing the talking. The
cheapest structural guarantee is corroboration — somebody else in the story behaves as
though the thing is there, without ever discussing it. Corroboration is not mechanism;
L-016 bans workings, not witnesses.

### 2. A murderer arrives with a motive, and a motive is a mechanism

L-021: *"A protagonist who does something monstrous supplies a motive, and a motive is
a mechanism (L-016)."* The story cannot say why he did it. Not self-defence, not money,
not a woman, not provocation — any of those is an explanation attached to the worst
fact in the story, which is the alibi failure running in the opposite direction from
story 01's.

The L-021 question, answered once in writing as STATUS.md requires: **what does this
story decline to let its protagonist be?** Candidate answer, for Mark to accept or
replace: *it declines to let him be sorry.* No remorse beat, no understanding beat, and
never the sentence in which he grasps what he has done.

### 3. There are no basements in south Louisiana

Water table. It is one of the few object errors a reader would actually hear, and D-005
protects register while `style-guide.md` still demands the objects be right. Available
substitutes, none of which cost the seed anything:

- the space between the brick piers, under the floor, which every house has
- a brick cistern, and a **drought** — low water in late summer is when things that
  were under water are not (`reference/period/swamp-and-weather.md`)
- a moss shed, where grey moss is hung for weeks to rot down to the black fibre
- a smokehouse; a root cellar built up on a mound; a sugar-house pit
- an above-ground tomb or a coping — the region's actual answer to wet ground
- **or move the story to real cellar country** — the Felicianas, the Natchez bluffs,
  the north Louisiana hills — and keep the word

## Step 0b — the reader's three guesses, banned

1. **He is caught, or gives himself away.** The confession, the floorboards, the
   officers in the parlour. It is Poe's ending and it resolves the story.
2. **It talks him into another killing.** The head as instrument; the escalation the
   premise most obviously offers.
3. **It was never there / it is his own head / he is the dead one.** The reveal, which
   the whole book forbids.

Also banned as `ai-tells.md` §F modal events: the corpse that is warm or has shifted,
the figure at the foot of the bed, footsteps stopping outside the door, the door that
will not stay shut, the clock stopped at the hour of death used as a signal.

  the **ordinary world** must beat him — the water, the season, a levee crew digging, a

## Candidates

Six axes, chosen by **who is affected** rather than by mood (protocol Part 2, rule 3).
Five candidates are seeded from `reference/period/` and are marked ※.

### A — It uses him

- **A1.** It asks for things a household gives a guest, and all of them are cheap: the door left a hand's width, the lamp left burning, not to be spoken to before noon. He does all of them. The one he refuses is the one nobody in the story ever names.
- **A2.** It says a name once — a living man six miles off, with whom he has no quarrel — and never mentions him again. Nothing whatever happens to that man. He is alive at the end, and the narrator goes on checking, without reason, for the rest of his life.
- **A3.** It wants him to *talk*, not to answer: the way you talk to a man laid up. The price of moss, whose fence is down, what the water did. He gets good at it. What he notices, late, is that he has stopped talking to anyone upstairs.
- **A4.** He cannot make out a word it says. He asks it to repeat, and it does, and he still cannot, and it goes on repeating. He is down there four hours. He says the word out loud in company the following week without knowing he has.
- **A5.** It has him bring things down. A chair. Then his father's coat. Then a second cup. He is furnishing a room, and nobody asks why there is a chair down there, which he takes for a while as luck.

### B — It affects the household

- **B1.** His wife says the name first. She has never been down there, does not know there is anything down there, and says it while doing something else, the way you say a name you have always said.
- **B2.** The children begin leaving the door open. Not going down — opening it, walking away, doing something else. He shuts it thirty times a week, never once catches them, and they are not lying when they say it wasn't them.
- **B3.** Somebody has been feeding it. There is a plate. The plate is one of the good ones, and it has been washed and put back.
- **B4.** ※ The boarder — a moss-picker taking a room for the season — tells him the man in the cellar has a bad chest and ought to sit up more. He says it the way you would say it about anybody. He has been in the house eleven days.
- **B5.** His daughter, who is nine and cannot read, brings him a paper with a word printed carefully on it. It is not a word she would know. She says the man downstairs spelled it for her and she is bored of the game now.

### C — It affects the district

- **C1.** A neighbour calls from the road that he heard them talking last night and could not make out the words, but the one voice was going on some. The window was shut. It is a good hundred and forty yards.
- **C2.** The priest comes on the ordinary round and asks after the sick one in the house. Not fishing, not testing — working off a list somebody in the parish keeps.
- **C3.** A stranger in town asks him to pass on a message. Not to anybody named. *Tell him I said I'd come.*
- **C4.** ※ On All Saints', with the whole parish out whitewashing and dressing the tombs, one coping has already been done that belongs to nobody — limed, swept, flowers laid. Four families stand at it in the sun working out whose it is. He knows the name that should be on it, and he goes on standing there.
- **C5.** The district settles on the sound being his wife, and is sorry for him about it. Nobody says so to his face. He can tell by how they are with her.

### D — It affects the victim's own people

- **D1.** The dead man's brother comes looking, and stays the night because that is what you do, and the two of them sit up late and it is not a bad evening. In the morning he is thanked, with real warmth, for the message.
- **D2.** The dead man's mother writes to him, by name, at his house, asking him to keep on with what he is doing, and thanking him, and enclosing something small.
- **D3.** Nobody comes looking at all. No search, no notice, no name in the paper. Whoever the man was, he is not missed by anybody, and that is a worse fact to live beside than a search would have been.
- **D4.** ※ The dead man's wife asks whether he will take on the crop lien her husband left. He does. It is the fairest arrangement he has ever made and she thanks him in front of people — and he now owes the parish an account of a man he cannot produce.

### E — It happens to his body (D-003: vegetal, late, once)

- **E1.** ※ Something is rooting in the roof of his mouth. It is not sore. He gets at it with a finger and what comes away is fibre — black and springy, the way cured moss is once the grey has rotted off it.
- **E2.** His beard comes in wrong on one side and will not shave. The razor goes over it and it is still there, and it is not hair.
- **E3.** He stops being able to shut one eye. Only the one. He holds it down with two fingers to sleep, and the fingers have to stay there.
- **E4.** His voice goes — not hoarse, but dropping over a season and settling somewhere it was not. His wife stops turning round when he speaks behind her.
- **E5.** He cannot lift his corner of a coffin. Four men and him, in daylight, in front of the parish, and his arms simply do not answer, and he has to be stepped around.
- **E6.** ※ Water comes out of him. He is not wet anywhere else. It comes from the corners of the eyes, it is not tears, it has the smell of the cistern in it, and it happens in the middle of ordinary sentences.

### F — It affects whoever comes after

- **F1.** He sells and moves nine miles, and the new man rides over friendly to say the cellar's fine, he's got it whitewashed — and by the way, what was the name? Because the wife's been calling it that and neither of them can think where they got it.
- **F2.** His son, grown, asks him for the house. Not for the land. He wants the house specifically, and he has a reason, and it is a good one, and he will not say it.
- **F3.** The house burns. He stands in the road with the parish and watches it go, and nothing about the arrangement changes at all.
- **F4.** He hires a mason to fill the cellar in. The man does the work and takes his money and, at the end, asks whether he wants a way left in — the way you would ask a man about a cat.

## Inversions of the premise itself (protocol Part 2, rule 6)

Not events; whole-story turns. Also unranked, and compatible with most of the pool
above.

- **Invert the concealment.** It is not hidden. It is in the house openly, the
  household has absorbed it, and there is no concealment plot at all — the horror is
  entirely accommodation, with nothing to discover.
- **Invert the target.** It does not talk to him. It talks to everyone else, and he is
  the only one it has never once addressed.
- **Invert who breaks.** He is calm, competent and functional throughout. His wife is
  the one going, and she has never seen it.
- **Invert the object.** The head is not what he kept. He put the head in the water and
  kept the rest, and the head is what came back.
- **Invert the ownership.** He does not have it. It has the house, and he is the part
  of the arrangement that can be moved.
