# The Empty Deck — Five Renderings

Plate: `the-empty-deck.png` (`plate-inventory.md` § VII). Mark, 2026-09-11: *the empty deck
is also quite good*, alongside `the-ones-at-the-rail`.

What the picture holds: a sail bagged along its boom and **half stopped off**, falls and coils
hanging from the pin rail, bitts, the horizon in the gap at the right, and nobody. The work is
not tidy and it is not wrecked. It is interrupted, and left the way a job is left when
somebody means to come back to it.

Five versions. They contradict each other. No plot, no mechanism, and in four of the five
nobody establishes what happened to the watch, because that is not the story — the story is
whoever is standing where the camera is standing.

**Deliberately split on the open water question** (`notes.md` §2, contested): #1 and #4 are
inshore, #2, #3 and #5 are offshore. Read them against each other and the register argument
settles itself, or it does not and I was wrong about it.

---

## 1 — Another man's hand

A sail left half stopped off will take the wind out of the gaskets that are on it and then it
will take the boom, so Naquin finished the job, because he was the one who found it and there
was nobody on deck to give it to. Eleven stops to pass. It took him four minutes.

He came back at noon to coil down the falls that had been left hanging, and he stood at the
pin rail a while instead.

A man's coils are his own. You learn it off whoever teaches you and then you do it that way
for fifty years — the lay of it, how many turns, which way you hang the bight. Naquin had
been coiling rope for thirty-one years the way his uncle in Golden Meadow coiled rope.

Every fall on the port pin rail was coiled. Not his way. Not the mate's way, and not the
boy's, who did not coil at all so much as gather.

He coiled them again, his way, all eleven, and he was not quick about it, and after that he
was no good in company, because he had got into the habit of looking at what other men's
hands had done and being unable to leave it.

## 2 — Before the coppers

Theriot came up the fore hatch at a quarter past eleven into a deck with the work set down in
it and not one man on it, and then he did the sensible thing, which is that he went and looked
for them, and he found them. Eight men and the mate, below, where men are below, and nothing
whatever the matter.

They had been called to dinner, was what Theriot had thought, all the way aft.

The cook had not rung. The coppers were not lit; there was no smoke at the galley funnel and
the fire was laid but not struck, which Theriot could see from where he stood, because the
galley door was hooked open the way it is hooked open all day in July.

Theriot asked him anyway. The cook said he would ring at noon the way he rang at noon.

Theriot went back up and finished stopping the sail off himself and said nothing to anybody,
and what it cost him was the meal, which he did not eat, and then a great many other meals
after that, until his clothes wanted taking in and the mate started watching him at the
table.

## 3 — Thirty seconds

Lasseigne wanted a marlinspike and his was in his chest, which is three steps down the
companion and eleven along, and he was gone out of the daylight for as long as that takes
twice.

When he came up, four more stops were passed.

He counted them, because there is nothing else a man can do. Seven when he went down and
eleven when he came up, and eleven is the number you pass on that boom, and the four new ones
were hitched the way he hitches them and hauled to the tension he hauls them to, and they
were the four he had been about to pass.

He put the spike back in his pocket.

For the rest of his life Lasseigne carried his tools on him. Spike, knife, palm, a fid,
dividers he had no use for at sea — in his pockets and on a lanyard round his neck, in a
shipyard and later in a hardware store in Morgan City, where a man does not need any of it,
and where they thought he was afraid of theft.

## 4 — The one who did not know

Dartez was a consignee's clerk and went along that once to see the cargo in, and he knew
nothing about a boat except which end of it he was told to stay out of.

Dartez came up at four and said the deck looked wrong.

They asked him what was wrong with it and he could not tell them, because he had no words —
he said the ropes were put down half-finished, which is what he could see, and a man who
does not know what a coil is cannot say more than that. The mate told him that half-finished
was what a deck looked like between one job and the next, which was true, and Numa told him
it was a working boat and not a parlour, which was also true, and they were kind enough
about it.

Dartez was the only man aboard who was right, and the reason he was right is that he was the
only one who did not know what he was looking at.

He did not go again. He sent a boy the next season and the boy went every season after, and
Dartez never said why, because the reason was that a deck had looked wrong to him once and
nine men who knew better had told him it did not.

## 5 — Where the camera stood

The plate came to me with his other things, and I have had eleven years to work out where he
put the tripod, and I can tell you it was not on that deck.

You cannot see aft along a deck like that from the deck. You are looking down on the boom
from above and abaft it, and there is nothing above and abaft it but rigging, so he was in
the fore crosstrees or on a staging slung off them, and a wet plate wants a tent, a bath, a
bottle of collodion and ten seconds of a man standing still.

He went aloft with all of it. Alone, because nobody is in the picture. He poured and
sensitised and exposed and came down and developed inside his ten minutes, which I have timed
on a ladder in my own yard and cannot do.

I have the day-book too. Every plate he made that season is entered in it by number, and
there are four hundred and six of them, and this one is not there.

What I have not been able to do is sell it, and I have not been able to burn it either, and
it has been in the same drawer as his day-book for eleven years while I have gone on being a
man who intends to do one or the other.

## What varied

| # | Who is affected | The wrongness | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The man who finished the job | Eleven coils in a hand that is not anyone's aboard | He can no longer leave other men's work alone |
| 2 | The man who looked for them | They are all below, and the galley fire is not lit | He stops eating at that table |
| 3 | The man who went for a tool | Four stops passed in thirty seconds, in his own hitch | Thirty years of carrying tools he has no use for |

**Where the picture's own instrument is.** Four of these five put the reader where the camera
is and leave it there. #5 is the exception and it is the one that explains the most, which is
the argument against it — see below.

**Three notes on what I refused:**

1. **The watch is never accounted for.** Nobody searches, nobody is missing, no muster. Four
   of the five never establish what happened to the men who set the work down, because the
   moment the story takes that question up it becomes a story about a disappearance, and a
   disappearance wants solving. The deck is the event. #2 goes furthest — he finds them, and
   they are fine, and that is worse.
2. **The mundane explanation dies once, to one person** (beat index). In #2 it is the cook,
   asked one question, and the fire is out and that is the end of it. Not a survey: Theriot
   does not check the bell, the watch list, the mate's log and the glass. A fact needs no
   lawyer.
3. **No hitch anybody can trace.** The first version of #1 had the stops passed with an old
   hitch that the oldest man aboard recognised from his father. That is an **origin** (L-016)
   wearing a costume, and it turns a wrong deck into a lineage. The coils are in *nobody's*
   hand, which cannot be traced anywhere, and Naquin's thirty-one years of his uncle's coil
   is there to make the reader feel how legible a man's rope is — not to hand him a suspect.

**The problem with #5, stated plainly.** It is the strongest-written of the five and it is
the one I would not choose. It shares its whole instrument with `the-ones-at-the-rail` —
*the impossible thing is in the making of the plate* — and the two plates cannot both be
spent on that, or the book has two stories about a photograph nobody could have taken. It
also leans on the found-album frame, which is still an open ⚠ in `book-bible.md`. Worth
having on the page so the choice is a choice.

**Checks:** banned phrases zero, anachronism grep zero, reference sweep run. Four faults
fixed, all in #2 and #4 — a *he* attaching to a man the sentence had just said was not there,
a *what he thought* that could have been the mate, an *asked him* with two men in it, and a
paragraph-opening *He* three paragraphs from its name. The tool reads sentence-initial words
as names (*Eleven*, *Four*, *Not*), which is noise; the four above are not.

**Not written, and available:** the version where the deck is right and the sea is wrong; the
version where he is the one who set the work down and does not remember doing it (flag: that
is a memory mechanism and L-016 is close); the version where the job gets finished every
time, faster each time, and the crew starts leaving work out on purpose.
