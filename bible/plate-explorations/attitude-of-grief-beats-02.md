## What those four have in common

Written down because it is aimable, and because L-002 says it is my job to find the mechanism
behind what you liked rather than hand the experience back. **Not a rule yet** — one round of
one reader — and if the second batch confirms it, it earns an L-ID then.

1. **The line is short, flat, and last.** No adjective is doing any work. *Stone does not hang.*
2. **Nobody in the scene reacts.** No fear is named or performed on the page; the reaction is left entirely to the reader, who has nowhere to put it.
3. **The register is ordinary competence** — physics, manners, bookkeeping. A small true statement about how the world works, which the thing has quietly broken.
4. **It arrives obliquely, in somebody else's mouth or somebody else's habit** — a child correcting you, an old man's hat, a parish that has learned a route nobody taught it.
5. **It re-describes what is already in view** rather than introducing an event. Nothing happens. The same object is simply named correctly, and the correct name is worse.

Twelve more, all built on those. Names are placeholders and the beats contradict each other
on purpose.

## 1. The rake

On All Saints' every monument in that ground gets used. Rakes leaned against crosses, a coat
folded over an arm of the Terrebonne stone, buckets set down on copings, men sitting on
plinths to eat their dinner. By noon the whole cemetery is a room that people are working in.
Nobody leans anything against the figure. Not a hat, not a broom, not once in the eleven
years Théo had been coming out, and there is no rule about it and nobody has ever said a word
to him on the subject. He tried it that year, on purpose, to see: stood the rake handle up
against the plinth. His hand took it back before he had finished deciding to.

## 2. The drying

After the Tuesday rain everything dried the way things dry. The copings went pale from the
edges inward, because the edges are thin. The shell path went grey and then white. The cross
lightened at the top first. The figure's drapery dried from the shoulder down and stopped
in a straight dark line at the hem, the way a dress dries on a line, because the water goes
down and gathers at the bottom and leaves last. Boudreaux knew both of those things. He had
never before had to hold the two of them in his head at the same time.

## 3. Frost

The hard freeze came in the second week of January and by seven the ground was white all over
— every coping, both crosses, the flat stones, the shell path gone stiff enough to hear
underfoot. There was no frost anywhere on the figure but the wings. On the wings it lay thick,
and it did not come off them until close on noon. Frost settles on whatever was coldest in the
night.

## 4. His name

The Prejean boy was six and had been walking the rows spelling names out, because that is what
you do with a child who is learning his letters. He stopped in front of the figure and waited
for her to start him off. There's nothing to read, she said. There's no plate on it. He
thought about that a while. He said everybody out here has got their name put on them so you
know which one they are. She said that was right. He said so how does anybody know.

## 5. At him

Her aunt said the angel was watching over Grandpère, which is the thing you say. Marguerite
said it wasn't watching over him, it was watching *at* him. Her aunt let that go, the way you
let a child's grammar go. They finished the plot and put the shears in the basket and started
down the path, and at the gate the child wanted to know whether Grandpère could see it back.

## 6. The hat

Prejean raised his hat to it. He was seventy-one and he did it going past, the same way he did
it at the Widow Ancelet's gate, and he did not know he had done it until his wife asked him
why. He said he hadn't. She said you did, I was behind you and I watched you do it. They
weeded the plot and put out the flowers and started back. He raised it again on the way out,
and this time he felt his arm go up.

## 7. The long way with a coffin

Six men carrying a coffin take the shortest line there is, because a coffin gets heavier every
yard and every one of them knows it before they start. The Landry funeral went round by the
far cross, which added forty feet over wet ground and a turn at the end that they had to
shuffle. Nobody had discussed it. Father Ancelet was walking in front and he went that way,
and the men went after him because he was in front. Afterwards not one of them could say who
had decided it.

## 8. The ivy

Ivy takes anything it can get a foot into, and in that ground it has taken everything — both
crosses, the Terrebonne coping, the brick of the old wall, and a marble lamb so completely
that you can only find it now by kicking along the row until your boot stops. It comes up to
the figure's plinth and stops, all the way round, in a line you could follow with a finger.
There is nothing wrong with the plinth. Théo has picked up a double handful and laid it against
the stone himself, more than once, just to see. It does what cut ivy does.

## 9. The price

The monument man came out to look at the cleaning and did what monument men do, which is rap
the stone with a knuckle in three or four places to hear what he is dealing with. He did the
plinth twice and the drapery once. Then he wrote his figure on a card and handed it over, and
it was a fair price for marble and they were pleased with it. He was most of the way to the
gate when he stopped and stood looking back at it for a while. He came and took the card, and
crossed the figure out, and wrote it again lower, and did not say anything about why.

## 10. Forward

You set a figure facing the path so it can be seen from the gate, and the mason had done that
properly. Odile had never once thought about the wings until the wet spring, when she came out
after three days of rain and found the ground at the foot of the plot dry, in the shape of
them. A figure that is meant to be looked at has its wings out flat, for the look of the
thing. These came forward.

## 11. The quiet stretch

That ground carries sound; he has worked in it thirty years and can tell you which family is
in it by listening from the shed. They talk the whole way up the shell path and they talk the
whole way back down. In between there is a stretch of about twenty yards where every family he
has ever heard goes quiet, and picks up again on the other side of it, and not one of them
knows they do it. He has never mentioned this to anybody. The priest does it too.

## 12. The colour of its hair

She sat down to write to her sister in Mobile, who had never seen the plot, and she got as far
as the wings and the drapery and the way the hands hang. Then she stopped, because the next
thing was the face, and she found she did not have it. Four times a year for nineteen years.
She could not have told her sister the colour of its hair. It does not have a colour. It is
stone.

## Scored 2026-09-09

**Landed:** 1 (*"an unspoken respect for the figure"*), 4 and 5 (*"children point out obvious
things that adults wouldn't... very effective at pointing out an unsettling fact"*;
*"whether Grandpère could see it back"* named as the line), 10 (*"the wings serve a purpose
that isn't in line with a typical statue... different in a way that is underdetermined"*),
11 (*"a subtle indication of respect"*).

**Missed, and the reasons are the finding:**
- **6, the hat.** *"The respect of a hat tip isn't as powerful as not laying tools on it
  because the latter could imply fear where the former, not really."* → **Deference is inert.
  A behaviour only frightens if fear is the only thing that explains it.** Courtesy has an
  innocent reading; not touching does not. Build avoidances and omissions, never gestures.
- **7, the coffin.** *"Seems routine... isn't as impactful as someone avoiding walking past
  the figure due to fear, and trying to convince themselves that that isn't what they are
  doing."* → Two faults: a group decision reads as **procedure**, which launders it; and there
  was **no denial attached**. The self-deception is the instrument, and it wants to be in a
  voice rather than reported.
- **8 and 9, flat.** No reason given, and my read is that both **argue their case** — the ivy
  reasons the anomaly out, the monument man's lowered price is a puzzle to solve. *Stone does
  not hang* states and stops. Same family as L-018: the event does not need a lawyer.

**Acted on in `attitude-of-grief-prose-01.md`** — first person, so the denial happens in the
sentences; every behaviour an omission; the two physical anomalies one sentence each.
