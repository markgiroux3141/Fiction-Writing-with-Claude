## 1r — Eleven a day

Delphine Cobb took faces out for a dollar sixty a day.

A face comes out with the knife, in short strokes across the grain, and then the ground goes
back over it with a brush so that a person looking at the print reads nothing at all. Two
tools and two stages, and they do not live in the same room. The knife is in her roll on the
desk. The white and the ground brush are on the shelf in the front room over Mr. Rey's desk,
because the white is dear and you sign for what you take.

She came to this one after dinner and started it at ten past one.

At a quarter to three her hand shut.

She has had cramp in that hand since she was nineteen and cramp lets go if you work the
thumb, so she worked the thumb. What the hand had hold of was the knife, and the knife was on
the print, and the whole arrangement went on moving — small, even, across the grain, at the
speed it had been going for an hour and a half — and Delphine watched the back of her own
hand do it from about a foot away.

She stood up.

The desk came with her. The print was pinned to it and the knife was in the print and her
fingers were around the knife, and she had the near legs an inch off the boards before she
understood what she was lifting, and a bottle went over and ran off the edge. She sat back
down.

That is the part she has never told anybody, and it is the part she goes back to. Not the
hand. That her first thought was to get away from her own arm and her second was that Mr. Rey
would hear the bottle — and that the second one arrived while the hand was still working.

Then it stopped.

She opened the fingers one at a time with her other hand and laid the knife in the roll.

The ground was already on. Brushed, stippled, carried off into the sky at the edges so the
join did not show, which is twenty minutes with a brush that was on the shelf in the other
room, dry, in the jar where it lives, and the white was in the book with nobody's name
against it.

Mr. Rey held the print up to the window for a long time.

He said it was the best knifing she had done for him. He said she was to do the rest of the
crate to the same standard, and he put her up to a dollar seventy-five.

The crate is under her desk where it has been since July. There are three hundred and forty
faces left in it.

## What changed, and why each change was made

| First version | Second version | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Four paragraphs of trade practice before anything happens | Two sentences of frame: the wage, the two tools, the two rooms | L-028, L-009 — the frame is the activity, the enabling fact and the map, not a lecture |
| The impossible thing is *the retouch was finished and she did not finish it* — a fact about a procedure, which cannot reach her | The impossible thing is *her hand went on working and would not open* — the same fact aimed at a person | L-026, L-012 test 1 |
| No fear anywhere. She sits with her hand in her lap until it opens, then goes looking for a knife-stroke | Involuntary event (the hand), the knowing (cramp lets go; this did not), the misfired action (standing up, lifting the desk, the bottle) | L-001, L-018 |
| The mundane explanation is never raised | Cramp is raised and killed in one clause, by the one person who would know, and never mentioned again | Beat index — once, not a survey |
| Ends on a wry raise, which reads as a punchline | Ends on the same raise, followed by the count of what she has been commissioned to do | Irony is on the bible's *Not:* list; the frightening thing goes at the end |
| Two paragraphs of forensic search for the stroke where her hand changed its mind | Cut entirely | It was the writer being interested, and it turned the event into a puzzle |

**The one thing kept from v1 that was doing real work:** the ground already on, and the dry
brush in the other room, and the white unsigned-for. In v1 that was the event and it was too
small to be one. Here it is corroboration arriving *after* the fear, which is where a
procedural detail belongs — it tells the reader something true that nobody in the story will
say out loud.

**Pre-flight item 0, answered in writing:**
**(a) What happens to somebody?** Her hand is used, with her awake and watching, and she
cannot stop it or let go of the knife. **(b) Where would a reader put the book down?** *She
stood up. / The desk came with her.*

**Still open.** Passages 2–5 have the same defect in different proportions. #3 and #5 are the
least guilty — both already have a frightening fact in them (the tender that never lowered a
boat; the fingers going without her knowing) — but neither has a body responding to it, so
both need the same operation. #2 and #4 need more than that. I have not touched them, because
the diagnosis should be confirmed on one before it is applied to four.
