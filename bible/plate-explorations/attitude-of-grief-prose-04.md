# The Angel — Ti-Jean, in Close Third

`wet plate photographs/the-attitude-of-grief.png`

Mark, 2026-09-10: *"I'm not sure I'm sold on the narrator talking in first person and being
part of the story... It's almost like a calm voice of a narrator is playing in my head, but a
person in a horror story should not be calm. You can get away from this by talking about other
people and being separate from it."* Plus: drop the *nineteen years* tallies, and — *"perhaps
the figure went up around the time the mother died, but no one can remember actually seeing it
go up, or know anyone that put it up. There's simply people that remember a time when they took
the short path through the cemetery and walked a little slower, and a time after."*

## What was wrong with the voice

**It was retrospective**, which is composed by construction: thirty years on, at a table,
putting it down, having survived it. A memoir is a settled thing and horror is an unsettled
one. It also did the reader's work — *I could not stand to watch* announces the fear instead of
producing it.

Close third, in scene, fixes both. Elmire watches other people; her fear happens in her body
and she never names it; and the reader gets to notice things about her that she does not.

**The tallies are gone.** *Nineteen years of that. I did it for nineteen years.* Duration is an
aftermath instrument and I was using it as a cadence — and it was not even true, since nobody
leads a baby away from a statue. His age is given once.

**Nobody can date the figure.** Better than my version, and it takes the mother-link down to a
temperature rather than a fact.

## 1. In the store

Ovide had the flour down and was making out the ticket when the Guidry boy came in behind her,
and Ovide said good morning to him, friendly enough, and said it to his hands.

Elmire had seen him do that before and had never thought about it. She thought about it now
because Ti-Jean answered — he always answered, he was well brought up in that one way — and
Ovide went on writing and went on looking at the boy's hands until it was over.

Then Ti-Jean said good morning to her.

She looked at his hands. There was flour on the counter and a smear of it on his knuckles where
he had put them down. She made herself look up and say it back to him properly, the way you
would to anybody, and she got it out, and it was worse looking up, and she was looking at his
hands again before she had finished the sentence.

## 2. His hats

His aunt made them. She took a child's hat and got the band out of it and cut it down and
worked it round again, and it was an evening's work and she never made anything of it. His
shoes were a child's shoes and had to be sent for. His coat was a man's coat, because a coat
does not have to fit.

## 3. Léonide

She had been Elmire's cousin and she had been twenty-two. It was a long birth and a bad one and
the doctor came out on the second morning to find there was nothing to do that had not been
done, and she lived four days after that.

There is a way of telling it in that family in which the child did it to her. It has never once
been said out loud where Elmire could hear it. Everybody in that house has heard it.

He went to his aunt because his aunt had the room.

## 4. Come see the lamb

He was nineteen and he went for the figure every time he was taken out there.

Not fast. He would be beside you, and then he would be four steps off and going up the middle
of the path, the way a child goes at a dog.

Elmire's hand was on his shoulder before she had thought about it, and she turned him, and said
come on now, Ti-Jean, come see the lamb, and he came. He was easy and he always came. He did
not look back at it, which she noticed and did not think about until much later, because a
child hauled off a dog looks back.

She got him down to the lamb and set his hand on it and he was pleased with it, and she stood
there with her heart going for no reason she would have given, and got her breath, and went
back to the coping.

## 5. Nobody saw it go up

Nobody could tell you when it came, which is not the same as nobody knowing.

Elmire had gone at it sideways twice, at a wedding and at a burial, and got the same shape of
answer both times: that it had always been there — and then a pause, and then the weather.

Madame Dugas was ninety-one and could give you the price of hogs in any year you cared to name.
She said she believed it had gone up around the time they lost Léonide. Then she sat a moment
and said she did not know why she had said that, and asked after Elmire's boy.

What people had instead of a date was themselves. Ovide had taken the short way through the
cemetery to the store his whole life, and he went round by the road now. In between there had
been a while when he still cut through and walked it faster than he used to, and if you had
asked him he would have said the shell was bad in there.

## 6. Maman

He called the figure maman.

He called Father Ancelet's housekeeper maman and she was seventy and she liked it. He called
Elmire's mother maman. He called the mare maman, and Elmire's brother thought that was the
best thing he had ever heard and used to get him to do it in front of company.

So it was nothing. It was a word he had, and he put it on things he was fond of.

## 7. The Saturday before All Saints'

There were forty people in that ground by nine in the morning with buckets and brushes and
their own dead to see to.

Elmire was three rows up doing her coping with her back to the middle of the ground, which is
how the north row runs, and she straightened to get the ache out and saw him go by on the shell
path.

He was going up the middle of it at his own pace with his hat on.

She had the brush in her hand. There were forty people in there. Ovide was in there and his
aunt was in there and Father Ancelet was in there, and the girl who was eleven was in there
somewhere with the flowers, and any one of them was nearer to that path than Elmire was.

She turned round and finished the coping. It took her the best part of an hour and she did it
properly, and afterwards she could not have said one thing about doing it.

## 8. What Ovide said

Ovide found him and Théo Landry was with him. Neither of those men ever said what they saw,
then or after, and both were asked by people with more right to ask than Elmire had.

She stood in Ovide's yard that evening and would not go until he gave her something, and he
gave her one thing, looking at the fence.

They could not get his hand down.

She never asked either of them another question about it.

## 9. His hat

The sexton had seen him get there twice, years before, on days when nobody was minding, and had
come out of the shed and led him off himself.

He told Elmire, at the wake, because it was the only thing he had that was worth anything to
anybody: that the boy went up and set his hat on the plinth first, the way a man sets his hat
down when he comes into a house, and then went on.

His hat was on the plinth when they found him.

So he had not been in a hurry. He had gone up there and put his hat down, which takes a moment,
and which nobody does if there is anything at all the matter.

That was the part Elmire could not put anywhere. Not what had been wrong with him afterwards.
That he had been comfortable.

## Notes

**What close third bought.** Her fear is now a thing the reader watches happen to her body —
the sentence she cannot finish while looking up, her heart going at the lamb for no reason she
would have given, an hour of coping she cannot account for — and she never once names it. The
denial survives; it just stopped being confessed.

**What it cost.** The book's first-person debt is not paid here. That debt is real but it does
not have to be paid by this story, and it should never again be paid in the retrospective
register, which is where the calm came from.

**The mother-link is fainter and better.** Nobody can date the figure; the one person who put a
date on it withdrew it in the next breath; and *maman* is still undercut by the mare. There is
nothing left to confirm and the temperature is unchanged.

**Doctrine.** Two lines, both narrowing rather than adding: the beat index's duration entry
gains *never as cadence — a tally that decorates is a tic, and check it against the character's
actual age*; and L-006 gains the note that **retrospective first person is composed by
construction** and is the wrong register for fear, whatever else it is good for.
