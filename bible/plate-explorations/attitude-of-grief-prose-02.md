# The Angel — The One Who Isn't Afraid

`wet plate photographs/the-attitude-of-grief.png`

Mark, 2026-09-09, on the graveside scene: *"This goes too far, almost to the point of comedy.
People avoiding going near this thing like magnets with the same polarity... The avoidance
works because it's subtle, because it could be overridden in principle, but never is. Testing
this by forcing 40 people near it and them all still resisting makes it seem more like a
supernatural force field... Something better would be someone perhaps young or different in
some way is seemingly immune to the repulsion of the figure. Perhaps they walk up to it and
caress the face or touch the wings. Or perhaps someone has a dream of doing this and wakes up
with a fright."*

## The diagnosis, and it is L-016 in a new coat

**A felt rule must never be tested at scale.** The graveside put twenty people in one place
and had every one of them obey, observably, at once — which is no longer a feeling, it is a
**demonstrated consistency**, and a demonstrated consistency is a mechanism. The scene proved
the thing has a property. The moment it has a property it has rules, and the enigma is over.

The right scale is **one person at a time, deniable, and overridable in principle**: walking a
little faster and not slowing down, because slowing down would be a decision.

**And the way to establish it is to break it.** Somebody who does not feel it proves that
everybody else does, without a single sentence asserting so — and proves the avoidance lives
in the parish rather than in the object, which is exactly where the ambiguity needs it to be.

Nestor Guidry, below, is that person. He also answers, without being asked to, the question
left open in shape D: who keeps that plot.

---

## 1. Where I walk faster

There is a stretch of the shell path where I walk faster. I know where it begins and I could
put my hand on the piece of shell it begins at. I have never slowed down through there, not
once in thirty-one years, and I am not going to start, because slowing down would be a thing I
had decided to do, and walking fast is not a thing anybody decides. I have a great deal to get
through in a day. I have always walked fast. My mother walked fast and her people were quick
on their feet and it is in the family.

## 2. Nestor

Nestor Guidry is forty-two and has never been right, which everybody knows and nobody makes
anything of. He carries for the store and he splits wood for four or five households including
ours and he is better with a horse than any man in the parish. He is out at that cemetery most
days of the week because he likes it in there.

He does not feel it.

I do not know a plainer way to say it than that. He goes up the middle of the shell path at
the same pace the whole way and he talks the whole way, out loud, to himself or to whoever is
in there, and he does not stop talking at the Terrebonne coping and he does not start again at
the cross, because he never stopped.

## 3. What he does when he gets there

He sets his hat on the plinth.

Then he takes the ivy off the base and the grass out of the joint at the back, and he brushes
the leaves off the feet with his hand, and he stands there and finishes whatever he had been
saying on the way up. When he goes he takes his hat off the plinth and puts it on.

I have watched him do it from our plot with my hands still in the coping. I have watched him
do it a hundred times. There is nothing to it. He does the same for the Widow Ancelet's steps
and he does it for nothing, and if you tried to pay him for either he would think you had made
a mistake.

## 4. Her forehead

He put his hand on the face.

Flat, the whole hand, up under the hair and across the brow, the way you do to a child at
night when you are checking whether there is a fever. He held it there long enough to know the
answer. Then he took it away and wiped the leaf-dirt off on his trousers and went on with what
he had been telling her.

I had my hands in the coping. I found that I had picked up a double handful of ivy and had it
gripped, and my arms had gone up against my chest with it, and I do not know how long I had
been like that. I put it down and finished the row. On the way out I could not have told you
one thing I had done to that plot.

## 5. What he says about her

Estelle asked him. Estelle has no more sense than to ask a question straight out, which in her
is not unkindness, and she asked him one Saturday in the store what he found to talk to that
statue about.

He thought about it the way he thinks, which is properly.

He said she is tired. He said she has been standing up a long while, and it is a good while
since anybody sat with her, and he does not know why they made her with no bench.

Nobody said anything. Estelle got her flour and I got my thread and Ovide behind the counter
wrote it in the book, and Nestor stood there a moment longer in case anybody wanted to talk
about it some more, and then he went out and got up on the wagon.

## 6. Why nobody stops him

I want to be honest here because there is nobody to be honest to.

Somebody ought to have said something to his mother, when she was living, or to Father
Ancelet, or to Nestor himself, which would have taken a minute and which any one of us could
have done any day for twenty years. Nobody has.

I have thought about why, and the answer I keep arriving at is not a good one. It is that the
ground he keeps is kept, and it is kept well, and none of the rest of us has to think about
who is doing it or what it would mean if nobody was. He is fond of her. He is glad to do it.
He would be hurt to be stopped.

That is four reasons and every one of them is true, and I notice that not one of them is the
reason.

## 7. The dream

I dreamed I did what he does.

There was nothing bad in it. I went up the middle of the path in the dream, at my own pace,
not hurrying, and it was a bright morning and the shell was white and I had nothing in my
hands. I went to her and I put my hand on her face the way he does, and it was easy. I was
glad. That is the part I have not got round, and I have had a year to do it in: in the dream I
was glad, and it was my own gladness, and I woke up out of it sitting straight up in the bed
with my heart going and Ulysse asking me what, what, what.

I told him a dog. I have never in my life dreamed about a dog.

## 8. The week he did not go

In the second week of November he did not go out there. He carried for the store and he split
for the Ancelets and he was at Mass on the Sunday and he was perfectly well.

Nobody asked him about it. I did not ask him about it.

He went back on the Monday and he has gone ever since, and I have never found out, and I would
like to put down that I did not ask because it was not my business. That will have to do,
because it is the only reason I have got.

## What this changes

**The avoidance is now proved by its exception**, so no sentence ever has to claim it exists.

**Shape D's open question is answered** — the plot is kept by Nestor — and answering it costs
the enigma nothing, because it is an answer about a man. A solved human question makes the
unsolved one louder.

**And there is a new pressure that is not administrative:** he is forty-two, he is the only
person in the parish who touches it, and he will not go on for ever. Somebody will have to
take that over or the ground will go over, visibly, in front of everybody, on All Saints'.
That is shape D arriving from the outside instead of by her own drift — and it is better,
because she does not choose it.

**Doctrine.** Folded into **L-016** rather than made a new rule: *a consistency demonstrated
at scale is a mechanism.* One person's deniable swerve is dread; a crowd obeying at once is a
law. Basis 3 → 4.
