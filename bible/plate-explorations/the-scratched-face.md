# Plate Exploration — *The Scratched Face*

`wet plate photographs/the-scratched-face.png`. Added by Mark 2026-09-11 with a premise:
*a siren that caused psychological torture, to the point they scratched out the face of the
photograph they took, to try and break its hold.* Riffed the same day.

⚠ **Read D-007 before this file, 2026-09-14.** This exploration is the worked example of the
habit Mark shut down: it reads the image's surface as though someone had made it, prices the
marks, and derives a trade, a wage and a retoucher from the count. L-031 corrected the
arithmetic; D-007 withdraws the activity. The images were generated, their detail is noise
rather than authorship, and **a plate supplies a subject and a feeling in one clause.** Mark's
own premise at the head of this file — a defaced face, because somebody could not stand to be
looked at by it — needed no reading of the plate at all and is the only thing here that was ever
load-bearing. The file is kept because the failure is instructive, not because its §1 stands.

Renamed from its generator filename to **the-scratched-face**, not *the-mermaid*, because the
subject of the object is the defacement. The creature is what the picture is *of*; the
scratching is what the picture *is*. (That last sentence is itself the error: it is a claim
about a photograph nobody took.)

## 1. What is actually in the frame

A figure seated on rock at the edge of open water. Scaled or ridged bodice and a long fish
tail with fins, draped over the stone; long wet hair; both arms raised, fingers bent and
held. Sea and low rock behind. Paper print, folded in quarters, foxed, the edges eaten and
the corners gone.

**The face is obliterated** — not struck through. Long curved strokes radiating out from the
face, running up into the hair, down across the shoulder and off into the sky behind it.
**This is fast work.** See the correction at the end of this section — the first version of this
file read it as slow, and that error drove everything downstream of it.

Five observations, and only the first one is about the creature:

1. **It is a costume.** A bodice with seams and a tail with a hem. This is a photograph of a
   woman on a rock in a made thing, which is the honest reading and also the useful one: it
   is a **commercial product**, made for money, for a sideshow or a stereo-card series or a
   museum agent. Nobody in the story has to believe in anything.
2. **The scratching is a gesture, not a job.** Arcs are a wrist pivot. A hand with something
   sharp in it, swung back and forth over the face, does all of this in a few seconds. Patient
   knife retouching looks the opposite way: short parallel strokes across the grain, worked
   *to a boundary*, because the print still has to sell afterwards.
3. **The scratching does not stop at the face**, and that is the corroboration. It runs up into
   the hair, down onto the shoulder and out into the sky. The boundary of the work is not the
   boundary of the thing being worked on — whoever did it stopped when they stopped, not when
   it was finished. A retoucher paid by the print is the one person who would never overrun the
   face, because overrunning it is what makes the print worthless.
4. **The print was kept.** Defaced, then folded in quarters, then carried or stored long
   enough to fox. Not burned, not thrown in the water. Filed.
5. **The hands were held.** A wet plate wants several seconds of stillness. Those bent
   fingers are a pose somebody maintained on a rock while a man counted.

**Correction, 2026-09-14 — Mark, on the forensics.** *“the face looks like it's scratched off
hastily. A person with a sharp object could move their hand back and forth over this image and
scratch that face off in 5 seconds.”* He is right, and observations 2 and 3 above are the
corrected text. Two things went wrong and only the first is a perception problem.

- **Mark-count is not duration.** The original reading priced each line as a separate decision
  at a needle's pace and arrived at an hour. One gesture makes a dozen lines. There is no motor
  model here to say otherwise, so that arithmetic has to be distrusted on sight: *never infer
  how long an act took from how much of it there is.*
- **The misreading was selected, not random.** It landed two days after the cold-read finding
  that *the labour is the differentiator*, quoted in §2 below. Handed an ambiguous image, the
  reading that flattered live doctrine won, and was then cited back as support for it. Logged
  as **L-031**.

What this costs the file: §3 #1, #2, #4 and #8 all assume a desk, a trade and a commission, and
they survive only as the thing the print was *later* filed inside. The hand belongs to §3 #3,
#5, #7 and #10 — and #10 is Mark's original premise with the one repair it needs, which is that
it did not work. Of the three rendered moments in §5, A and B are built on the slow reading.
They are kept unchanged as the record. The fast reading is rendered in
`the-scratched-face-prose-02.md`, commissioned by Mark the same day: three passages, the act on
the page and fast in all three, futile in three different ways, **unscored.**

**And there is rock, which Louisiana does not have.** No natural stone on that coast — sand,
shell, marsh, mud. Precedent: `the-undercut-tree` carries the same kind of flag for its hills.
There is exactly one way rock gets to the mouth of this river, which is that somebody barged
it there and a gang laid it, and that is §4 below. [check: the South Pass jetty works of the
late 1870s are the obvious referent and are **not** covered in `reference/period/` — verify
before any date goes in prose. D-005 applies: register, not research standard.]

## 2. Where the premise as stated runs into the craft folder

Named plainly, because the part of it that is right is worth more than the part that is
wrong, and they are separable.

1. **It states the mechanism, and worse, it makes the defacement work.** *It holds you
   through its face; scratch the face and the hold breaks* is a rule with a method and a
   counter-method — L-016 straight on, and D-004 says the absence of the explanation **is**
   the horror. It also puts a character in possession of correct knowledge about how the
   haunting operates, which no story in this book allows. The repair is one word long: it
   did not work. See §3 #10.
2. **"Siren" is a named mythology with a documented method.** `book-bible.md`: no shared
   monster with a name and a mythology. A lure that works by singing is about as modal as an
   event gets, and the classical register is the wrong ocean — this collection is Gulf-South
   damp, and the picture is already the least *Standing Water* photograph in the set, the way
   `the-painted-man` is.
3. **"Psychological torture" replaces the event with a diagnosis.** `anachronism-traps.md`
   bans nearly the whole modern vocabulary of the interior, and *it drove them mad* is the
   least specific cost available. This book's costs are a fee in a ledger, a name she has to
   give it, thirty years of not shaking hands. Madness is what a writer reaches for when the
   cost has not been decided.
4. **Ledger collision: `the-specimen`.** That entry is already *a curiosity that cannot be
   sold and cannot be got rid of, and the impossible thing is in the correspondence.* A
   mermaid gaff is the same object in a different costume. One of the two has to move, and
   the third instance is closer than it looks — `prose-01.md` #5 in `stories/04-ship/` is
   also an object nobody can destroy.

**What is right about it, and it is the important half.** Mark's instinct puts the story in
the *act of defacement* rather than in the creature, and that is exactly where the collection
wins. The book's own cold-read finding was that the premise is not the differentiator, the
labour is — *the horror is administered by women with brushes, on a schedule, forever*. The
siren is packaging; the hand is the story — not *somebody's afternoon's work*, which is what the
first version of this file claimed and which the emulsion does not support. Five seconds of
somebody's life, and then thirty years of having done it.

## 3. The riff — ten readings of the scratching

Generated to stay off the ban list, not ranked, and I am not choosing (L-013).

1. **Retouching.** Paid piecework at a retoucher's desk — a real trade and substantially a
   women's trade. A dead operator's stock is bought as a lot and somebody is paid by the
   print to make it saleable. Scratching out a face that will not print right *is the job*.
2. **Killing the negative.** An operator defaces a plate so it cannot be printed again.
   This one was killed and printed anyway, in that order or the other one.
3. **Family excision.** Somebody removed a person from the record, the way families did,
   with a pin, because **they recognised the face.** The story never says whose it was.
4. **The buyer's condition.** The purchaser stipulated, in writing, that the face be
   obliterated before the print left the premises. The impossible thing is in the letter
   that came with the money. [flag: this is `the-specimen`'s instrument]
5. **A child did it**, and nobody knows which, and it lay on a parlour table within reach
   for two years before anybody looked.
6. **It is wear, not scratching** — a print carried in a coat wears where the thumb rests,
   and the thumb rested on the face, so the obliteration is the residue of *looking at it*.
   **The photograph contradicts this**: what is in the emulsion is needle work, not abrasion.
   Kept on the list because it is a good story the plate cannot carry.
7. **The model did it.** The woman in the costume, paid two dollars to sit on a rock, went
   through the stock afterwards. Domestic, legible, and no creature required.
8. **It was scratched to make it sell.** Faceless is worth more — a curiosity with a face is
   a woman in a costume, and a curiosity without one is a specimen.
9. **The sitter's own hand** (flag: gives the thing an aim, which is a mechanism).
10. **Mark's, inverted: they did it to break the hold, and it did not break.** The futility
    is the labour. And the negative is still an asset, so the studio prints another, because
    a print is a dollar.

## 4. The rock, which solves more than it costs

Follow the stone and the picture stops being mythological. Rock on that coast was barged in
and laid by a work gang, which means this photograph was made **at a construction site**,
with men working within earshot. So:

- The picture is a commercial product made on a job site, not an encounter.
- There were forty witnesses, and their testimony is about a woman in a costume.
- The rock itself is the collection's register — imported stone, contract labour, a thing
  the parish will be maintaining forever.

That is where a story set on this plate should probably start, and it is available to the
vessel story in `stories/04-ship/` as the same coast seen from the other side.

## 5. Three rendered moments

Per L-023 — a candidate is a rendered moment, not a plot fact. These contradict each other.
Nobody in any of them names the thing, and none of them says what it wanted.

### A — Eleven a day

Delphine was paid a dollar sixty for a day of faces, which came out at eleven or twelve
prints if the light held, and the light in that back room held from about nine until three.
A face that will not print is worked out with the needle and the ground put back over it, and
done properly it reads as nothing at all — as though the operator had framed it that way.

They had bought a dead man's stock in July, four hundred and six plates in a crate, and she
had been going through it since.

She did this one after dinner and it took her the rest of the afternoon.

She knew what she had done when she put the needle down and could not straighten her hand.
The face was out. So was the hair above it, and the ear, and a length of the shoulder where
the light was, and none of that had wanted touching — she had run off the edge of the work and
gone on, and she could not find the place where she had decided to.

She was paid a dollar sixty for that day, the same as every day, and the entry in the book
says one print retouched.

### B — The second one

Aristide burned four dollars getting the thing done: two for the print, one for the man who
made it, and one for a pin and an afternoon, and the pin was his own.

It did not work. He had not expected it to and he did it anyway, which is a distinction he
would make later to himself a great many times.

What he had not thought about was that a negative is property. The studio on Camp Street had
it in a numbered sleeve with four hundred and five others, and a print off it was a dollar,
and a man who comes in and asks that a numbered negative not be printed is a man who has to
give a reason.

He gave them eleven dollars instead, which was what they wanted for it, and he had nothing to
show for eleven dollars but a glass plate he could not look at and could not put down
anywhere, and a wife who had been told it was for the boat.

### C — The men on the rock

They were laying stone that summer, barged down and tipped and set by hand, and a photographer
came out to the works in July with a tent and a woman and a costume in a trunk.

Numa was on the low course below them and had the whole thing thirty feet away for two hours.
She sat where she was put. She held her arms up while the man counted and she put them down
between plates and rubbed her shoulders, and she asked twice for water, and at noon she ate
with the gang and was civil and dull and from Algiers.

Numa can say all of that under oath, and he has.

What he cannot say, and what he did not put in front of the coroner, was that she came up out
of the water to get onto the rock, and nobody carried her out there, and the tender lay off
the works all morning and never lowered a boat.

## 6. What these refuse

- **Nobody sings.** No lure, no voice, no calling. The word *siren* does not appear in any of
  the three, and no character has a name for it.
- **The defacement is never efficacious.** In A it is a job, in B it fails and is admitted to
  have failed before it is attempted, in C it has not happened yet.
- **The cost is money or work in all three** — a dollar sixty, eleven dollars, sworn
  testimony — because *madness* was the word to get rid of first.
- **C withholds one fact and it is a fact, not an atmosphere** (L-003: obliquity is not
  restraint). The boat on the davits is plain and checkable, and the story never explains it.
