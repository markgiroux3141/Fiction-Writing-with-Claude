# Shape A, Rewritten — Where the Fear Actually Is

`wet plate photographs/the-attitude-of-grief.png`

Mark, 2026-09-09: *"I think we're bumping up against your wanting to satisfy the constraints
of the story, and losing the reason for the story, the fear, the horror, the unsettling spine
tingling. For A, explain what is scary about a story where a person buys the plot of land and
buries someone where the figure is."*

## The honest answer: as written, nothing much

**The ending was a shape, not a scare.** Buying the unnamed plot has the *form* of a good
ending in this book — a permanent expense, no explanation, complicity, the door left open —
and I assembled it out of those requirements rather than out of anything that happens to a
reader. It also fails on its own terms: buying the ground is a **protective, competent act.**
She is making sure nobody else is put there. The story closes on a woman resolving her
problem. Horror endings expose; that one settles.

**And the fault is the one already logged, one scale up.** L-023 says a candidate is a
rendered moment. I obeyed it at the beat level and then, the moment the work zoomed out to
plot, went straight back to specification — arithmetic, a sister-in-law, a clerk. There is no
instant anywhere in shape A where a reader's skin moves.

## Where the fear is in that material

Not the purchase. **The funeral**, and then what the funeral costs her for the rest of her
life.

Everyone in that parish has spent thirty years not going near it. A burial in that corner puts
twenty people, for forty minutes, closer to the figure than any of them has ever voluntarily
stood — and none of them can leave, and none of them can say anything, and all of them are
doing the same arithmetic at once. The horror is a **crowd** obeying a rule nobody has ever
stated, in public, in daylight, at a moment when they are supposed to be thinking about
somebody else.

Then the part that lasts. She has kept that plot for thirty-one years with her back to the
middle of the ground — the lower gate, the north row, the work done facing the wall. Somebody
she loves is now in the corner. **She cannot mourn her brother without standing in front of
it.** Grief has been made into a place she has to go and stand.

## What the ending becomes

Not the purchase. The family ground has two places left, and she is the next to want one, and
everybody knows it — that arithmetic is done in every household in the parish and admitted in
none. So she has known, since the funeral, which piece of ground she is going to be put in.

She arranges to go elsewhere. She will be buried with her husband's people at Bayou Sale,
which is where a wife goes anyway, and which nobody thinks anything of, and which is a
perfectly ordinary thing for a woman to want.

Which means her brother stays out there in the corner on his own, and she has left him there,
and the last movement is what she does about visiting him — which is that she visits from the
near side of the coping, at the distance she can manage, which is about the distance the
horseshoe kept.

> He is four feet the other side of it and I have not been closer to him than the coping since
> the day we put him in. I take the flowers as far as the corner of the Guidry stone and I set
> them down there and he would not mind. He was not a man who wanted a fuss made.

**What that ending does that the purchase did not.** She is not solving anything. She is
abandoning her own family ground and leaving her brother alone in it, and giving a reason that
is true, and the reader watches her do it and cannot say she is wrong. It is the same
instrument as the shell path and her knees, run at the scale of a life.

## The doctrine note, one line

**L-023 extends to story shapes.** A proposed shape comes with its worst scene written, not
with its machinery described — otherwise the shape gets assembled out of constraints, which is
how this one was built, and the assembly always passes the rules and never frightens anybody.
