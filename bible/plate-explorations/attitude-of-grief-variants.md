# The Angel — Variants

Mark, 2026-09-10: *"Is there another way to show that people are uncomfortable looking at this
face? I do like 'they look at his hands', but this phrase is used so often I can imagine people
thinking there's something wrong with his hands."* And: *"the line 'they couldn't get his hand
down' is very good, but I want to toy with other ways we could describe this scene minimally
without revealing too much."*

Two sets. Nothing here replaces anything yet; the hands version and the hand-down version both
stay in play.

---

# I. Nobody looks at his face

Eight ways. The problem with the hands is that a repeated tell gets audited: a reader told
three times that people look at his hands starts wondering about the hands. **The tell should
move**, and half of these do work the hands could not.

## F1. Ask his aunt

Ovide leaned over the counter and asked his aunt whether the boy would want a peppermint.

Ti-Jean was at her elbow and had asked for it himself, out loud, a moment before.

## F2. The hat

Everybody in that parish had said something nice about his hat at one time or another. It was
a good hat. His aunt made them and she made them well, and there was always a new one coming or
an old one going, so there was always something to say about it.

It was a place to put your eyes and it was always there.

## F3. Come on now

Children looked at him. Straight at him, the whole time, the way children look at a man with
one arm or a woman with a goitre — not unkindly, just entirely.

Their mothers took them by the shoulder and turned them and said come on now.

## F4. Which one is he

A drummer up from Franklin wanted the Guidry place and asked her which of the boys about the
store was Guidry's, so he could send word.

Elmire said he would be the small one, and he would have a hat on.

She was most of the way home before it came to her that there was a thing she could have said
that would have told him at once, and that she had gone round it without slowing down, the way
you go round a hole in a road you have known about for years.

> *Does double work: the parish cannot describe the boy's face and cannot describe the figure's
> face, and nothing in the story ever puts those two facts side by side.*

## F5. The offertory

His aunt had got into the way of coming in at the offertory and going out ahead of the last
blessing, which meant the back of the church and no standing about in the yard either side.

Nobody said a word to her about it. Father Ancelet noticed everything in that parish and never
mentioned it once.

## F6. Peltier

Peltier came out to the house to cut his hair rather than have him in the shop, and charged
what he charged in the shop, and put it on the aunt's account like anybody's.

Nobody had arranged that. Nobody could have told you when it started.

## F7. The one who looked

Father Ancelet looked at him.

Straight at him, the whole time he was speaking to him, the way you look at anybody at all.
Elmire watched him do it at a burial and found she was angry about it, and got hold of that
and turned it over, and could not find anybody to be angry at.

## F8. The photograph

There was one photograph in that family with him in it, from Adèle's wedding, and he is at the
edge of the group and has turned his head.

They kept it because of the bride.

# II. The discovery

Nine, arranged from least disclosed to most. **Not ranked** — the order is how much a reader is
given, so the dial can be turned in either direction. Several of them combine; two or three
together is probably the shape of it, and all nine is a catalogue.

## D1. No viewing

The box was closed at the house and it stayed closed at the church.

For a boy of that age, dead out of doors on a clear morning, with no cart and no water and no
machinery anywhere in it, that is a thing a parish notices. Nobody asked. His aunt said, to two
or three people, that he had never liked being looked at.

## D2. Eighty yards

Ovide and Théo Landry went in at the lower gate a little after ten.

It is eighty yards from the lower gate to that corner. They came out at noon.

## D3. The sound

Elmire was at her own coping when Théo Landry made a sound three rows off.

She had known Théo since they were both children in the same schoolroom and she did not know
what it was, or that it was him, until she saw him come out through the gate.

## D4. Four men

Ovide came out for help and was asked what he wanted help with and did not answer.

Four men went back in with him. One of them was seventy-one and nobody suggested he stay
behind, and he went, and he was not much use, and it seemed to be understood that he was going
anyway.

## D5. The coat

His hat was on the plinth. His coat was folded on the plinth underneath it.

There had been frost on the flour barrels at the store at first light and the morning had not
warmed until ten.

## D6. Afterwards, Théo

Théo Landry never went into that cemetery again.

He was buried in it eleven years later and that is the only way he went back into it, and his
wife had to be told by somebody else why he would not go with her on All Saints', because he
would not tell her himself.

## D7. Doudouce in the kitchen

There are two women in that parish who lay out the dead and they had been doing it between them
since before Elmire was married.

That night one of them did the work and the other one sat in the kitchen with the aunt.

The one in the kitchen went home when it was done and did not come to the funeral, and she sent
word that she was not well, and she was not well.

## D8. The heart

The doctor came out from town in the afternoon and said the heart, which is what they say when
there is nothing to be seen, and there was nothing to be seen. No mark on him anywhere. He had
not fallen and he had not been struck and nothing had been at him.

That was all accepted, and there was no inquest, and nobody in the parish ever disputed a word
of it.

What was not discussed was the way he had been sitting.

## D9. His hand

Ovide gave her one thing, standing in his own yard that evening, looking at the fence the whole
time he said it.

They could not get his hand down.

---

## Notes

**On the face set.** F1, F3 and F5 are *behaviour*; F2, F6 and F8 are *arrangements the world
has quietly made*; F4 and F7 are Elmire finding something out about herself and not pursuing
it. A story wants some of each and not many of any — two or three across the whole piece, well
apart, and never the same one twice.

**On the discovery set.** The reveal ladder runs D1–D9 and the top of it is where the danger is:
D8 says he was sitting and D9 says where his hand was, and either alone is a posture the reader
will build. **Both together is a description**, and a description of the body is a fact about
the figure.

**One structural note, offered and not decided.** D6 and D7 both put the horror in *somebody
else's* permanent cost — a man who never went back, a woman who did not come to the funeral —
and neither shows the reader anything at all. They are the only two in the set that would let
the story get to the end without ever describing the scene, which is the version I would most
want to see fail before we abandon it.
