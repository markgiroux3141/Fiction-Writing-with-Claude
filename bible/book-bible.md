# Book Bible — *Standing Water*

**Full title:** *Standing Water: Of Roots, Reflections, and the Faces Beneath*
**Form:** A collection of ghost stories, illustrated with wet-plate photographs.
**Status:** pre-draft. Structure, period, and restraint settled 2026-09-08.
Remaining ⚠ items await Mark's decision.

---

## Premise

The cover presents the book as an object with a history — a black-boarded album with
a plate set into the front. The photographs are not illustrations commissioned for
the stories; the stories are what the photographs are *of*.

⚠ **Frame decision.** Two options:
- **(a) Found album.** An explicit frame: someone has acquired the plates and the
  stories are the provenance they assembled. Adds a spine and a final payoff.
- **(b) Implicit.** No frame; each story simply faces its plate. Cleaner, less
  gimmick risk.

I lean (b) with one exception — a short front-matter note, unsigned, half a page,
that establishes the album without committing to a narrator. Cheap to write, easy
to cut.

## Tone

Restrained, physical, Southern-damp. The horror is patient and vegetal rather than
violent. Things wait, soak, root, and rise. Nothing in this book is in a hurry, and
neither is the prose.

Not: haunted-house romp, gore, cosmic horror, irony, meta.

## The three named motifs

The subtitle promises three things, and the book should deliver them as an actual
structure:

**I. Roots** — What grows up through. Inheritance, family, the thing under the
floor that has been there longer than the house. Plates II, V.

**II. Reflections** — Doubles, water, photographs, the copy that has its own
intentions. Plates III, I. *Photography itself belongs here* — a plate is a
reflection that was made to stay.

**III. The Faces Beneath** — What is under the veil, the sheet, the stone, the
skin. Plates I, IV, V.

⚠ Proposed: three sections of 3–4 stories each, 10–12 total, ~45,000 words. Each
section opens with its plate. Sections group by motif only — the stories inside them
remain unconnected.

## Setting

**Standalone.** No shared waterway, parish, family, or object across stories, and no
continuity between them. Each story builds its own place from scratch and is read
without reference to any other.

What holds the book together instead is register and motif: Gulf South damp,
cypress and saw grass, flat brown water, above-ground burial, houses that are losing
to the vegetation around them. The plates read Louisiana and the prose should agree
with them, but every story invents its own corner of it.

**Period: mostly period, some modern.** Center of gravity c. 1850–1920. One or two
stories in the present day, inheriting a period object or house — see
`craft/craft-principles.md` §10 for what a modern story has to earn.

**Restraint: willing to get physical.** Body horror is an available instrument,
spent precisely and late, in the vegetal register the plates suggest rather than the
visceral one. See `craft/craft-principles.md` §11 for how the budget works across the
collection.

**Filled progressively, not up front (L-015).** Stories are grown from vignettes rather
than outlined, so a row starts as mostly TBD and is completed as the story acquires its
shape. An honest TBD beats a guess that then constrains what the story could have
become. Haunt logic and period land early; ending type and physical band land late.

Fill a row *before* drafting. If a row duplicates another on two or more of
haunt-logic / ending-type / period / physical, change the story rather than the row.
`Physical` records how far the story went: `edge` (nothing shown), `threatened`, or
`explicit`. Target roughly a third in each band across the book.

## Names and language

Plain Gulf-South surnames: Fontenot, Ivey, Dell, Cobb, Marsh, Thibault, Rey.
No Blackwood, no Ravenshollow. See `craft/ai-tells.md` §C.

## What this book is not doing

- No recurring detective or investigator.
- No shared monster with a name and a mythology.
- No crossover, callback, or shared continuity between stories.
- No story that ends by defeating the haunting.
- No epigraphs from invented grimoires.
