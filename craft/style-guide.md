# Style Guide — Prose Level

Sentence-by-sentence standards for *Standing Water*. Architecture lives in
`craft-principles.md`.

---

## Voice

Plain, concrete, unhurried. The narrator of a ghost story should sound like someone
who does not believe in ghosts and is being scrupulously careful with the facts
because they know how it sounds. Understatement is the house style. The prose stays
calm while the content does not.

The model to aim at: a well-read person telling you something that happened, who
keeps stopping to get a small detail right.

## Sentences

- **Vary length aggressively.** A run of three long sentences, then a short one, is
  the basic rhythm of dread. Uniform sentence length is the single most reliable
  marker of machine prose.
- **Put the frightening thing at the end of the sentence.** Syntax is timing.
  "In the water below her, something turned over" — not "Something turned over in
  the water below her."
- **Prefer the active, concrete verb.** Not "there was a sound of movement in the
  hall" but "something moved in the hall."
- **One clause per idea.** Resist stacking subordinate clauses to sound literary.

## Diction

- Anglo-Saxon over Latinate: *rot*, not *decomposition*. *Water*, not *moisture*.
- Name things exactly. Not "a bird" but "a grackle." Not "a tool" but "a hay knife."
  Precision reads as authority, and authority is what the reader lends you when you
  ask them to believe something impossible.
- No thesaurus reaching. If the right word is "wet," the word is "wet."
- Period diction should be light-touch: one or two authentic terms per page, not a
  costume. Get the *objects* right and the language can stay clean.

## Description

- **Three senses minimum per scene, and never the obvious three.** Smell and
  temperature do more work than sight. Sound does the most.
- **Describe by consequence.** Instead of describing the damp, describe the wallpaper
  lifting at the seam and the matches that will not strike.
- **Anchor the supernatural in the physical.** A ghost that is "translucent and
  shimmering" is a special effect. A ghost whose dress is *heavy with water*, that
  leaves a print on the boards that is still there at noon, is a fact.

## Emotion

**Render, do not name.** The reader should arrive at "she was terrified" without the
sentence ever appearing. Show the misfired action: she puts the kettle on the cold
ring. She counts the stairs twice. She says something pleasant to the dog.

Banned as a class: "she felt a chill of unease," "a wave of dread washed over him,"
"his blood ran cold," "an inexplicable sense of foreboding."

## Dialogue

- Sparse. People in these stories say less than they mean.
- No exposition through dialogue. Nobody explains the town's history to someone who
  already lives there.
- Regional voice through **syntax and word choice**, never phonetic spelling. No
  dropped-g apostrophes, no "ayuh."
- A conversation about the haunting should be a conversation about something else.

## Paragraphs

Vary length as deliberately as sentences. A one-line paragraph is a hammer — use it
perhaps twice in a story, and never for a cheap sting.

## Tense and time

Past tense by default. Present tense only when a story has a specific reason for
immediacy, and never for a period piece. Be scrupulous with chronology — the reader
should always know what time of day it is and how long has passed.

## Punctuation

- Em-dashes: sparingly, and never more than one pair per page. They are the reflex
  of prose that has not decided what its clauses are.
- Semicolons are welcome in the period voice, rare in the modern one.
- Italics for emphasis are a tell. Let the syntax do it.
- No ellipses for atmosphere. Trailing off is not the same as restraint.

## Titles

Concrete, slightly off, drawn from the story's own vocabulary. Noun phrases.
*Standing Water*. *The Second Sitting*. *What the Ditch Kept*. Avoid the abstract
gothic ("Shadows of Sorrow") and avoid the cute.

## Length discipline

Every scene must either advance the escalation or tighten the constriction. A scene
that only establishes atmosphere is a scene that should be two sentences inside
another scene.
