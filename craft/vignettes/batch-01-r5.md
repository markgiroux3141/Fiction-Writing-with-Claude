## 2 — The extra setting  *(B1: dread as arithmetic and decision)*

There were seven at Mrs. Ivey's that spring, and she had laid for seven since the
Pardue boy went home in February, and her hands did it without her. So she was most of
the way back to the kitchen with the empty tray before she understood what she had
walked past.

Eight places. The eighth one down at the foot end — fork, knife, spoon, the plate
squared to the table edge, the napkin folded the way she folded them and nobody else in
the parish did.

The men would be down in four minutes. She stood there for two of them.

To take it up meant she had set it. To leave it meant she had not. She left it, and
poured the water, and when they came in Mr. Dell went straight down to the foot end and
sat in it and shook out the napkin, and she watched him eat off that plate for an hour.

She laid eight the next night, and every night after, and washed the extra plate
herself so the girl would not ask.

## 3 — The handover  *(D1: touch; the impossible as courtesy)*

He lost the knife off the gunwale in three feet of water. The bottom there was silt and
it would be gone in a minute, so he went over the side.

The water came to his chest and was the temperature of a bath somebody had got out of
an hour before. He put his arm down and swept it along the bottom in the flat way you
do, wrist loose, palm up.

He found the handle. He had it.

And then it was pressed into his hand, closed, and the fingers folded over his own to
be sure of it, the way you would give a man his change.

He got back into the skiff. He did not look at the water. He sat with his knees drawn
up and the knife in his fist, and the boat went around slowly in the middle of the
pond, and he was still sitting like that when the light started to go.

He lost a pair of pliers off the same gunwale in September and a whetstone the year
after, and he let them both go.

## 4 — The game  *(I1: tonal inversion; daylight)*

It was the fourth day of the heat and the children had got into the linen press, which
she would deal with later. From the gallery she could see them going around the fig
tree — two under sheets, three not, and the ones under sheets went slow with their arms
out, because that was the rule.

She shelled peas and watched a third sheet come around the far side of the tree.

It was taller. It went the same slow way, and the children screamed at it properly, and
it followed the Ledoux girl twice around and let her get away, which was correct, and
she thought Hollis had come up from the field to play, though Hollis was not a man who
played.

She called them in at noon. Four came, and then the fifth, pulling the sheet off her
head as she came up the steps. She counted them at the table and got five, and counted
again and got five, and the sixth stayed out by the fig tree with its arms down at its
sides in the full sun.

She kept the press locked after that and gave the sheets out herself.

## 5 — Trimming  *(G1: explicit vegetal physical, flat delivery)*

Sunday he did it at the window, where the light was better, with the scissors and the
enamel bowl and a square of newspaper on his knees.

It came in faster in the wet months. He took the outside first, the long threads that
had got down past the second button and gone pale at the ends the way a thing does when
it has run out of something. Those cut easy. Closer in it was tougher and had a grain to
it, so you went across rather than straight on, and it was wet at the root end and came
off on his fingers and smelled of the underside of a boat.

He worked up under the jaw by feel. Some of it went through the skin and some of it only
lay against the skin, and after eleven years he could tell the difference in the dark,
and he did not pull on the ones that went through, because of what pulling on them did.

He folded the newspaper over and put it in the stove. He had the window up before he did
it, on account of the smell.

## 6 — The worn chair  *(F1: patience evidenced by wear)*

The house had been shut since the old woman went into her daughter's in 1889, and Rey
had the key and the list. Cypress press, poor. Two bedsteads. Kitchen safe, tin panels,
sound. He had written all of it a hundred times before.

He had six chairs, cane, four of them sound, and then he went into the north bedroom and
there was a seventh, facing the window.

The cane of it was worn through in the seat to a hole you could put a fist in, the arms
were worn down to the pale wood, and the whole of it had the polish that comes off a
person and cannot be put on with anything. He put it down as bad, went on into the hall,
and came back.

The boards in front of the chair were worn too. Not scuffed the way boards go by a door,
where the traffic spreads. Two patches side by side, about a foot apart, each
the size and shape of a shoe sole, going down into the wood a good sixteenth, and
they had not moved in any direction at all.

On the sheet that went to the auctioneer he put the house at six chairs.

# What changed, and why

Vignette 1 was the only one Mark scored. These five are the same doctrine applied
without evidence, so treat the confidence as borrowed.

## The change that hit all five: L-006 endings

Every one of them ended on **duration without a price** — a flaw I flagged as a
possible tic in the original handover without knowing what it was. Duration alone is a
mood. Duration you *pay* for is a measurement. Each now ends on an accommodation:

| | old ending | new ending — the price |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | watched him eat off it for an hour | lays eight every night, and **washes the extra plate herself so the girl will not ask** |
| 3 | still sitting when the light went | **lets a pair of pliers and a whetstone go** rather than reach in again |
| 4 | could see it all through dinner | **keeps the linen press locked** and gives the sheets out herself |
| 5 | put it in the stove | **has the window up before he does it**, on account of the smell |
| 6 | they had not moved in any direction | **puts the house at six chairs** on the sheet that goes to the auctioneer |

None names what is being avoided. #6 is the one I would defend hardest: the
accommodation is a falsified line in a ledger, which costs him nothing, takes two
seconds, and leaves the chair in the house permanently.

## Register audit (L-007)

Fear-renderings tagged; no vignette now repeats a register, and none uses the tactile
one for fear.

- **2** — cognitive lag (back to the kitchen before she understood), temporal (four
  minutes, stood for two), motor (left it), attentional (watched him eat).
- **3** — tactile **as content, not as fear** (the handover itself); motor for the
  aftermath (knees drawn up, did not look).
- **4** — cognitive (counted, and counted again), attentional (the third sheet).
- **5** — no fear-rendering at all. He is not afraid, which is the lever.
- **6** — motor (went into the hall, came back), and wear standing in for a body that
  is not there.

## L-004 — asides cut

- **3** — *"which was nothing, except that"* gone. Plain statement of the silt.
- **6** — *"nothing on the list he had not written a hundred times"* was a subordinate
  aside; now a flat sentence of its own.
- **5** — *"because the light was better there than at the washstand"* trimmed to
  *"where the light was better."* It characterised rather than defended, so it survived
  in substance, but the justifying syntax went.
- **4** — *"which she would deal with later"*, *"because that was the rule"*, *"which
  was correct"* all **kept**. These are her reading the game's rules, and the horror is
  that the thing follows them. Not asides; mechanism.

## L-003 and L-005 — small repairs

- **5** — *"there was a wetness at the root end"* → *"it was wet at the root end."*
  Same failure as "It was wet there," caught by the doctrine this time.
- **3** — *"swept it along the bottom"* had "it" as his arm, then two more "it"s for the
  knife. Now *"put his arm down and swept it"* with a clean antecedent.
- **5** — *"some of it was only against it"* had two referents for "it" in one clause;
  now *"only lay against the skin."* Repeating "skin" is the cheaper cost.
- **4** — *"counted them and got five, and counted again and got five"* is deliberate
  restatement that **intensifies** rather than reverses, which L-005 permits and which
  is also the arithmetic lever doing its work.

## Structural fix, #2

Her hands now lay the table *without her* — so when the eighth place appears, the
reader has already been told she does this unconsciously. That is what earns the
dilemma four lines later (*"To take it up meant she had set it"*). In the original the
dilemma was asserted; now it is loaded.

## Structural fix, #6

The old draft listed "six chairs, cane, four sound" in the opening inventory and then
introduced "the seventh," which made the count contradict itself. The chairs now come
as discovery, which also sets up the last line.

## One rule refined rather than obeyed

#3 keeps *"the way you would give a man his change"* — a simile at the exact moment of
fear, which L-007 appeared to ban. The ban is on similes that **decorate a sensation**;
this one **classifies an action** and makes the impossible act ordinary and social,
which no direct description could. L-007 now carries the boundary: *is the comparison
telling the reader how something felt, or what kind of thing it was?*
