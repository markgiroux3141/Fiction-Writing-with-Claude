## Three objections

### 1. Literalism — the general failure

The idiom was shorthand for the *state*. I built the mechanism instead. This is the
larger pattern and it will recur on story-level notes: a note that names a detail is
almost always naming the effect it wants, and the named detail may not need to appear
at all. → **L-002**.

There is also a craft error underneath the misreading, and it survives even if the
brief had been literal: **fear narrows perception, it does not sharpen it.** A woman
who is afraid does not feel individual follicles release in sequence. She feels
something large and stupid and she wants to be outside. Draft r2 gave her an
anatomist's attention at the exact moment she should have had almost none — which is
why the paragraph reads as observed rather than felt, the *same* charge as critique
01, arriving by the opposite route. → **L-001 amended**.

### 2. "It was wet there"

Correct, and worth naming precisely: the reader has to work out that it means sweat.
That decoding step is fatal — it moves the reader into puzzle-solving at the moment
they were meant to be inside her. I wrote around the plain word because "damp with
sweat" felt like a stock phrase, and reached obliquity instead. Restraint is saying
the plain thing about a strange event, not a strange thing about a plain one.
→ **L-003**.

### 3. "a fault in the building and not in her"

Mark could not place the strangeness. The diagnosis:

**The sentence defends her against an accusation nobody has made.** Nothing has
suggested the hall is her fault — nothing has happened yet at all. So the reader
stops and searches for the argument the sentence is answering, finds none, and is
left holding a rebuttal to a missing charge. That is the "odd" feeling: a hunt for
absent context.

Two aggravating effects:
- It is **the narrator being witty at the threshold of the fear**, which puts a
  commentator between the reader and the woman.
- By pre-excusing her, it **announces that she is about to be frightened and that it
  is reasonable** — thirty words before anything happens. It spoils and it defuses.

→ **L-004**.
