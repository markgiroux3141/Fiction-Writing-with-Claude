# Critique 05 — Vignette 1 r4, "her back had never been so much of her before"

## Mark, 2026-09-08

> "Also, 'her back had never been so much of her before, she could feel the whole width
> of it' was very good. It captures the feeling of someone being behind you that
> doesn't feel cliche"

---

## Why it is not cliché — the register switch

Every stock rendering of *someone is behind you* is **tactile**: hair rising, skin
crawling, prickling, a cold spot, gooseflesh. That register is exhausted, and no
amount of freshening the adjective repairs it — r2 proved this by rendering the
tactile version at maximum resolution and still reading as dead.

This line abandons the register entirely. Nothing touches her and nothing tingles. What
changes is **the proportion of her own body in her attention**: a part of her becomes
larger, as a fraction of her, than it has ever been. That is proprioceptive and
attentional, not tactile, and it is unclaimed ground.

**General form: when a sensation is clichéd, change the sensory register, not the
adjective.**

## The refinement of L-003

Set the two lines side by side:

| | perception | phrasing | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| "It was wet there" | ordinary (sweat) | oblique | fails |
| "her back had never been so much of her before" | strange (proportion) | plain | works |

**Put the strangeness in the perception, never in the phrasing.** Every word in the
good line is common and the syntax is flat; the *idea* is what is unusual. Nothing has
to be decoded, so there is no bookkeeping — the reader gets an odd thought in plain
English and recognises it immediately as true of their own body.

## Two smaller things

**"Width" is the right axis.** Not size, not shape. Width is the lateral dimension —
the surface that can be *seen*. It quietly says the threat is a gaze and not a hand,
without either word appearing.

**No simile.** "It was as if her back had become enormous" would be dead. A simile at
the moment of fear inserts an author doing comparisons. Flat declaration of an
impossible-sounding fact is stronger, and it is unfalsifiable — the reader cannot
dispute her relationship to her own back, only check it against their own, where it
turns out to be true. Recognition, not persuasion.

---

## Not a fluke: the whole paragraph does this

Counting the fear-renderings in each draft by register:

**r2 (failed)** — hair unsticking from wet skin (tactile), each hair letting go
(tactile), gooseflesh down the arms (tactile), forearm over the nape (motor).
**Four renderings, three of them tactile.**

**r4 (works)** — the hall was nine feet and took a long time (spatial/temporal), her
back as a proportion of her (proprioceptive), the basket on the wrong hip (cognitive),
the latch worked twice (motor), her legs went (motor).
**Five renderings, none tactile.**

The praised line is not an isolated good sentence. It is one instance of a paragraph
that has systematically left the exhausted register — which is the argument that this
is repeatable.
