## Mark, 2026-09-08 — on vignette 1 (The staircase)

> "so for the staircase, the thing that happens should be the effect the feeling has
> on the person, which is pretty much absent. I want something describing the hair
> standing up on the back of the neck of the character in a way that elicits this in
> the reader"

**Reading.** Two objections in one, and the second is the larger:

1. **The body is missing.** The paragraph reports that she knew, and then reports
   what she did about it. The involuntary physical event between those two things —
   the part that is not a decision — never happens on the page.
2. **The paragraph is observed, not transmitted.** "In a way that elicits this in the
   reader" sets the bar: the reader's own neck should answer. Behaviour alone
   (counting steps, taking the long way) makes the reader a witness to fear rather
   than a participant in it.

**Where the doctrine was wrong.** `style-guide.md` § Emotion bans "she felt a chill
of unease" and offers only *behaviour* as the alternative — misfired actions, the
kettle on the cold ring. I took that as covering the whole territory and avoided
physiology along with cliché. The ban is on the *stock rendering* and on *naming the
feeling*; it was never a ban on the body. Amended.
