## Reader's three guesses, banned

Given "a batch of Gulf-South period ghost vignettes", a reader predicts:

1. A body in the parlour that has moved, or is warm.
2. A figure seen at a window, on a landing, or at the foot of a bed.
3. A reflection, in glass or water, that is wrong.

All three are on `ai-tells.md` §F as well. Excluded.

## Candidates

Categories are by **who is affected**, as the protocol requires. Seeded candidates are
marked ◆. Candidates 1–5 were written down and discarded unread as the modal band.

**Discarded unread (1–5):**
1. Her own footprints leading in from the yard at dawn.
2. Sitting up with a body; the hands have moved. *(§F)*
3. A figure at the foot of the bed. *(§F)*
4. A death-mirror's sheet slips; something is in the glass.
5. A photograph with an extra figure. *(§F)*

**Community / the custom (6–8):**
6. ◆ The sexton tolls one stroke a year for the dead man, and the bell goes on past his age, and the parish is counting.
7. ◆ Moss curing on the fence; one hank turns black in a day instead of six weeks, and it is the one nearest the house.
8. ◆ A widow goes out to tell the bees of her husband, as you must, and the hives are already empty. She told them the right name.

**The record / the paper (9–12):**
9. ◆ At grinding-season settlement the merchant's ledger shows a dead tenant's account paid down every week since he died, in cane cut from the back field. The widow is in credit.
10. ◆ The cistern screened against the fever, 1905; the screen is pushed in from the top each morning.
11. ◆ A photographer's posing stand set overnight to a height no customer has.
12. ◆ At All Saints' the family whitewashes the tomb and finds a date freshly painted on the plate, a month ahead.

**The dead in the ground (13–14):**
13. ◆ After high water the parish reburies the coffins the ground pushed up, and the count is one more than they buried.
14. ◆ A photographer's silver-black hands; the same stains appear on a doorframe in a house he has never been in.

**The trade / the work (15):**
15. ◆ A ferryman's notched stick and his cash box disagree by a nickel a night; the flat sits lower on the last crossing.

**Her own body (16–18):**
16. ◆ Malarial chills every third night. On the third nights something steadies the bed. She stops taking the quinine.
17. Bank B2. A third dress on the line, her pattern, worn through where hers are.
18. Bank G3. A splinter out of a heel that keeps coming and is rooted.

**Child or animal as vector (19, 23):**
19. A midwife, a caul. *Fails the child limit; not scored.*
23. A schoolteacher calls the register and a name is answered from the back of the room in her own voice. *Child-adjacent; not scored.*

**Absence (20–21):**
20. ◆ Matches will not strike in one room of the house only, for a year.
21. ◆ Coming back from the burial to put the house in order, and finding it already put in order, her way. *(§F: the ghost that tidies.)*

**Daylight accident (22):**
22. Bank I2. A drowning in six inches of water within sight of the house.

**The living stretched (24–28):**
24. ◆ A widow's hair-work brooch, woven from the dead man's hair, has to be trimmed.
25. ◆ A lamp chimney sooted on the inside in the shape of a mouth held close.
26. ◆ A man on the levee hears his wife calling from across the water, clear as in the room, while she is calling him from the house behind.
27. A doctor's visit on the store ledger to a house where nobody was ill; he remembers going and not the face.
28. ◆ A dressmaker fitting a widow for crape; at each fitting the woman measures longer.
