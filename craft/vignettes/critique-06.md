## 1. "What's happening exactly?" — I did not know either

The honest answer for both #2 and #3 is that **I had not decided.** I wrote the shape
of a haunting and left the middle empty, and Mark felt the hollow.

This is a misreading of `craft-principles.md` §3 ("the haunt has rules — and you never
state them") and of James's *"we do not want to see the bones of their theory."* Both
presuppose that **there is a theory**. Withholding a rule from the reader and never
having formed one produce completely different prose: the first is dense with
consequences that imply a cause, the second is a set of gestures that imply nothing,
and the reader's response to the second is precisely Mark's — *what is actually
happening?* That question is the diagnostic. → **L-008**.

The second half of his note is the same failure from the other side: *"are there any
consequences of him going into the water?"* He is owed something in exchange for being
denied the rule. Nothing happened to that man. He got handed a knife and went home.

## 2. "The description of the water doesn't include any feelings that something is
amiss"

Correct, and `reference/influences/james-rules.md` §3 already says why: *be most
concrete in the approach and least concrete in the touch.* I inverted it — a bland
approach and a heavily rendered touch. The wrongness has to be in the water **before**
he reaches the bottom. → **L-010**.

## 3. The "and" chains in #4

Mark is right, and he is right about a real thing, though not quite the thing he
named. Taking the question seriously since he asked for a justification:

**They are not fragments.** They are grammatically complete clauses joined by *and* —
polysyndeton — and none of the sentences in #4 begins with "And." (One does in #3.)

**The technique is period-correct.** Chained *and* is genuine to nineteenth-century
vernacular narration, to the KJV-inflected Southern register, and to the whole line
from Twain through Hemingway to McCarthy. Nothing about it is anachronistic and I
would not want it gone.

**But the concentration is wrong, and that is what he is feeling.** In r5 *every*
long sentence in #4 is built the same way. When polysyndeton is the only method of
extension, the passage has exactly two speeds — very short, or a chain — and the
reader gets no signal about which clause in the chain matters, because they all arrive
at the same weight and the same pace. It flattens the count of children, which is the
entire lever. Used once or twice against varied construction it accumulates and
accelerates; used throughout it is just a texture. → **L-011**.

So: keep the instrument, cut the frequency, and put it where accumulation is the
point.

## 4. "Is he cleaning a fish?" — the worst failure in the batch

Total comprehension loss. He spent the paragraph solving instead of reading and never
reached the horror at all.

Cause: I never named the ordinary activity. Not *beard*, not *face*, not *shaving* —
nothing. I withheld the frame because naming it felt like spoiling something, but
**there is nothing to spoil**: the horror was never a reveal, it was supposed to be the
routine. Meanwhile every prop I did supply — scissors, enamel bowl, newspaper on the
knees, good light — fits cleaning a fish exactly as well as it fits a man trimming his
beard. Ambiguous props are not clues. They are noise.

This is L-003 (decoding tax) at the scale of a whole scene, and it inverts
`craft-principles.md` §1: the mundane buys the impossible, and I never paid.
→ **L-009**.
