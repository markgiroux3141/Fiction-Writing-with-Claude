# The Wake — candidate events

Mark, on batch 02 #3: *"I really like the setup, but it was a little bit of a let down
when basically a ghost combed his hair... I think a part of this process will be coming
up with potential things that could happen in the story and whether that thing is scary
or unsettling."*

The setup is fixed: the two-till-four turn, she is told not to sleep, she sleeps, she
wakes at four. **What was the interval for?**

---

## Why the comb failed

Worth naming before generating alternatives, because the diagnosis is the selection
criteria.

1. **It is a considerate act.** Grooming a corpse is tenderness. James: *the ghost must
   want something, and what it wants must be bad for the living.* A ghost that tidies
   is scenery.
2. **Nothing happened to her.** She slept in a room with it and woke unharmed and
   uninvolved. The event is entirely about the dead man. `craft-principles.md` §6 runs
   notice → intrude → **implicate**, and this barely reaches intrude.
3. **It is complete.** You can picture the whole event and nothing is left over. It
   implies no second act and no worse version of itself.
4. **It does not change her future.** The horror is over when the paragraph is.

**So the test for a candidate event:** does it touch the living, does it imply
something larger than itself, and does it leave the character in a worse position than
she was in before she slept?

## The options

### A. It used her

**A1 — Her hair.** Her own hair has been combed, and done wet, and there is no water in
the room. *The original act, turned on the living. Same image, opposite direction.*

**A2 — Laid out.** She wakes in the chair with her hands folded one over the other on
her chest, the way you arrange a body. She did not sit down like that. *Someone
practised on her.*

**A3 — Moved.** She wakes in the chair on the far side of the coffin, facing the door.
She sat down facing the window. *She was carried, chair and all, and did not wake.*

**A4 — The shawl.** Her sister's shawl is over him, tucked at the shoulders the way you
do it for someone who is cold. She is cold. *Her possession, borrowed for him.*

**A5 — The coins.** The coins are off his eyes and in her lap, and his eyes are properly
shut without them. *It declined the fare. It is not going.*

### B. He went somewhere

**B1 — Shoes.** He is wearing his shoes. He was laid out without them, and they are
dirty, and the dirt is wet. *The vigil is not containing him, and this is night two of
three.*

**B2 — Wet hem.** The bottom four inches of the burial shirt are soaked and there is
nothing in the room to soak them with. *Same as B1, quieter, harder to argue with.*

**B3 — Hands.** His hands are no longer folded. They are at his sides, palms up, the
way a man's hands sit when he has been carrying something and put it down.

### C. She went somewhere

**C1 — The gallery chair.** The front door stands open and a chair has been set out on
the gallery facing the road. The seat is still warm. *Something sat up outside. It may
have been her.*

**C2 — The comb, in her hand.** She did comb his hair. Her comb is in her fist, and
there is hair in the teeth, and it is not his colour or hers.

**C3 — Her feet.** Her shoes are by the chair where she put them and her feet are
filthy to the ankle. *The most economical version — one image, no explanation
available.*

### D. It is still in the room

**D1 — The second chair.** There are two chairs drawn up to the coffin. She brought one
in. The other is warm.

**D2 — The list.** The turns are written up on the mantel in her mother's hand. There
is a name on the two-till-four that is not hers, and it is not anybody's.

**D3 — Taking a turn.** Somebody is sitting in the other chair, and it is not moving,
and the relief is not due for a quarter of an hour. She does not look at it. She sits
until four.

### E. The arithmetic

**E1 — The lamp.** The lamp is dry. A lamp that size burns nine hours and she filled it
at two. *The interval was longer than the clock says.*

**E2 — Three nights, all hers.** The turns for the third night are already written out,
and her name is on every one of them.

### F. Absence

**F1 — The smell.** Nothing in the room has changed. The room smells of cut grass and
river water. *Cheapest option, and possibly too quiet.*

**F2 — Gone.** Emile is not in the room. At a quarter past four the household comes
down, and the vigil continues, and nobody says anything about it.

---

## My three

**B1 (the shoes)** — the dirt is wet, which means recently, which means he will do it
again tomorrow night, and there is a third night to come. It is small, physical, gives
the reader something to picture, and the picture is not the frightening part; the
schedule is.

**A2 (laid out)** — the most direct threat to the living. Someone arranged her in the
posture of a corpse while she was unconscious, and the obvious reading is practice.

**C3 (her filthy feet)** — one image, no available explanation, and it makes her the
agent without her consent. Cheapest of the three and possibly the most frightening,
because she cannot tell anyone.

Several of these combine (B1 + C3 is the same night from both ends), but that is two
impossible things and the vignette only gets one.
