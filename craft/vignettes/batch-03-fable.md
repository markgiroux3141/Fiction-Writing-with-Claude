## 1 — Telling the bees

> **Rule (off-page):** Bees stay with a house only while they know everyone living in
> it. She told them the right name. They left because there is someone in the house she
> has not told them about, and she cannot, because she does not know it is there.

The morning after Anselm died she went out to tell the bees, because it had to be done
before he was buried and there was nobody else to do it.

There were three hives on the bench under the pear tree. His mother had done this the
year his father went, and she had watched: you knocked on the lid, you said who had
died, you said who would keep them now, and you tied a strip of black on the hive so
they would be in mourning too.

She knocked on the first one and it made no sound.

A hive in June is a thing you hear from the back step. She stood with her knuckles on
the lid and her hand would not come off it. When it did she put her ear to the second
hive, and to the third, and then she lifted the lid of the third and looked in.

Comb. Capped honey, a good weight of it. Not one bee, not even dead on the board. They
had gone in the night and taken nothing with them.

She tied the black on all three anyway and said his name to each, and said her own, and
waited, in case there was another name she ought to say.

After that she knocked before she went into a room. Every room, her own included, and
she said who it was.

## 2 — Settlement

> **Rule (off-page):** He is still working the place. The debt was his and he means to
> clear it. The merchant's book is honest, and credits whatever is cut, whoever cuts it.

Grinding was done by the second week of December, and Odile Pitre went in to the store
to settle her husband's account, which she had put off since July, when they buried him
with the crop half made.

Broussard had the book open on the counter when she came in. He ran his finger down the
page and turned it, and ran his finger down again, and looked up at her.

"You're square," he said. "Better than square. You've eleven dollars standing to you."

She said that could not be right.

"Guidry's sent your tonnage over every Saturday since August. Fourteen ton a week,
steady, out of the back field." He turned the book so she could read it. "Your man's the
only one on the place cuts the same every week. Everybody else falls off in the wet."

Her hands were a long way off on the counter. She watched one of them turn the page back
to July, where the entries stopped, and then, after four blank weeks, started again in
the same clerk's hand.

She took flour and a length of the brown cloth and signed for them.

At Christmas Broussard asked, not unkindly, whether she wanted the balance in cash. She
said to leave it stand. She said he would want it left.

## 3 — The last crossing

> **Rule (off-page):** Something crosses on the last boat every dusk and pays its fare.
> It crossed there before there was a ferry. A ferry that stops carrying it does not stop
> being crossed.

Naquin kept the ferry at Bayou Chien and kept his count on a stick, a notch a crossing,
and at night he set the notches against the coins in the cigar box, and for nine years
they had agreed.

In October they stopped agreeing. A nickel over, every night. He counted the stick twice
and the box three times and had his wife count, and she got what he got.

He found out where it came from by paying attention, which he did not care to do. On the
last crossing of the day, with the light nearly gone, the flat sat lower in the water
than one man and a mule should sit it, not by much, the width of a hand. And the cable
came through his palms heavier than it ought from the first pull, before the flat had
properly left the bank.

He did not turn round. His arms knew the weight before he did and kept on pulling, hand
over hand, the whole way across, because stopping in the middle of that water with it
behind him was not a thing he could make them do. On the far bank he stood facing the
bayou until he heard the nickel go in the box.

After that he cut the last notch of the day before he went down to the landing, so the
stick and the box would agree.

## 4 — Third nights

> **Rule (off-page):** It comes when the chill does and stays as long as the shaking
> lasts. It wants to be waited for, and it has taught her to want it back.

Her chills came every third night, which is how the ague goes, and Dr. Odom left a
bottle of quinine and said to take it in whiskey and not to miss.

The third nights went the same way. About ten the cold came up from her feet, and by
half past she was shaking so hard the headboard ticked against the wall and her teeth
would not let her shut her mouth. It went on an hour. There was nothing to do but be in
it.

The fourth time, the bed went still under her while she went on shaking in it.

Not stopped. Held. Her eyes went to the foot of the bed and she made them come back.
Something had a hold of the frame down there and was keeping it steady, the way you hold
a ladder for somebody. She lay in the middle of her own shaking with the bed quiet on
every side of her, and it stayed until the chill passed, and then the frame eased, the
small give of a hand coming off it.

The bottle stood on the washstand. Every morning after that she poured a spoon of it
into the slop jar, so the bottle would go down right, and when Odom came again he wrote
her down as improving.

## 5 — Monday wash

> **Rule (off-page):** Another woman keeps this house in the hours she is out of it, in
> the same clothes, doing the same work, and the work is wearing through both of them in
> the same places.

She took the wash in off the line Monday evening, before the dew, and counted it into
the basket the way she always did: his four shirts, the two sheets, the towelling, her
two brown checks.

There were three brown checks.

The third hung between the sheets, and it was dry, bone dry, when everything either side
of it was still damp along the seams. She stood and looked at it a while with the basket
against her. Then she took it down.

It was her pattern, the cheap check from the store that half the parish wore. Worn
through at both elbows. Gone thin across the front where you lean on the washboard. Let
out at the waist, twice, by hand, with a thread not quite the colour of the cloth,
exactly as she had let out her own.

Her arms held it up against her before she had decided anything, the way you would in a
shop.

It came to the right place at the wrist and the right place at the ankle.

She put it in the press with the other two. She wore it Thursday, and it sat easier on
her than either of them.

## 6 — The fitting

> **Rule (off-page):** She went into the bayou after him and the water kept the part of
> her that mattered. What came out is being drawn back down toward it, slowly, from the
> feet up, and the dressmaker is being paid to keep pace.

Mrs. Savoie came to be fitted for her mourning on the Tuesday, nine days after the
burial, and Delphine measured her and pinned the crape at the length the tape said.

The second week the hem was short. Delphine let it down an inch and said nothing; a
woman in first mourning does not eat, and the body changes.

The third week she knelt to pin it and found the hem short again, by more, and the dress
hung wrong from the shoulders, everything on her a little further down than the seams had
been made for. Delphine had pins in her mouth and did not know how many. Her knees would
not get her up for a while, so she went on pinning.

Mrs. Savoie stood the way you stand for a fitting, looking at the wall.

She was in her stockings, and the stockings were wet, and Delphine had watched her walk
in over dry boards. Above the stocking tops the skin was white and had the give of a hand
that has been in the washtub all morning.

Delphine let the hem down as far as it would go and put four inches of false hem under
that, which she had never done for anybody, and charged her for the cloth.

## Where I think it might fail

- **Length.** All six run 200 to 240 words, over the bank's 100 to 200. Batch 02 ran
  the same. If the ceiling matters, 2 and 3 have the most to lose.
- **L-004 risk in 3.** *"which he did not care to do"* is a dry aside just before the
  approach. It is meant as the ferryman's character (`ai-tells.md` §D asks for a
  preoccupation) rather than as wit at the threshold, but it sits where L-004 says wit
  should not.
- **L-007 boundary in 6.** *"the give of a hand that has been in the washtub all
  morning"* is a simile. I read it as the permitted kind, classifying what the skin is
  rather than how it felt, but it is on the line.
- **Explaining-away in 6.** *"a woman in first mourning does not eat, and the body
  changes"* is Delphine's rationalisation at the notice stage, which
  `craft-principles.md` §6 asks for. If it reads as the narrator defending her, it is
  L-004 and should go.
- **4 and the sitter on the bed.** Nothing is seen and no figure is described, but a
  presence at the foot of a bed is next door to a §F entry. The argument for it is
  that the event passes all four tests and the fear is that she wants it. If that
  argument does not hold, 24 (the hair brooch) or 26 (the voice over water) from the
  invention file are the replacements.
