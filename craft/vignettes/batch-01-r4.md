# Vignette Batch 01 — Revision 04

Vignette 1, against critique 03 (L-005). One sentence changed.

---

## 1 — The staircase  *(A1: presence with nothing shown; no impossible thing)*

She was halfway across the hall with the mending basket when she knew there was
somebody at the top of the stairs, looking down at her.

She did not look. She kept going for the door at the same pace, because to go faster
was to admit it, and the hall was nine feet and took a long time.

Her back had never been so much of her before. She could feel the whole width of it.

The basket was on the wrong hip. It wasn't, but this was the thought she had all the
way to the door, where she worked the latch twice before it lifted.

Outside it was ten in the morning and white hot. She set the basket on the step for
the Boudreaux girl, and then her legs went, and she sat down beside it in full view of
the road with her head down until it passed.

She went back in by the kitchen door. She used the kitchen door for the rest of the
summer, which added eleven steps each way.

---

## What changed

Mark's line, adopted whole: **"It wasn't, but this was the thought she had."** Both
his choices are right — the pro-form, and *this* over *that*.

Two further edits in the same sentence, from the same principle:

- **"all the way to the door" replaces "and she carried it to the door."** It gives
  the stupid thought a duration instead of a hand-off, and it kills an ambiguity: with
  "the thought" in the preceding clause, *carried it* flickers between the thought and
  the basket before settling.
- **"where she worked the latch twice before it lifted."** The old version ran three
  different referents for "it" through one sentence — basket, thought, latch. Now two,
  far apart.
