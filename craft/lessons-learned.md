# Lessons Learned

Distilled from Mark's critiques. This is the highest-authority craft document in the
repo: where it conflicts with `style-guide.md` or `craft-principles.md`, **this file
wins**, and the conflicting doc should be amended.

## The status of these rules

Mark, 2026-09-08: *"we shouldn't have hard rules, if you can break a rule and it has a
good effect, then do that."*

**The effect is the only authority.** Every rule in this file is a *cached judgment* -
a compressed record of something Mark reacted to once. The cache exists because he
cannot read every sentence, not because the rule outranks him. When a rule and the
effect on the page disagree, **the effect wins and the rule gets amended the same day.**

That is easy to write and hard to act on, so three mechanisms make it real.

### 1. Rules come in three kinds, and they do not have equal force

| Kind | What it says | Force |
|---|---|---|
| **Prohibition** | *Avoid this - it reliably fails* | Near-absolute. Cheap to obey; not doing something rarely costs originality. Break only with an argument for the specific case. |
| **Prescription** | *This move worked once* | **Available, never required.** Ration it. A prescription applied every time has stopped being a rule and become a shape. |
| **Working** | *How we operate* | Obey. These govern process, not prose, and breaking them just means working badly. |

**This distinction is the whole answer to the tension.** L-006 collapsed into formula
because it was written as a prescription and then obeyed like a prohibition - a move
Mark liked once became the ending of 71% of everything. Prohibitions cannot collapse
that way: avoiding the tactile register or refusing to answer unasked questions
constrains failure without dictating what fills the space.

### 2. Every rule carries its evidence, and thin evidence is a warning

`Basis: n` records how many independent critiques support a rule. **Almost everything
here is n=1.** A prescription with `n=1` that has fired more than three times without
re-validation has outrun its evidence and is due for a deliberate test - write the
version that ignores it and put both in front of Mark.

Current standing: **L-006 is n=1 and has fired twelve times.** It is the most
over-trusted rule in the repo.

### 3. Breaking a rule is an experiment, and experiments get logged

Not a licence and not a quota. When a passage is better for ignoring a prescription,
do it, then record it in `craft/deviations.md`: the rule, what was done instead, the
argument, and Mark's verdict. A break Mark likes amends the rule. A break he does not
becomes a counterexample that strengthens it. Either way the doctrine learns, which an
unlogged break cannot make it do.

**What this does not license.** "It felt better" is not an argument. Neither is breaking
a prohibition to seem bold - using a banned phrase is not a deviation, it is a mistake
with a story attached.

## The beat index — where each rule fires

**Added 2026-09-09, harvested from story 01. Read this before reaching for any rule
below.**

Three of the eleven critiques on story 01 were **correct rules applied at the wrong
point in the story.** Not misunderstood rules, not bad prose — right instrument, wrong
beat. That failure mode was invisible because this file listed rules without saying
*where* they apply, and a rule with no beat attached will get used wherever it seems to
fit, which is usually too early.

| Instrument | Fires | Never fires |
|---|---|---|
| **Flatness / withdrawn interiority** (L-006) | the **final** beat, after the character has stopped having a crisis | mid-story — it reads as the event being *skipped*, not absorbed (L-018) |
| **Accommodation behaviour** (L-006, §6 beat 2) | only after she has fought and the ordinary world has beaten her | before the fight; the reader asks *why is she still sitting there* (L-019) |
| **Duration / a number of years** (L-006) | when the fear is **unseen** and the price is the reader's only instrument | on a fear the reader can already see; and **never as cadence** — a repeated tally is a tic, and it must be checked against the character's actual age (Mark, 2026-09-10: nobody leads a baby away from a statue) |
| **A recurring schedule** | never. A ritual on a timetable is an accommodation and domesticates the horror | anywhere. One unterminated occasion instead |
| **The banked-image relocation** (L-017) | the last sentence, as a refrain the reader already holds | as an elegant inference — restraint at the close is withholding |
| **Establish the frame** (L-009) | first sentence of every scene | — |
| **Wrongness in the approach** (L-010) | the run-up to each contact | the contact itself, which stays thin |
| **The body answers first** (L-001) | every moment of fear, throughout | — |
| **Killing the mundane explanation** (L-018) | once, as a question to the one person who could have done it | as a survey; a fact needs no lawyer |
| **Explicit physical** (D-003, §11) | late, once | early — a story that opens physical has nowhere to go |

**The general form of the failure:** the instruments of *aftermath* — flatness,
accommodation, duration, permanence — are the ones this file documents best, because
they were all extracted from a single 200-word vignette that was **nothing but
aftermath**. At story length they are the last movement only, and reaching for them
earlier hollows out the middle. Ask of any rule before applying it: *what has the
character already tried, and has the reader felt the event yet?*

## The stage index — *when* a rule is allowed to fire

**Added 2026-09-09 from Mark, and it outranks the beat index above.** The beat index says
where in a *story* an instrument fires. This says where in the *pipeline* a rule may be
applied at all, and it was written because I had been applying all of them at the worst
possible moment.

| Stage | What may filter here |
|---|---|
| **0. Invent** — generating candidates | **Nothing.** Generate as though the craft folder did not exist. Supply is free here and variance is the entire product. |
| **1. Select** — choosing what to write | Repetition across the collection, **as a flag, not a verdict** — is this a near neighbour of something spent, and if so, is the story it grows into actually a different story? Mark picks (L-013). |
| **2. Draft** — writing prose | Prohibitions come on. Prescriptions are available and rationed. The beat index governs. |
| **3. Revise** — after critique | Everything, plus the fresh-context review (L-020) and the pre-flight (`ai-tells.md` §E). |

**Why the deferral is free rather than a trade.** Every prohibition in this file was
extracted from critique of *finished prose* — a tactile adjective, an orphan clause, an
unasked question, a last paragraph that explains. They describe **sentences**. At the
invention stage there are no sentences, so deferring them costs nothing and applying them
costs the only thing invention produces.

**The tests are the sharpest case.** D-004 asks *could a reader finish this and answer
"what was it" in a line?* That is a test on a **finished story**. A premise is a one-line
answer by construction — every idea in this book is — so running D-004 at invention
forbids everything. The question is never *is the premise legible* but *does the prose let
the reader assemble it.*

**Kind** determines force (see above): prohibitions are near-absolute, prescriptions are
available but never required, working rules govern process. **Basis** is how many
independent critiques support the rule. **Fired** counts prescriptions only - how many
pieces have applied it. A prescription whose Fired count badly exceeds its Basis is a
formula waiting to be noticed.

**The bookkeeping family (L-003, L-004, L-005).** One failure in three shapes: a
sentence that makes the reader stop and do clerical work. All three read as *odd*
rather than *wrong* - when a line is reported as strange rather than incorrect, look
for the bookkeeping.

## How entries are made

After each critique round, every piece of feedback is converted into a durable,
generalisable rule. Raw feedback stays in the story folder
(`stories/<slug>/critique-NN.md`); only the generalised lesson comes here.

A good entry states the rule, the evidence, and how to apply it — so that it is
actionable on a story that has nothing to do with the one that produced it.

Entry format:

```
### L-007 — Short rule name
**Rule:** One sentence, imperative.
**Origin:** Story slug, critique round, date.
**Evidence:** What Mark actually objected to, briefly quoted.
**How to apply:** What to do differently, concretely.
**Status:** active | superseded by L-0NN
```

## Standing decisions from Mark

Direction given outright rather than extracted from a critique. Same authority as a
lesson: these override anything written elsewhere in the repo.

### D-001 — Fully standalone stories
**Rule:** No shared geography, family, object, or continuity between stories; none
may depend on another having been read.
**Origin:** Setup, 2026-09-08.
**How to apply:** Invent a fresh corner of the Gulf South per story. Cohesion comes
from register and motif only. Resist every temptation toward callbacks — including
the two plates that share a staircase.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §12 rewritten; `book-bible.md` § Setting
rewritten; the linked-collection proposal in `plate-inventory.md` withdrawn.
**Status:** active

### D-002 — Mostly period, some modern
**Rule:** Center the book c. 1850–1920; allow one or two present-day stories that
inherit a period object or house.
**Origin:** Setup, 2026-09-08.
**How to apply:** A modern story must be frightening in a way a period story could
not be — otherwise set it in the past. Track period in the ledger so the modern
stories stay rare.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §10 rewritten.
**Status:** active

### D-003 — Willing to get physical
**Rule:** Body horror is a regular instrument, spent precisely and late, in a
vegetal rather than visceral register.
**Origin:** Setup, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark chose "willing to get physical" over my restraint default,
consistent with Plate V.
**How to apply:** Growth through the body, waterlogged tissue, root and fibre — not
blood and wounds. Flattest sentence in the story delivers the worst image. Roughly a
third of stories explicit, a third threatened, a third at the edge of frame. The
child-harm limit still stands.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §11 rewritten from "Cruelty budget"; ledger
gained a `Physical` column.
**Status:** active

### D-004 - The absence of an explanation is the horror; `wound` is not a key
**Rule:** No story explains what the thing is, and no story is *about* what the thing
is. The `wound` field records what the haunting costs, never what it means.
**Origin:** Mark, 2026-09-08, choosing the wound for story 01.
**Evidence:** *"I think that there isn't an explanation of what the thing is and that's
where the horror lies. It's unexplanable enough to be supernatural, and it has an
effect."*
**How to apply:**
- **This does not repeal L-008.** L-008 requires *me* to know the rule; D-004 forbids
  the story from having a thesis. Compatible: a mechanism I can state in one sentence
  and never write down, versus a meaning the reader is meant to extract. Keep the first,
  refuse the second.
- **What it does repeal is the `wound` field as written.** `stories/_template.md` asked
  for *"what the haunting is actually about"*, and the four options I offered Mark were
  four theses - a dead sibling, arithmetic as her only power, exclusion, exclusion from
  play. He declined all four, correctly: each of them is an answer, and an answer is
  what §7 calls the worst ending arriving early.
- **Two clauses, not one.** *"It is unexplainable enough to be supernatural, and it has
  an effect."* Both halves are load-bearing and the second is the one that fails in
  practice. Unexplained-with-no-effect is not mystery, it is the hollow L-008 was
  written about. The bar is: nothing in the story accounts for it, and something
  measurable is different because of it.
- **The test.** If a reader could finish the story and write a one-line answer to *what
  was it*, the draft has an explanation in it whether or not a character stated one.
  Costs, schedules and habits are permitted; causes are not.
- **A visible consequence is not an explanation. Added 2026-09-09.** Story 01's draft-03
  ending shows the reader what became of the missing child — her father finds her in the
  top of the fig tree. Mark, accepting the trade: *"It does point more towards the thing
  being real but that's ok."* This looks like a breach of the first clause and is not one.
  - **What the ending supplies is one act, once, on one child.** Three others died of
    fever in their beds the same summer and stayed dead. Same summer, same thing,
    different outcomes, so there is nothing to extract. The reader still cannot write the
    one-line answer, which is the actual test.
  - **This is the second clause working, not the first failing.** *"It has an effect"* is
    the half this rule records as the one that fails in practice, and an effect the reader
    can **see** is its strongest available form. Unexplained-with-no-visible-effect is the
    hollow L-008 was written about.
  - **The line to hold:** show what it did. Never how, never why, never under what
    conditions, and never twice the same way. A single consequence is a fact; two
    consequences that match are a rule.
- **A rhyme is a cost if it attaches to a character and an explanation if it attaches to
  the thing. Added 2026-09-09.** Story 01's draft-03 fresh-context review (L-020) proposed
  a religious layer whose stated mechanism was that *the monster is a rule-follower and so
  is the Church*, delivered by planting the priest's extended-arms posture once and
  unremarked so the reader would silently join it to the laid-out child's arms and to the
  game.
  - **The join would have been completed, not searched**, so it passes L-006's good-gap
    test. The objection is not bookkeeping. It is that **what the reader completes is a
    meaning**: with the posture planted, *what was it* acquires a one-line answer, and that
    is this rule's only real test.
  - **The distinction that survives is the usable half.** The same layer's other half -
    ritual precision as ordinary household life, forms done in order, a thing done wrong
    not counting - attaches to **the protagonist and her household**, and its payoff is
    that a woman exact about forms all summer to no effect is exact about a different one
    at the end. That is a *cost*, which is what §4's replacement test asks for, and it
    answers nothing.
  - **The question to ask of any proposed rhyme: which end of it is the thing?** Attach it
    to a person and it prices the haunting; attach it to the haunting and it explains it.
  - `craft-principles.md` §4's *"a thing that is the shape of grief is an explanation"*
    generalises - substitute devotion, inheritance, debt. A rhyme is the same failure at
    lower volume, which is why it gets through.
**Consequences:** `stories/_template.md` `wound` field rewritten; the `haunt_logic`
ledger column records the off-page mechanism, which is why it stays off the page.
**Status:** active

### D-005 - Period is a register, not a research standard
**Rule:** Get the feel and the objects right. Do not audit the stories for historical,
economic or chronological consistency. Concept anachronism stays banned, because it is
heard rather than checked.
**Origin:** Mark, 2026-09-09, declining `critique-02.md` Tier 3b.
**Evidence:** *"this level of historical accuracy is not necessary. We're going to be
writing ghost stories. If ghosts can be real then being one year off (1877 vs 1878) and
having slight contradictions that no one would notice is fine. I don't want to lessen the
impact of the story by trying to fix these issues unless you think there's a way to do it
cleanly."*
**How to apply:**
- **This is L-016 arriving in the reference library instead of in the plot.** It is the
  same sentence that killed the game's rules — *"a horror story doesn't need this level of
  internal consistency and plot coherency"* — pointed at the period research rather than
  at the haunting. **Historical coherence is not a horror value either.** A contradiction
  no reader will notice costs nothing; a paragraph correcting it costs the page.
- **What stays binding.** `reference/period/anachronism-traps.md`, and above all **concept**
  anachronism — the psychological vocabulary, *traumatised*, *catatonic*, *depressed*.
  That is not a fact-check, it is a register failure: the reader hears a modern interior
  and leaves the story. Also the objects, per `style-guide.md`: get the objects right and
  the language can stay clean.
- **What is released.** Dates, scale, economics, the labour system, who owned what, and
  the internal arithmetic of a farm. Story 01's plantation-versus-smallholding
  contradiction was worth fixing only because the fix was a dozen words and *improved*
  the story by explaining why Hollis is never available. That is the bar: a fix earns its
  place by making the page better, never by making the record consistent.
- **The exception Mark left open, and the only route by which this returns.** Mark:
  *"I'm fine explaining that the woman laying out the bodies is a black woman if it would
  add to the story."* **Specification is permitted as a craft move and forbidden as a
  correction.** A character made concrete is `style-guide.md`'s own discipline — not a
  bird but a grackle. A character specified to answer a charge is throat-clearing, and it
  reads as the author stepping in front of the story. Test: does the detail do work in
  the scene it stands in? Then it is in. Does it exist because a reviewer noticed
  something missing? Then it is out.
- **Consequence for the fresh-context review stage (L-020).** A clean reader will raise
  this note on every period story in the book, and will be right that the vacancy is
  there. It is **triaged and declined, not unnoticed.** Do not spend a round re-litigating
  it; log the recurrence and move on.
**Consequences:** `critique-02.md` Tier 3b closed - the cheap half applied in draft 02,
the wider half declined here rather than promoted to a research programme. The Mrs Rey
specification survives as a craft option and is being taken through the laying-out
asymmetry, not as a fix.
**Status:** active

## Lessons from critiques

### L-001 — The body answers before the mind does
**Rule:** Render the involuntary physical event in sequence — body first, then
knowing, then behaviour — and give the body its own sentences.
**Origin:** Vignette batch 01 (A1, "The staircase"), critique 01, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"the thing that happens should be the effect the feeling has on
the person, which is pretty much absent. I want something describing the hair
standing up on the back of the neck of the character in a way that elicits this in
the reader."*
**How to apply:**
- Put the autonomic event **before** the character understands anything. She does not
  become frightened and then get gooseflesh; the gooseflesh is how she finds out.
- Pick **one or two** physical facts and render them with anatomical precision and
  **duration**. Not "her hair stood up" but the nape wet since six, and the hair
  unsticking from the skin a few at a time. The cliché versions are compressions; the
  fix is to decompress, not to substitute a fresher cliché.
- Prefer sensations that contradict the conditions: gooseflesh in a hot hall, sweat
  going cold on a bright morning. Physiology that fights the weather reads as fact.
- **Behaviour alone is under-rendering.** Misfired actions (`style-guide.md` §
  Emotion) are the *aftermath* of fear and cannot carry it by themselves. A paragraph
  built only of behaviour makes the reader a witness rather than a participant.
- The ban in `style-guide.md` covers **naming the emotion** and the **stock
  renderings** ("a shiver ran down her spine"). It does not cover the body.
**Ceiling (added 2026-09-08, critique 02):** render the body at the resolution the
*frightened person* has of it, never an anatomist's. **Fear narrows perception; it
does not sharpen it.** She does not feel individual hairs release in sequence — she
feels something large and stupid and wants to be outside. One or two blunt physical
facts, then get her moving. Precision belongs to the *approach*; the moment itself is
coarse. Over-rendering fails in exactly the same way under-rendering does: the reader
watches instead of feeling.
**Discovered, not felt (added 2026-09-08, story 01 movement three, basis → 4):** Mark,
on a woman doing the wash while the thing is in her yard: *"she wouldn't be casually
washing things like it's just another day, she could be unknowingly clenching her fists
or some other unconscious activity that she becomes aware of."* Two clauses:
- **The involuntary act is evidence she finds on herself.** Not *her hands tightened* but
  *she noticed her hands were closed and had to be got open.* The order is act → notice →
  work out how long. This is sharper than the original rule, which only required the body
  to precede the knowing; here the body has been at work for some time before she arrives.
  Duration is the whole effect, and she is the last to know.
- **Inaction must be physically expensive.** A character who is not acting is not idle,
  and prose that renders her routine as routine has quietly told the reader there is
  nothing wrong. Give the stillness a cost with a domestic object in it — a piece wrung
  past wringing until it is dry in the twist, water gone still in the tub, a thing put in
  the basket unrinsed. **The tell is a character performing an ordinary task at ordinary
  speed while something impossible is in view.**
**Consequences:** `style-guide.md` § Emotion rewritten into three tiers;
`ai-tells.md` §E gains check 9. Basis 3 → 4; the most-evidenced rule in the repo, and the
only one to have replicated three times.
**Status:** active, amended by L-002


### L-002 — Feedback names an experience, not a mechanism
**Rule:** When Mark names a detail, he is naming the effect he wants; build the
experience, and treat the named detail as optional.
**Origin:** Vignette 1 r2, critique 02, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** *"'Hair on the back of your neck' was meant to be a metaphor for
'describe a human experiencing fear in this situation', not a request to give a
medically accurate description of the process of piloerection."*
**How to apply:** Read every note as *"produce this feeling"* first and *"insert this
thing"* only if the literal reading still makes sense afterward. Idioms in particular
("her blood ran cold", "it made my skin crawl", "a slap in the face") are names for
states, not specifications. The tell that the literal reading has been taken: the
draft explains the mechanism of something the reader already knows by feel. If a note
could be satisfied without the named noun appearing once, that is usually the better
draft.
**Status:** active

### L-003 — Obliquity is not restraint
**Rule:** Say the plain thing about a strange event; never a strange thing about a
plain one. If the reader must decode a sentence, it has failed.
**Origin:** Vignette 1 r2, critique 02, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** *"'It was wet there', is this describing sweat on her neck? That's a
super weird way to describe it."*
**How to apply:** Sweat is sweat. The urge to avoid a plain word because it feels
stock is the wrong instinct — the cure for a stale phrase is a *more specific* plain
statement, not an oblique one. Test: could a reader name the referent of this sentence
without going back a line? If not, rewrite it flat. The strangeness budget belongs to
the haunting; ordinary bodies and ordinary furniture get ordinary words.
**Third replication, 2026-09-10, story 03 movement one, basis 2 → 3 — and it names the
mechanism by which this rule gets broken while being obeyed.** The approved beat read
*"His shoes were a child's shoes and had to be sent for."* I compressed it to *"His shoes
had to be sent for."* Mark: *"I think we should keep the childs shoes line instead of just
saying they were sent for which could mean anything."*
- **The compression instinct removes the concrete half, because the concrete half is what
  looks redundant.** *A child's shoes* and *had to be sent for* appear to say one thing
  twice — a fact and its consequence. They do not: the second half alone is a procurement
  detail that fits a man with big feet, a mourning dress, or a plough part. All the
  information was in the clause that looked like decoration.
- **Procedure, and it costs nothing:** when trimming an approved beat, name the fact each
  half carries before deleting either. If one half is the fact and the other is the
  consequence, the consequence is the cuttable one.
- **Why it is this rule and not brevity.** *Sent for* is not vague, it is **oblique** — it
  points at the fact from one remove and asks the reader to arrive at *small* on their own.
  That is the same failure as *"it was wet there"*, at a smaller scale and in a sentence
  that reads perfectly well, which is why three rounds have not exhausted it.
**Second instance, same round, 2026-09-10 — and it is the one that disguises itself as
craft.** Mark, on *"In August she dreamed about it"*: *"'It' is the figure, but it's not
immediately clear. In this case you can deduce it and the omission might be deliberate for
subtlety, just wanted to check, because there's a few occurrences of this throughout
regarding the figure."* It was not deliberate. The nearest antecedent was **the crape
myrtle**, from *take the other side down to match it* at the end of the paragraph before.
- **Withholding a name is not withholding an explanation, and only the second one is the
  method.** The haunting has a neutral noun — *the figure* — that dates nothing, accounts
  for nothing and commits to nothing. Using it costs **zero** mystery. So there is never a
  trade to make: an unanchored pronoun for the thing buys no subtlety, it only charges a
  decode. D-004 and L-016 govern whether anything *accounts* for it; they have nothing to
  say about whether the prose may point at it.
- **Why this one is dangerous:** an evasive pronoun *feels* like restraint, and it is
  exactly the feeling this rule exists to refuse. It is also the only member of the
  bookkeeping family a writer will defend on principle.
- **The test for a pronoun standing for the haunting:** is the haunting the **nearest**
  noun of that gender/number? Not the most important one, not the one the scene is about —
  the nearest. Four failed in story 03, including *"Nobody could tell you when it came"*,
  the head of the entire reference chain, whose preceding paragraph ended *Her mother had
  walked fast.*
- **The specific trap: two pronouns of the same form in adjacent sentences, one of them the
  haunting.** *"He said it was watching at her. / Elmire let it go."* — the first *it* is
  the figure, the second is the child's remark. Fixed by moving the ordinary one to *that*
  and leaving the pronoun to the thing.
**Status:** active

### L-004 — No answers to unasked questions
**Rule:** Cut any sentence that defends, excuses, or corrects something the reader has
not yet thought about.
**Origin:** Vignette 1 r2, critique 02, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** *"the layout of the house was a fault in the builder and not in her.
That just seemed like an odd thing to add... when I read it, it just seemed strange"*
**How to apply:** The reader stops and hunts for the argument the sentence is
answering, finds none, and registers it as *off* without being able to say why — Mark
could not, and it still cost him the paragraph. Three specific forms to grep for:
- **The pre-emptive defence.** Excusing a character before anything has been alleged.
- **The narrator's aside at the threshold.** Wit immediately before the fear puts a
  commentator between the reader and the character. Be funny early or not at all.
- **The pre-excused reaction.** Signalling that a character is about to be frightened
  and that it is reasonable. It spoils the event and defuses it in one move.
**Status:** active

### L-005 — Elide on the second mention; never restate to negate
**Rule:** When a sentence reverses or corrects the one before it, replace the repeated
words with the shortest pro-form English allows, and put the resolution within a
clause of the contradiction.
**Origin:** Vignette 1 r3, critique 03, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark on *"The basket was on the wrong hip. It was not on the wrong hip,
but that was the thought she had"*: *"sounds a little awkward reading it because 'but
that was the thought she had' resolves the strangeness of the contradiction too late.
It's something to do with saying something, and then using almost the same words to
say the opposite."*
**How to apply:**
- **Mechanism.** A restated phrase makes the reader re-parse words they already hold,
  hunting for what changed. If the only change is a negation, they have spent five
  words of attention to receive one bit — and for the whole length of that phrase the
  contradiction is standing unexplained. "It wasn't" costs nothing and carries the
  same bit, so the resolution arrives while the contradiction is still warm.
- **Rule of thumb:** a contradiction may stand unresolved for about a clause. Longer
  and the reader stops reading and starts doing bookkeeping.
- **Not a blanket ban on repetition.** Full restatement is an emphasis figure and it
  works when the repetition *intensifies* — "He was not a kind man. He was not a kind
  man at all." It fails when the repetition *reverses*, because reversal is new
  information and verbatim repetition signals the absence of new information. The
  form contradicts the content.
- **Near deixis in free indirect style:** *this* thought, not *that* thought. "That"
  narrates her from outside; "this" is in her head. Use the closer word wherever the
  prose is inside a character.
- While rewriting, **count the pronouns.** Three referents for "it" in one sentence is
  the same tax arriving by another route.
- **A cut can orphan a referent, and this is now the commonest way it happens.**
  Added 2026-09-08, story 01 draft 01. *"They were out again when she got there"* was
  anchored by the paragraph before it and by *there* = the room. Cutting that paragraph
  and trimming to *"when she went back in"* left the pronoun with no nearby antecedent,
  activated the indoor/outdoor frame, and Mark read *they* as **the children**, not the
  arms - not ambiguity, a wrong resolution. Fixed by naming the noun: *"When she came
  back in, the arms were out to the sides again."*
  - **After every cut, re-read the two sentences that now abut** and name the referent of
    every pronoun and deictic in them. Material that was doing anchoring work is invisible
    once removed.
  - **Watch for collocation traps.** *Out* belongs to children far more strongly than to
    arms; *in* and *out* in one clause recruit the house frame whether or not it is meant.
    Two senses of a common preposition in adjacent sentences is a decode.
  - **First replication of this clause, 2026-09-09, story 01 draft 04, basis 1 → 2.** Mark
    on the story's climactic sentence, *"She went round, and she stopped, and she stood
    where she had stopped"*: *"'She went round' almost seems like it refers to Emmy even
    though the reader might infer it's Alma, should we say Alma went round?"* Same failure
    as *they* = the children: a **wrong resolution**, not an ambiguity, at the most
    important sentence in the story — and again the fix was to name the noun.
  - **The collocation trap is stronger than distance, and this is the sharpening.** The
    orphan clause above reads as a rule about *proximity*, and the draft-04 case satisfies
    proximity: the correct antecedent was in the immediately preceding clause (*"he did not
    go out to her again"*) and the wrong one was four paragraphs back. It still misread,
    because **the verb selects the antecedent.** *Went round* belongs to whoever is at the
    tree — going round is what the thing and the children do — and the child in the tree
    is the wrong woman. Ask not *how far away is the antecedent* but **which noun does this
    verb already belong to in this story.** A signature verb is a magnet, and the story's
    own repetitions are what magnetise it.
  - **A possessive is not an antecedent, and a section break resets anchoring. Added
    2026-09-10, story 03, basis 2 → 3.** Mark, on a paragraph opening *"One afternoon in July
    she was at her own plot"*: *"who is the 'she' referring to in the second paragraph? Is it
    clear and I'm just missing it?"* He was not missing it. The paragraph above it read
    *"...he called **Elmire's** mother maman. He called the mare maman, and **Elmire's**
    brother thought that was the finest thing he had ever heard... and the mare would put
    **her** ears back at nothing at all, being a mare."*
    - **Elmire appears twice and is available as an antecedent neither time.** A genitive is
      grammatically subordinate — the reader holds *mother* and *brother* as the referents and
      files the possessor as a modifier. Two mentions of a name can therefore leave a pronoun
      with **nothing** to attach to, which is why this survives proofreading: the name is
      right there on the page.
    - **The nearest available antecedent was a horse**, and there were two others competing
      (a seventy-year-old housekeeper, the mother). The fix is the same as every previous
      instance of this rule: **name the noun.**
    - **A section or movement break resets pronoun anchoring.** Movement three opened *"She
      went over to Azélie's"* across a break; a reader who has just crossed white space is not
      still holding a pronoun. Name the subject in the first sentence after any break — which
      is L-009 arriving at the same place from the other side.
    - **Sweep, do not spot-check.** Print the first line of every paragraph and check each
      opening pronoun against the *last named referent of that gender*, not against who the
      paragraph is obviously about. Five faults in this draft, four of them invisible to me
      until the list was in front of me, and one — *"He said it was watching at her" /
      "She let it go"* — had the dead grandmother as its nearest antecedent.
    - **This is L-025's POV clause arriving as grammar.** All five faults are places where a
      first-person original had *I*, which needs no antecedent at all. **Converting to close
      third turns every free pronoun into a resolution problem**, and the conversion is exactly
      when nobody is looking for one.
  - **A move is two seams and a stale reference, and it is the case this clause was
    under-reading. Added 2026-09-10, story 03, Mark's process question:** *"are you just
    slotting a paragraph between other ones, or are you manipulating the surrounding text to
    make sure everything still flows?"* The clause above is written for **cuts**, so I ran it
    on the cut end of a paragraph move and not on the other two exposures. A relocation has
    three:
    1. **The cut seam** — the two sentences that now abut where it left.
    2. **The insertion seam** — the two it now sits between, in both directions.
    3. **What the paragraph itself depended on where it used to live** — its time-phrase, its
       pronouns, and above all **first mentions.** A paragraph that was the reader's
       introduction to a person, place or object stops being that the moment it moves, and
       every later reference silently becomes a forward reference.
    In story 03 the peppermint paragraph moved from movement one to movement two. Its
    time-phrase was rewritten and its neighbour chosen deliberately; nobody checked the
    third exposure, and it landed one paragraph below *"Ovide had taken the short way through
    to the store his whole life"* — so the reader now had a man walking **to** a store and
    then standing **behind** its counter, with nothing joining the two. The underlying fault
    was older and worse: **the store had never been established at all**, in any draft. It is
    called Hébert's four times across three movements, glossed nowhere, and Ovide's relation
    to it never stated. That is L-003 and L-009 on a proper noun, and it survived a critique
    round, a fresh-context review and a backward pass because a name that recurs *looks*
    established.
  - **Procedure:** on any move, re-read three places, not one. Then grep the moved
    paragraph's proper nouns across the whole draft and check that the **first** occurrence
    is the one that explains them.
  - **Deictics resolve against the nearest landmark, not against the character's posture.**
    Same round, same movement. *"There was a smell down there that he knew and did not have
    a name for"* was written from where his head was — bent over, hands on his knees — and
    Mark read it as the ground: *"Down makes it seem like the ground."* The reader has no
    access to the posture and every reason to resolve *down there* against the only landmark
    in the sentence's vicinity. **A bare deictic of place is only safe when the landmark it
    points at is the one the reader is already holding.** Fixed by restoring the refrain's
    place-phrase: *in the hall* → *in the yard*.

**Pattern — the bookkeeping family (L-003, L-004, L-005).** These are one failure in
three shapes: **a sentence that makes the reader stop and do clerical work.** Decoding
a referent (L-003), hunting for an argument that was never made (L-004), holding a
contradiction that has not yet resolved (L-005). None of them reads as *wrong*; each
reads as faintly *odd* — which is why Mark reported the symptom twice while saying he
could not explain it. **When a line is reported as strange rather than as incorrect,
look for the bookkeeping.** That is the diagnostic.
**Status:** active

### L-006 — Price the fear; end on the accommodation

**Register note added 2026-09-10: retrospective first person is composed by construction.**
Mark, on a confessional narrator: *"It's almost like a calm voice of a narrator is playing in
my head, but a person in a horror story should not be calm. You can get away from this by
talking about other people and being separate from it."* A narrator writing thirty years on has
survived the thing, and survival is settled; the voice also announces the fear (*I could not
stand to watch*) instead of producing it. Close third, in scene, puts the fear back in the
body where the reader has to notice it — and lets the reader see things about the character
that the character does not. This does not retire first person, which the book still owes; it
retires the **memoir** register for fear.

**Rule:** Measure a fear by what the character permanently pays to avoid it. State the
price in a unit, never state the fear, and never join the two.
**Origin:** Vignette 1 r4, critique 04, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"It was an inconvenience for her to use the kitchen door but she
did it to avoid going by the stairs, and this was a good way of describing it."*
Control available in-repo: draft 01 ended on the same detour and the same eleven steps
and was inert.
**How to apply:**
- **Quantify the cost; leave the fear unnamed.** "Eleven steps each way" is exact and
  countable; the thing on the landing is never described or referred to again. The
  reader divides one by the other and sizes the fear themselves.
- **Make the price small.** Fleeing the house is proportionate to fright and therefore
  carries no information. A trivial, permanent, daily tax is worse, because it says the
  character has neither fought the thing nor run from it — she has built a workaround
  and gone on with her life. **The accommodation implies the haunting is a settled
  condition and that she knows it.**
- **Never join cause to cost.** The word *stairs* must not appear in the paragraph that
  charges for them. The reader closes the gap in one step, and closing it is what makes
  them complicit.
- **Withdraw interiority for the last beat.** Retreat to logistics and behaviour. She
  has stopped having a crisis about it, because it is her life now; the narration
  stopping too is the proof.
  **Boundary, added 2026-09-08 (L-018): the last beat only.** This move is why the
  ending works and it is why story 01's mid-story arms beat failed. There, no settled
  condition had been established yet, so the same withdrawal read as the event being
  *skipped* rather than absorbed. Mid-story, the reader must be made to register the
  event first - body, knowing, misfired action, failed search - and the prose may go
  cold only afterward.
- **Duration, not escalation.** Nothing gets worse; it gets permanent. "For the rest of
  the summer" is the whole move.
  **Boundary, added 2026-09-08 (story 01, ending r2): duration prices an unseen fear and
  dilutes a seen one.** The move works in the staircase vignette because the thing on the
  landing is never described — eleven steps a day is the reader's only instrument for
  sizing it. Story 01's ending put *"She kept it up eleven years"* on a woman the reader
  can already see going round a tree in a cut sheet, and Mark's verdict was that it *"doesn't
  hit... her doing this at all is creepy enough."* **Once the image is fully visible, a
  number stops multiplying it and starts measuring it** — and measuring is the arithmetic
  L-016 bans. Test: has the reader *seen* the thing being priced? If yes, drop the number.
  - **If permanence is still wanted, give it an object instead of a figure.** A ring worn
    in the grass round the tree carries the same years without counting them, and it is a
    thing in the world rather than a sum.
  - **A ritual on a schedule is an accommodation, and it domesticates the horror.**
    Third amendment to this bullet, 2026-09-08 — L-006 is now the most-corrected rule in
    the repo. Story 01's ending had the woman go out *"at two every day after that"*, and
    Mark: *"doing it day after day at 2, then stopping makes it seem like she goes back to
    her regular scheduled day... we can't have her indefinitely insane so we have to cut
    it off. She does it for hours, and the reader fills in the rest."* **Recurrence
    implies a life lived around the thing, which is exactly what an accommodation is** —
    and a character with a life around it is eccentric rather than lost. The unbounded
    version is a **single occasion that is never shown ending**: hours, not years, and the
    reader never sees her come back in. Her fate — dies, committed, goes on — is left to
    the reader, which is D-004 applied to the protagonist and not only to the haunting.
  - **Watch for lines that presuppose conversation.** *"She never asked him to again"* was
    cut for smuggling coherence back in: it implies a marriage still capable of a request
    and a refusal. A character meant to be beyond reach cannot be given a sentence that
    assumes she can be talked to.
  - **Never render permanence as an accumulation list.** *She did it in the rain. She did
    it in January. She did it in August.* Three anaphoric sentences hand every element the
    same weight and flatten all three — L-011's objection arriving by anaphora rather than
    polysyndeton. One instance, placed where it does most work, beats an inventory.
- **Precondition — this is why the draft-01 version was dead.** A price is only
  information if the reader already knows what is being bought. Do not reach for the
  accommodation ending unless the fear has actually landed on the page first.

**Boundary — the good gap vs. the bookkeeping family (L-003/004/005).** The reader must
infer *kitchen door → avoiding the stairs*, which resembles the clerical work those
lessons ban. The test that separates them: **does the reader complete the inference or
search for it?** Complete: every piece present, one step, unambiguous, and a small
pleasure to arrive at. Search: re-parsing words already held, hunting material never
supplied, or holding a contradiction open. Aim for gaps that get *closed*, never gaps
that get *searched*.
**Status:** active

### L-007 — Change the register, not the adjective
**Rule:** When a sensation is clichéd, render it in a different sensory register
rather than hunting a fresher word for the same one. Put the strangeness in the
**perception**; keep the **words** plain.
**Origin:** Vignette 1 r4, critique 05, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark on *"Her back had never been so much of her before. She could feel
the whole width of it."* — *"It captures the feeling of someone being behind you that
doesn't feel cliche."*
**How to apply:**
- **The tactile register is spent.** Hair rising, skin crawling, prickling, cold
  spots, gooseflesh — every stock rendering of *someone is behind you* lives here.
  r2 rendered the tactile version at maximum resolution and was still dead, which is
  the proof that resolution is not the variable. Register is.
- **Registers available**, none of them worked out:
  - **Proprioceptive** — how big a part of you feels, whether a limb still seems
    yours. *(the back becoming a larger fraction of her)*
  - **Spatial** — distance misbehaving. *(the hall was nine feet and took a long
    time)*
  - **Temporal** — duration misbehaving.
  - **Cognitive** — the stupid thought that will not drop. *(the basket on the wrong
    hip)*
  - **Motor** — what the body tries to do, or fails at. *(the latch worked twice; her
    legs went)*
  - **Attentional** — what she cannot stop monitoring.
- **Strange perception, plain words.** Compare the two lines from the same vignette:

  | | perception | phrasing | result |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | "It was wet there" | ordinary | oblique | fails (L-003) |
  | "her back had never been so much of her before" | strange | plain | works |

  Oblique phrasing of an ordinary perception is the failure; plain phrasing of an odd
  perception is the fix. This is the positive statement of L-003.
- **No simile *for a sensation*.** "It was as if her back had become enormous" inserts
  an author performing a comparison. Declare the impossible-sounding fact flatly. A
  statement about a character's relation to her own body is unfalsifiable — the reader
  can only check it against theirs, where it is true. Aim for recognition, not
  persuasion.
  **Boundary (added 2026-09-08):** a simile that identifies an *action* or its *social
  register* is permitted and is often the sharpest instrument available — *"the fingers
  folded over his own to be sure of it, the way you would give a man his change."* The
  banned kind decorates a feeling the reader must be made to have; the permitted kind
  classifies a behaviour, and by making the impossible act *ordinary and social* it
  does the work no direct description can. Test: **is the comparison telling the reader
  how something felt, or what kind of thing it was?** The first is dressing; the second
  is evidence.
- **Choose the axis deliberately.** *Width*, not size or shape: the lateral dimension
  is the surface that can be **seen**, so the word says the threat is a gaze rather
  than a hand without either word appearing.
- **Audit by register.** Count the fear-renderings in a passage and tag each one. r2:
  four renderings, three tactile — failed. r4: five renderings, none tactile — worked.
  If two or more sit in the same register, move one.
**Consequences:** `style-guide.md` § Emotion tier 1 rewritten (it still recommended
the tactile examples and still asked for anatomical resolution, both reversed by the
L-001 ceiling); `ai-tells.md` §C gains "The tactile default".
**Status:** active

### L-008 - Know the rule; never state it. Undecided is not withheld.
**Rule:** Before drafting, write down in one sentence what the thing wants and what it
does. Keep it off the page permanently - but do not begin without it.
**Origin:** Vignettes 2 and 3 r5, critique 06, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"what's happening in this story exactly? Why is there an extra
spot? who sits in it? What is the reason that this is scary?"* and *"what happened when
he retrieved his knife? He let other things go for a reason. Why?"*
**How to apply:**
- `craft-principles.md` §3 and James's *"we do not want to see the bones of their
  theory"* both presuppose **that there is a theory**. Withholding a rule and never
  having formed one produce opposite prose: the first is dense with consequences that
  imply a cause; the second is gestures that imply nothing.
- **The diagnostic is the reader's question.** *"What is actually happening?"* is never
  a request for exposition - it is the reader reporting that the gestures do not add
  up. Do not answer it in the story. Fix the hollow.
- **The reader is owed consequences in exchange for the rule.** Something must be
  measurably different afterward: an object changed, a habit changed, a cost paid. In
  r5 nothing happened to the man in the pond - he was handed a knife and went home. A
  contact with no consequence is an anecdote.
- Record the rule in the vignette or story file as **"Rule (off-page)"**, above the
  prose, never inside it. If it cannot be written in one sentence, the story is not
  ready.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §3 amended; `vignette-bank.md` gains a
mandatory Rule field.
**Status:** active

### L-009 - Establish the frame before the strangeness
**Rule:** Name the ordinary activity plainly in the first sentence. The wrongness needs
a normal to be wrong against.
**Origin:** Vignette 5 r5, critique 06, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"I honestly have no idea what he is doing, I spent the entire time
trying to decode the hidden message of what this guy is doing with a scissors a bowl and
newspaper, instead of being engrossed in the story telling. Is he cleaning a fish?"*
**How to apply:**
- Say *beard*. Say *table*, *coffin*, *baby* - whatever the mundane frame is, in plain
  words, immediately. This is L-003 at scene scale.
- **Ambiguous props are noise, not clues.** Scissors + bowl + newspaper + good light
  describes cleaning a fish as well as it describes trimming a beard. A prop only works
  once the reader knows what activity it belongs to.
- **Withholding the frame is not suspense.** Suspense requires the reader to know what
  is at stake. A reader who is decoding is not afraid; they are working, and they will
  finish the paragraph having felt nothing.
- Ask of every opening: *could a careless reader name what this person is doing?* If
  not, rewrite until they can. The horror is never the frame - it is what the frame
  turns out to contain, and it cannot turn out to contain anything until it exists.
**First replication, 2026-09-10, story 03 movement one, basis 1 → 2. It arrived twice in
one movement, at two scales neither of which is the opening of a scene — so the rule is
not about openings, it is about ordering.**

**Clause 2 — the enabling fact goes before the image, never after.** An image whose force
depends on a fact the reader has to be holding — an age, a relation, a measurement, a
price — is inert until that fact is on the page, and supplying it afterwards does not
retroactively charge the image; it asks for a re-read that no reader performs. Movement
one gave the hat cut down from a child's hat, the child's shoes and the man's coat, and
put *he was nineteen that spring* in the paragraph after. Mark: *"mention that Ti-jean is
19 sooner so that the lines about him having a childs hat hit harder (without the age he
could just be a child)."* Without the age those three sentences describe a child being
dressed by a poor family, which is not strange at all. **The frame was missing and the
frame was one word long.** Test: for each image meant to land, name the fact it needs and
check it is already behind the reader.

**The rationalisation to distrust, added 2026-09-10 — this clause was broken in the same
draft that produced it.** Story 03's M3 has Elmire stand a rake against the plinth to see
whether she can; the norm that makes the act legible — every monument in that ground gets
used as a shelf — was in **M4**, a movement later. I noticed the ordering when I placed it
and argued myself past it on the grounds that the later passage would *retro-illuminate*
the earlier image. Mark: *"do we have the setup for this somewhere?"*
- **Readers do not re-read.** A fact supplied later does not reach back and charge an image
  the reader has already passed; it produces a second, milder recognition at best, and at
  worst the image simply reads as an odd act and is filed as one.
- **The tell, in my own reasoning:** any sentence of the form *"the reader will understand
  this once they reach X."* If X is later than the image, the image is inert. The only
  question is whether the enabling fact is **behind** the reader, and the answer is
  mechanical, not aesthetic.
- **Where the fact goes is a separate question from where the norm is loudest.** The fix
  here was one clause of scenery in M1 — *the copings take the buckets, and the rakes lean
  on the crosses* — which leaves M4's fuller catalogue in place as the same habit at full
  volume on the one day the ground is crowded. Establishing early and amplifying later is
  free; the reverse is not.

**Clause 3 — the space is a frame too, and a destination must be on the map before
anybody walks to it.** Same movement: the figure stood on the plot beside Elmire's, and
Ti-Jean broke away *up the middle of the shell path*, which pointed at nothing. Mark: *"I
wonder if it's clear enough that Ti-Jean is trying to get to the figure."* The prose was
not vague — the geography was wrong, and no sentence-level fix would have worked. Where a
character's **movement** is the event, the reader must be able to draw the ground first:
lay the path, say where it ends, put the thing at the end of it, and then let somebody go
up it. And once the map is right, **name the destination flatly anyway** (L-003): *going
for the figure at the top of it*, not *with his eyes up*.
**Status:** active

### L-010 - Put the wrongness in the approach
**Rule:** Signal before contact. The passage leading to the impossible carries the
concrete detail; the contact itself stays thin.
**Origin:** Vignette 3 r5, critique 06, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"The description of the water doesn't include any feelings that
something is amiss."*
**How to apply:**
- This is `influences/james-rules.md` §3 - *most concrete in the approach, least
  concrete in the touch* - and r5 had it exactly backwards: a flat approach and a
  heavily rendered touch.
- **Prefer absence-signals to presence-signals.** *"Nothing moved away from him when he
  came in. A pond that size, you put a leg in and the whole of it goes somewhere
  else."* An ordinary thing that fails to happen is more disturbing and far cheaper
  than an extraordinary thing that does, and it does not spend the story's one
  impossible thing.
- One or two signals, placed early, unremarked by the character. He notices; he does
  not interpret.
**Status:** active

### L-011 - Polysyndeton is an instrument, not a default
**Rule:** Vary how long sentences are made long. Chained *and* is for accumulation, once
or twice a passage.
**Origin:** Vignette 4 r5, critique 06, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"Sometimes the sentences seem short and awkward though, they don't
seem to flow. lot's of fragments that start with 'and'."*
**How to apply:**
- **The technique is sound and period-correct** - chained *and* is genuine to
  nineteenth-century vernacular, the KJV-inflected Southern register, and Twain through
  McCarthy. Do not remove it on principle; the "fragments" are in fact complete
  clauses, and the objection is to frequency, not grammar.
- **The failure is monotony of construction, not of length.** `ai-tells.md` §B warns
  about uniform sentence *length*; this is uniform sentence *architecture*. When every
  long sentence is a chain, the passage has two speeds and no way to mark which clause
  matters - every element arrives at the same weight, which flattens exactly the beats
  that need to land.
- Extend sentences by other means as well: subordination, apposition, a colon, a full
  stop where an *and* was. Then keep one chain and put it where accumulation is the
  point.
**Consequences:** `ai-tells.md` §B "Rhythmic uniformity" extended to construction.
**Status:** active

### L-012 - Choose the event; do not arrive at it
**Rule:** Decide what happens before writing how it happens, and test the candidate
against four questions. Execution quality cannot rescue an unchosen event.
**Origin:** Vignette 3 batch 02 ("The wake"), critique 07, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"I really like the setup, but it was a little bit of a let down
when basically a ghost combed his hair... I think a part of this process will be coming
up with potential things that could happen in the story and whether that thing is scary
or unsettling."* And, on the eighteen alternatives: *"all of these are better than the
original, which tells me that even pointing out that this should be something we focus
attention on improves the result."*
**How to apply:**
- **The finding is about attention, not about ghosts.** Naming the event as a thing to
  be worked on produced eighteen candidates that all beat the default. Any element that
  is never made an explicit object of attention will be filled with the modal answer,
  written well. Applies to endings, names, objects, weather, and the last line as much
  as to the haunting.
- **The four tests.** An event earns the page only if all four pass:
  1. **Does it touch the living?** Decorating the dead, the house or the past is
     scenery. A ghost that tidies has no appetite.
  2. **Does it implicate the witness?** `craft-principles.md` §6 is notice → intrude →
     implicate; defaults stop at intrude. Is she worse off than before?
  3. **Does it imply more than itself?** A complete, fully picturable event is a closed
     loop. The frightening version leaves a residue - a second night, an appetite that
     is not finished.
     **Boundary, added 2026-09-08 (L-016).** *Residue is not system.* This test is the
     most dangerous of the four because "implies more" reads as "has machinery behind
     it", and it will happily pass a candidate whose residue is a rule set, a tally or a
     schedule. Those imply a mechanism, and a mechanism is solvable. **A residue is
     something left over; a system is something that adds up.** Cut "a schedule" from
     the original wording for exactly that reason. Test the residue: can the reader
     *diagram* it? Then it failed.
  4. **Does it change her future?** If the horror ends when the paragraph does, it was
     an anecdote. (L-006 then prices the change.)
- *"Somebody had combed his hair"* scored nought of four while being some of the
  cleanest prose in the batch. That is the whole lesson.
**Consequences:** `craft/invention-protocol.md` created; `revision-protocol.md` gains
step 0; `ai-tells.md` gains §F.
**Status:** active

### L-013 - Defeat mode collapse structurally; the model does not get to pick
**Rule:** Generate long, quota by category, discard the head of the list, seed from
outside the genre - and hand selection to Mark or to position, never to my own ranking.
**Origin:** Mark's process note, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** *"Another thing that I think will be invaluable in our process is creating
lists of things like this, and taking items further down the list to avoid the RL mode
collapse of LLM's and their propensity to do the same thing if not prompted otherwise."*
**How to apply:**
- **The ranker is as collapsed as the generator.** Producing twenty candidates and then
  recommending three re-applies the prior the list was built to escape: the list widens,
  the pick narrows it straight back to the middle. Presenting a shortlist as the
  deliverable is the relapse, not the remedy.
- **Selection order of preference:** (1) Mark picks; (2) by position, default 8+;
  (3) if I must, take the lowest-ranked candidate that passes L-012's four tests, not
  the highest.
- **Twenty minimum, discard the first five unread.** The obvious runs out somewhere past
  item eight. A genuinely right answer in the discarded band is re-derivable; defaulting
  to it is not recoverable.
- **Quota by category, and choose the categories by who is affected** - not by mood.
  Variance came from the six-axis structure of the wake list, not from any instruction
  to be original.
  **Amended 2026-09-08: the quota can manufacture pseudo-variance.** Story 01's pool ran
  seven who-is-affected categories and Mark read four of them - D, E, F, G, twelve
  candidates - as *"more of the same"*. They were: the same over-systematised idea
  distributed across seven headings, which the category structure disguised rather than
  exposed. **The quota guarantees spread across the axis, not across the idea.** Before
  handing over a pool, name the underlying idea of each candidate in three words and
  count the duplicates; twenty-four candidates covering four ideas is a list of four.
  **The audit failed on its first use, same day.** Story 01's ending pool ran the check,
  reported *"seven distinct ideas, ratio is honest"*, and was then beaten outright by an
  ending Mark wrote himself - because all eighteen candidates relocated an image onto a
  **body**, and I had audited the images rather than the move underneath them. Mark's put
  the child into the game alive instead. **Audit the move, not its furniture:** ask what
  the reader is made to do, not what the candidate is about. Seven images serving one
  move is still a list of one.
- **Boundary - this rule governs invention, not critique triage. Added 2026-09-09,
  Mark's instruction.** Handed nine ruled-on items from a fresh-context review, Mark:
  *"I think I will take your recommendation for all the decisions. Let's just write draft 4
  as you see fit using this critique. I feel I work better when you make the decisions,
  then I can read it and give my notes based on intuition."*
  - **The two acts are not the same act.** Selecting an *event* is a draw from a
    distribution, and the ranker is as collapsed as the generator - which is the whole
    reason for this rule. Triaging a critique is **argument against evidence**: each item
    carries a doctrine citation, a word cost and a named risk, and the calls are checkable
    rather than preferred. Handing them over to be arbitrated spends Mark's attention on
    work that is already done, and he reads prose better than he reads a decision table.
  - **What is owed instead:** the argument in writing before drafting, every decline named
    as a decline with the rule it rests on, and the tripwire that would reverse it. Then
    draft.
  - **What this does not release.** L-013 stands unchanged wherever the choice is
    generative - events, endings, images, names, a complicity pool. Those still go over
    long and unranked. **The tell that this boundary is being abused:** a "triage" whose
    items are alternatives rather than verdicts.
- **Seed from `reference/period/`.** A real object, custom or belief picked *without
  regard to whether it is spooky* is the strongest decorrelator available, because it
  draws from outside the horror-fiction distribution entirely.
- **Write the reader's three guesses first and ban them.** Converts the prior into an
  exclusion list for the price of one line.
- **State the limit honestly:** I cannot sample independently of myself. Two "separate"
  generations in one context are correlated, my sense of what is unusual is unreliable,
  and my randomness is not random. The mechanical steps carry this lesson; my
  self-diversification is a prior, not a control.
**Consequences:** `craft/invention-protocol.md` Part 2 and Part 3 (spent-events log).
**Status:** active

### L-014 - The doctrine is itself a collapse vector
**Rule:** Ration ending shapes the way events are rationed, and require every piece to
break one rule deliberately and say which.
**Origin:** Batch 03 (Fable) read against batches 01-02, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"Check out the fable batch entries which were written by a more
powerful LLM. To my eye I don't notice a massive improvement. I think in some ways I
like the stories a bit less."*
**How to apply:**
- **The finding.** Batch 03 was written by a stronger model under thirteen lessons and a
  full invention protocol, and reads as *smoother and less surprising* than batch 01,
  which was written under two or three. A rule followed faithfully by a more capable
  model produces more uniform output, not better output. Capability raises the floor and
  the doctrine lowers the ceiling, and the two meet in the middle.
- **The visible symptom is the audit.** When the rubric can be reverse-engineered from
  the prose - one non-tactile register per rendering, no repeats, an accommodation
  ending, a quotable line per piece - the piece has become an exam answer. Batch 03's
  own pre-flight note reports "no two renderings in one vignette share a register",
  which is a spreadsheet result, not a reason anything is frightening.
- **Ending shapes are rationed.** `craft-principles.md` §7 lists three. No more than one
  in three consecutive pieces uses the same one. L-006's accommodation ending is the
  current offender: five of six in batch 01 r5, four of six in batch 03. **A rule that
  fires every time has stopped being a rule and become a shape.**
- **Do not fix this with another hard rule.** The first version of this lesson required
  *"every piece breaks one rule and says which"*, which Mark's next note correctly
  killed: a mandatory deviation is still a rubric item, and it would have been satisfied
  by breaking something trivial. Replaced by the three mechanisms in **The status of
  these rules** above - kind, basis, and the deviation log. Deviation is permitted
  wherever it works and recorded when it happens; it is never scheduled.
- **Corollary for reading critiques.** The best lines in this project - *"her back had
  never been so much of her before"*, *"the way you would give a man his change"* - were
  discoveries that the rules were written to explain **afterward**. Rules preserve
  discoveries; they do not produce them. Treat every lesson as a floor, never a target.
**Consequences:** `invention-protocol.md` Part 5; `ai-tells.md` §D extended;
`lessons-learned.md` gains the rule-status section and `craft/deviations.md`.
**Status:** active

### L-015 - Grow stories from vignettes; do not outline first
**Rule:** Build a story outward from tested paragraphs. Write the outline last, if at
all.
**Origin:** Mark's process note, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** *"I was starting to get ideas for stories while reading the short
paragraphs. The way to write these stories might be to start from snippets like these,
get ideas, and slowly build up like that, instead of trying to come out with the outline
for a story all at once."*
**How to apply:**
- **An outline is where collapse happens first.** The model's plan for a ghost story is
  the modal ghost story, produced before a word is drafted; every well-executed scene
  afterward then serves a generic shape. An outline is also cheap to make and expensive
  to abandon, so the first one survives by default.
- **The signal to grow from is appetite, not quality.** Which vignette makes the reader
  want to know what happened on either side of it - a different question from which is
  best written, and one only Mark can answer. Ask for it explicitly at each scoring
  round.
- **Grow by question.** What had to be true before this? What does it cost tomorrow?
  Each answer is another vignette, run through the same tests.
- **Fill the bible ledger progressively.** An honest TBD beats a guess that then
  constrains what the story could have become.
**Consequences:** `invention-protocol.md` Part 4.
**Status:** active

### L-016 - No mechanism behind the thing; coherence is not a horror value

**Basis 4, added 2026-09-09: a consistency demonstrated at scale is a mechanism.** Mark, on a
graveside scene in which twenty mourners all avoided the same side of a grave: *"This goes too
far, almost to the point of comedy. People avoiding going near this thing like magnets with the
same polarity... The avoidance works because it's subtle, because it could be overridden in
principle, but never is. Testing this by forcing 40 people near it and them all still resisting
makes it seem more like a supernatural force field."*

A felt rule survives only while it is **one person at a time, deniable, and overridable in
principle** — walking a little faster and not slowing down, because slowing down would be a
decision. Put a crowd in one place and have all of them obey at once, observably, and the
behaviour stops being a feeling in people and becomes a **property of the object**, which is a
rule, which is a mechanism. The scene proves the thing works, and a thing that demonstrably
works can be described.

**The fix is Mark's and it inverts the instrument: establish the rule by breaking it.** Give it
somebody who does not feel it — young, or different, or simply outside the code — and let them
walk up and touch the face. Their exception proves everyone else's avoidance without a sentence
ever asserting it, and it locates the dread **in the parish rather than in the object**, which
is where the ambiguity needs it. Worked in `bible/plate-explorations/attitude-of-grief-prose-02.md`.

**Rule:** The event is a thing that happens, not a system that explains itself. Give the
haunting no rule set, no arithmetic, no score-keeping and no internal consistency beyond
what one sentence of appetite requires.
**Origin:** Story 01 event pool, Mark's selection, 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark, rejecting categories D, E, F and G of a 24-candidate pool outright:
*"I think this is getting too technical, the game doesn't matter, they're playing tag...
we don't need in depth specific about how it's played. If its a game for kids the rules
would be nonsense anyways, the whole point is just that there is a strange entity out
there with them, that we don't know until she counts."* And the general form: *"A horror
story doesn't need this level of internal consistency and plot coherency. This isn't a
mystery novel where you solve a mystery and in the end all the pieces come together."*
**How to apply:**
- **The failure is not bad candidates; it is a productive test misfiring.** L-012's
  third test - *does it imply something larger than itself?* - was satisfied by eight
  candidates that implied a **system**: the rule changes and improves, something is
  keeping score, the linen count reconciles, the flyleaf gets a new line. Each of those
  reads as a mechanism with visible workings, and a mechanism is solvable. See the
  boundary added to L-012 below.
- **Elaborating the interior of the frame is the specific tell.** The children's game is
  the *frame* (L-009), and a frame's job is to be immediately recognisable and then
  contain something. Tag. Once I began specifying its rules, its correctness, its
  score, the game stopped being furniture and became plot, and the reader starts
  solving instead of watching.
- **Nonsense is period-correct and it is free.** Children's games have arbitrary rules
  that adults cannot follow, so a thing keeping to them tells the reader nothing it can
  work with, which is exactly the desired condition. Consistency here buys nothing and
  costs the dread.
- **One arithmetic, once.** Six in the field and five at the table. That is the entire
  device: *"the whole idea is that she sees six in the field and counts 5 meaning one of
  them wasn't real... that's it."* Repeating the count weekly, writing it down, or
  reconciling it against the linen converts a single cold fact into a running subplot.
  The reader needs the fact one time.
- **No provenance either. Added 2026-09-09, basis 1 → 2 — first independent replication
  of this rule.** Draft 02 gave the game's rule a teacher: *"Alma had taught her the rule
  herself, on the step, the first week she was up, because at the Ledoux place they played
  it some other way."* It was written to implicate her (L-021) and Mark cut it: *"I don't
  think Alma should teach the rule in the beginning. I want this to be something that the
  kids just came up with themselves. It doesn't need any explanation."*
  - **The rule already banned the game's interior; this bans its history.** Specifying
    where a child's game came from is the same move as specifying how it is scored, one
    step further back. A game that arose on its own among children is unownable, and
    therefore nothing the thing keeping to it can be traced through.
  - **Watch for this arriving as a service to another rule.** The sentence was not written
    out of a wish to explain — it was the cheapest way to make Alma complicit in the rule
    that kills the child. **A prohibition does not weaken because a different lesson wants
    the space.** Where L-021 and L-016 both apply, take the complicity from the domestic
    material — the boots, the pot, the wagon, the letters — never from the haunting's
    furniture.
  - **General form:** origins, first occasions and how-it-started are mechanism. If the
    reader can say where any part of this came from, that part has a history, and a history
    can be followed.
- **The unfollowed consequence. Added 2026-09-09, basis 2 → 3.** I recommended cutting
  *"There is no stone for Emmy Ledoux"* from the tree ending on the grounds that a
  recovered body implies a grave. Mark: *"I think 'no stone' can stay. It opens the
  possibility that they all went insane and never buried her. Perhaps the property was
  abandoned. The specifics don't matter, but for some reason having a body and it never
  being buried is deeply unsettling."*
  - **The error was checking the line for consistency instead of asking what the new fact
    made it mean.** With no body, the sentence reports a failed search. With a body in the
    tree, the same nine words say nobody ever got her down — and neither the story nor the
    reader is told why. **An event's consequences do not have to be tracked.** A statement
    that has become impossible to account for is frequently the strongest thing on the page.
  - **Procedure, and it is cheap:** before cutting a line because a new event contradicts
    it, write down what the new event makes the line mean. Do that before reaching for the
    edit, because the edit will present itself as tidying.
  - **The class of horror this belongs to: the observance not performed.** Not injury to
    the body — the *absence of the ordinary thing done with* one. A body nobody buries, a
    grave nobody digs, a name nobody says. Cheaper than gore and it does not spend D-003.
  - **Third instance in one thread of the same standing tendency** (L-018): the game's
    rules, the game's origin, and now the plot's bookkeeping. Every correction Mark has
    made on this story runs the same direction, and every one of my proposals ran the
    other. **When an instinct says "but then this wouldn't add up", that is the instinct
    to distrust.**
- **The diagnostic.** If I can draw the haunting as a diagram with arrows, it has become
  a mechanism. If the strongest thing I can say about it is *there was something out
  there with them*, it is still a haunting.
- **Companion to D-004, from the other side.** D-004 forbids the story from having a
  thesis; L-016 forbids it from having a mechanism. A stated cause and an inferable
  system are the same failure at different volumes.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §3 first paragraph withdrawn ("logic the reader
can *almost* infer") and §4 rewritten from "The haunt must mean something" to "must have
an effect"; L-012 gains the residue boundary; L-013 gains the pseudo-variance note.
**Status:** active

### L-017 - End by relocating a banked image, not by delivering information
**Rule:** Describe something about the thing early and plainly, let it read as scenery,
then in the last sentence let that same description come true of a person. The ending
delivers no new fact; it moves an old one.
**Origin:** Mark, 2026-09-08, naming an exemplar for story 01's ending.
**Evidence:** *"A good example of an ending would be 'the thing' in scary stories to
tell in the dark where children see a horrible looking creature on a walk that grabs one
of them by the arm. The child later gets sick and the story ends with the line 'at the
end, he looked just like the creature'. Something impactful like that. But take the
essence of this, not this literal example."*
**How to apply:**
- **The transferable move is the relocation, not the resemblance.** *The victim becomes
  the monster* is one instance and it will collapse into a shape within three stories if
  it is read as the rule (L-014). The move is: **an image the reader already holds
  arrives in a person.** A posture, a rate of movement, a colour, a measurement, a
  silence, a habit - anything previously attached to the thing and later true of someone
  who matters.
- **Bank early, unremarked.** The image must be specific enough to be recognised
  forty paragraphs later and must have looked like furniture when it was laid down. If
  the reader senses it being planted, the ending becomes a trick they saw coming. This
  is L-010's approach-detail doing double duty.
- **Summarise the decline; never dramatise it.** In the exemplar the illness and death
  occupy a sentence or two of report. A death scene spends the ending's charge in
  advance and puts a crisis where the flatness needs to be.
- **The last sentence is the flattest in the story.** Short, declarative, no simile, no
  adjective doing work, no interiority. `craft-principles.md` §11 already says the worst
  image gets the evenest sentence; this is the same instrument at the ending.
- **A witness must have seen both ends.** The comparison exists in somebody's head, and
  that person now carries it and cannot say it to anyone. Route the last line through
  the POV character noticing, not through a narrator informing.
- **It answers nothing, and that is why it lands.** No account of what the thing was or
  why contact did this. Compatible with D-004 and L-016 by construction: a relocated
  image cannot be diagrammed, so there is nothing for the reader to solve.
- **One contact fact is not a mechanism.** The exemplar has the creature grab a boy by
  the arm and the boy sickens. That is a single fact of contact, permitted. *Rules
  about* contact - what it can take, what must be offered, what calls it in - are the
  mechanism L-016 bans.
**Two clauses added 2026-09-08, from Mark's verdict on story 01's first ending (basis
→ 2):**
- **Say it plainly. A refrain beats an inference.** The rejected ending closed on *"The
  sun does not come off that yard until five"* — which required the reader to recall an
  image from movement one, notice the protagonist standing where the figure had stood,
  and infer a resemblance. Three unstated steps. **Restraint at the last line becomes
  withholding**, and withholding is what L-003 forbids. Mark: *"the ending should
  definitely be 'because that was the rule'. That line was said many times in this story
  and I think it's impactful at the end."* The strongest last line is a **phrase the
  reader already has**, returning on a new subject — establish it, deepen it, detonate
  it, three strikes and no more. An earned refrain lands where an elegant inference does
  not.
- **Fear, not pity, and the two are mutually exclusive.** If the last beat asks the
  reader to feel sorry for the protagonist, it cannot frighten them. Mark: *"we should
  definitely be afraid of her at this point."* What converts pity into fear:
  - **Competence.** She performs the terrible thing *correctly* — slowly, precisely, to
    the rule. Incoherence is pitiable; method is frightening. A raving widow is stock; a
    woman who **hems the eyeholes because a cut edge in linen will run** is not.
  - **Preparation.** Something bought, cut, measured, kept. A deliberate act with a tool
    in her hand, rendered as plain domestic work, and never explained.
  - **Withdrawn interiority — here it is correct** (L-006's proper home). By the last
    beat she must be **opaque**, exactly as the thing was opaque. The reader loses access
    to her, and that loss is the horror.
  - **Somebody in the story is afraid of her.** It licenses the reader. Best delivered by
    inverted authority: a man standing in the door of his own kitchen waiting to be told.
  - **Render the state from outside, on the face, and let someone fail to reach her.**
    Mark, 2026-09-08: *"we should put more emphasis on her mental state, not from the
    inside, but what she looked like while doing this... perhaps her husband tries to talk
    to her to no avail."* The instrument is a **failed attempt at contact**: a person she
    loves says her name, takes hold of her, and gets nothing — and the reader watches him
    give up and stand out of her way. Give the observer one or two plain facts about her
    body and no more: what her eyes are doing and what they are not on; whether her mouth
    is shut; whether she is out of breath when she should be. **No clinical word may
    appear** — *catatonic*, *unresponsive*, *vacant* are the modern interior
    (`anachronism-traps.md` § Psychological), and the point is what a husband in 1878 can
    see, not what a doctor in 1970 would call it. Her not resisting is worse than her
    resisting.
**Ceiling, written before this has ever fired:** `Fired: 0`, `Basis: 1`. This is a
**prescription** and it is the exact profile of L-006 on the day L-006 was written - one
line Mark liked, generalised into a move. L-006 then ended nine of twelve pieces.
Ration this from the start: §7 has three ending shapes and no more than one in three
consecutive pieces may use the same one.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §7 - the *recontextualization* shape gains an
execution mechanism, which it did not previously have.
**Status:** active

### L-018 - Register the impossible before going flat; kill the mundane explanation on the page
**Rule:** An impossible event earns a calm sentence only *after* the reader has felt it
land. Render the body, then let the character hunt for who did it, then let her run out
of candidates in full view of the reader - and only then withdraw.
**Origin:** Story 01 draft 01, movement two, critique 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark on the arms of a laid-out child going back out to the sides: *"I think
more attention should be paid to the arms being out to the sides and her putting them
back down. She either needs to be unsettled by this, or it should be implied to the
reader that she thinks someone else did it. In this case the reader must know that there
is no way anyone could have done it (no real person)."*
**How to apply:**
- **The diagnosis: flatness is an *ending* instrument and I used it mid-story.** L-006
  says withdraw interiority for the last beat, because a character who has stopped
  having a crisis proves the haunting is a settled condition. At a mid-story event there
  is no settled condition yet, and the same move does not read as cold - it reads as
  *elided*. The passage delivered the worst physical fact in the draft in a subordinate
  clause and then said *"there was a good deal else"*, which is the prose declining the
  event on the reader's behalf.
- **Second clause: the natural explanation has to die where the reader can see it.** An
  impossible event is only impossible once the possible version is gone. Narrating *there
  was nobody there* does not do it; the character eliminating people does, because she is
  not trying to prove a haunting - she wants to know who handled her child. **The reader
  believes an exclusion she performed for her own reasons.** She stops when she runs out
  of people, not when she reaches a conclusion.
- **The exclusion is one question to one person. Never a survey.**
  **Amended 2026-09-08, hours after this rule was written, because I broke it in the same
  turn I wrote it.** The first version said *keep the elimination domestic, never
  forensic, three clauses at most* - and I then wrote a three-clause alibi survey
  (husband in the field, neighbour woman in sight of the window, children in the yard)
  and shipped it. Mark cut the whole paragraph: *"everything that precedes this paragraph
  does enough to explain the arms by the side, so if there's anything in this paragraph
  or anything after that is still trying to explain this, it isn't needed. Standing by
  the door, asking about it, that's good enough."*
  - **The clause count was never the variable.** A survey is narration; a question is
    drama. Ask the one person who could plausibly have done it, take their answer, and
    stop. Everyone else is the reader's business.
  - **Why the survey actively hurts.** It closes a gap the reader was enjoying closing.
    See L-006's good-gap boundary: a gap the reader *completes* is a pleasure, and one
    that is completed *for* them is a debit. The survey also carries an unspoken *I have
    checked, you may stop wondering* - which is L-004 arriving at scene scale.
  - **The tell.** Any sentence after the impossible fact whose job is to make the fact
    hold up. If a line exists to defend the event, cut it; the event does not need a
    lawyer.
- **Standing tendency, recorded because it is now the pattern.** Three consecutive
  critiques have cut my elaboration and none has asked for more: categories D/E/F/G of
  the event pool (mechanism), the ending pool (eighteen variants of one move), this
  survey (proof). **My failure mode is making things add up.** Mark's correction every
  time is to leave them not adding up. Same family as L-016; when in doubt, the shorter
  version with the hole in it is the one to ship.
- **Order, and it is not negotiable:** involuntary body → the knowing → the misfired
  action → the failed search → *then* flat. Skip any of the first four and the flat
  sentence has nothing to be flat about.
- **This is L-001 replicating.** L-001 already warns that *behaviour alone is
  under-rendering*, and the failed passage was pure behaviour - she folded them, she put
  them down, she stopped thinking about it. **First independent replication of a rule in
  this project**; L-001's basis moves 2 → 3.
**Consequences:** L-001 basis 2 → 3; L-006 gains a boundary confining withdrawn
interiority to the final beat; `craft-principles.md` §11 "keep the prose calm" gains the
ordering requirement.
**Status:** active

### L-019 - Earn the accommodation; she fights, and the ordinary world beats her
**Rule:** Before a character adapts to a haunting, she must do what a real person would
do - loudly, more than once - and be defeated by the period, the household and the
neighbours. Never by the thing.
**Origin:** Story 01 draft 01, movement three, critique 2026-09-08.
**Evidence:** Mark: *"immediately something feels off about this part. This woman already
noticed this strange figure which initially she thought was her husband but was unsure,
she knows that it disappears when she pays attention to it, and that her daughter
recently died, but then she's just casually watching it play with her children? We need a
realistic scenario because any reasonable person in this scenario would be freaking out,
screaming at it and chasing it out of the yard."*
**How to apply:**
- **The diagnosis: I reached for the accommodation two beats early.** `craft-principles.md`
  §6 says *"the character begins to accommodate it - which is the true horror: people
  adapt"*, and L-006 prices that adaptation. Both are correct and both presuppose a
  fight. **Adaptation without a fight is not adaptation, it is passivity, and passivity
  reads as the author needing the character to stay put.** The reader does not think *how
  chilling*; they think *why is she still sitting there*.
- **The mechanism must be the world, never the haunting.** If the thing stops her acting,
  it has acquired a rule and a reach, which is L-016. If **1878** stops her, that is
  history doing the work for free. In story 01: germ theory is not general, the
  explanatory frame is bad air, and a shut hot house in August is understood by everyone
  in the parish to be how children take the fever. Her husband says it, the neighbour
  woman says it, the doctor has said it in front of six people. **Letting the children out
  is the correct, loving, medically endorsed thing to do**, and it kills them.
- **Make her fight with the wrong weapon.** The true argument - *there is a tall thing in
  my yard* - is unavailable, because she made it once at the top of her voice in front of
  her children and her husband walked the yard with a lamp and a gun and then was kind to
  her all week. So she must argue the fever, which she does not believe, against three
  people who do, and lose on those grounds. **A character who cannot state her real
  reason is trapped without anything supernatural having to trap her.**
- **The cost of acting is her standing, and that is what closes the option.** Not
  futility - *humiliation*. She can go at the tree again any time she likes. What stops
  her is that the last time, Wick looked up at her with a sheet over his arm and Emmy put
  her hand over her mouth. Once a character's reasonable action has made her the thing
  that is wrong with the household, she stops, and the reader never asks why again.
- **Refuse the modern interior.** *Paralysed with fear*, *depressed*, *traumatised* were
  offered as options and all three are concept anachronisms - see
  `reference/period/anachronism-traps.md` § Psychological, *"the largest single risk, and
  exactly the vocabulary an AI drafts in by default."* The period-true state is not a
  diagnosis. She is a woman who has been overruled by everyone who loves her, on grounds
  she cannot argue with, and who has nothing left but to watch. Render that as
  arrangements - which end of the gallery she does the wash at - never as a condition.
- **It applies to every character, not just the protagonist, and reaction *time* is part
  of it.** Added 2026-09-08. Story 01 had the husband first go out to his wife on *"the
  third day"* of her circling a tree in a cut sheet. Mark: *"him waiting 3 days to check
  on her is crazy. he should go after her almost immediately unless you can think of a
  good reason why not."* There was none that survived: he is in the cane before light and
  back after dark, so he would genuinely miss a two o'clock — but she cut the sheet on a
  **Sunday**, when no man in that parish is in a field. **The delay existed because I
  needed him absent, and a reader feels that as the plot moving a person.** Test every
  gap between an event and a reaction: is the delay the character's, or mine? If a
  plausible reason has to be constructed after the fact, there isn't one.
  Immediacy also paid better — he intervenes while the sheet is new and he still believes
  it can be stopped, so his hope is intact when it fails.
- **The precondition is per character, and it is spent once. Added 2026-09-09.** For the
  tree ending I proposed giving Hollis a failed attempt on the tree and two ordinary
  reasons for abandoning it, because this rule says an accommodation must be bought. Mark:
  *"Hollis sitting on the gallery isn't a decision, it's a place he is forced to out of
  mental exhaustion. He is unable to think or act, or do anything but sit and watch in
  horror."*
  - **He had just bought it.** In the same scene he goes off the gallery after his wife,
    says her name five times, takes her arm, puts both hands against her, gives ground the
    whole way round the tree, shouts her name and her mother's and the names of three
    buried children where the road can hear, and loses. **A second defeat in the same
    scene is this rule fired at the wrong beat** — the exact mirror of L-018's error, and
    it reads as the author topping up a character who is already empty.
  - **The test:** ask what this character has already tried *in this scene*, not in this
    story. If they have just lost, the collapse is paid for; write the collapse.
  - **Render it on the body and never as a state.** No clinical word (L-019 above; the
    modern interior is the largest single risk in the repo). What is available: **the verbs
    stop** — he gets as far as the steps *because that is as far as he got*; **an ordinary
    thing not done**; and best, **an earlier action of his own that does not recur.** He
    took the lamp and the gun round that yard for the better part of an hour in August;
    he does not get up for either of them now. The contrast renders the state without
    anybody naming it.
- **The order of construction.** Try → fail → try differently → be beaten by an
  unanswerable ordinary argument → *then* the arrangement. Only after that does L-006's
  price mean anything, because only then does the reader know what she is buying.
**Consequences:** `craft-principles.md` §6 beat 2 gains the precondition; `ai-tells.md`
§D gains "the uncannily calm protagonist".
**Status:** active

### L-020 - Review in a fresh context, with the same model, before Mark sees it
**Rule:** Run every finished draft past the same model in a clean context, holding the
story and nothing else. It is a real stage of the pipeline, not a formality.
**Origin:** Mark's process note, 2026-09-09, correcting me on story 01's review.
**Evidence:** I received `review-of-draft1.txt`, called its best notes *"ones I couldn't
have generated"*, and Mark: *"the review was generated by Opus 5 which is you, the same
model that wrote it so you absolutely could have generated the notes, probably in a fresh
context. This is important to note going forward that LLM review steps are still
useful."*
**How to apply:**
- **The correction matters more than the compliment.** I did not generate those notes; I
  *could* have. Framing a miss as a capability limit rather than a **context** limit
  quietly excuses it, and the excuse would have stopped this becoming a pipeline stage.
- **Why the writing context cannot see it.** Four reasons, all structural rather than
  about ability:
  1. **Commitment.** Every beat in the draft is one I chose and then defended. Emmy's
     letters ran the exculpating direction *because I decided that*, and having decided
     it I stopped seeing it as a decision.
  2. **Accumulated justification.** By round eleven there is a reason for everything. A
     clean context has no reasons, only the text.
  3. **Objective.** Writing optimises *make this work*. Reading optimises *what is wrong
     with this*. Same weights, opposite gradient.
  4. **Doctrine contamination — the important one.** My context held the whole craft
     folder, so I read the story *through* the rules and saw compliance. The reviewer,
     given only the prose, saw the symmetry as a **reader** and diagnosed L-014's rubric
     tell without knowing a rubric existed. **Withholding the craft folder from the
     reviewer is the load-bearing design choice**; a reviewer holding it grades an exam.
- **The retrospective is proof that self-knowledge does not transfer.** I had already
  written *"the failure mode is making things add up"* and *"every critique was
  structural"* — and still could not apply either to the draft in front of me. Seeing a
  pattern in one's past output is a different act from seeing it in one's present output.
- **Placement.** Between Draft and Critique — `revision-protocol.md` step 1b. It is free
  relative to a round of Mark's attention, and its purpose is to stop his rounds being
  spent on things a fresh read catches.
- **Calibration, both ways.** The review also independently picked the same beat Mark
  responded to most (Hollis at the steps with his hand on Sam's shoulder), which is
  weak evidence that fresh-context reading tracks his taste. But four of seven notes were
  actionable, one was wrong for this story, one was collection-level. **Useful, not an
  oracle.**
- **Fifth structural reason, added 2026-09-09: vacancy.** The four reasons above all
  concern material the writing context *chose* and then defended. Draft 03's review found
  something none of them covers - an entire register never raised at all: three children
  dead in a Catholic parish, in a story full of Ledoux, Thibault, Rey and Bertrand, turning
  on a laying-out, with no priest, no rite, no grace and no scripture anywhere in it.
  **The writing context can audit what it decided; it cannot audit what it never
  considered.** A clean reader arrives holding the world's ordinary furniture and notices
  the missing piece. Expect **absences rather than errors** to be the highest-value class
  of note from this stage, and expect them to be invisible from inside.
- **Second calibration, draft 03.** Nine items: six taken whole or in part, three declined
  on D-004 and L-016 grounds, and two faults found that the review missed - a joined
  cause-and-cost in the wash-bench sentence (L-006) and the game's interior specified to
  justify a plot beat (L-016). Consistent with round one's *useful, not an oracle*, with
  one addition worth holding: **the declines clustered on the review's single most
  confident idea**, the one it called "the whole idea" and told the reviser to read twice.
  A review's confidence does not track its correctness, because it holds no standing
  decisions - it cannot know which of its good ideas has already been ruled out.
- **Run it per movement, not per draft. Added 2026-09-10, story 03.** Mark ran it on two
  finished movements — 1,800 words, no ending, nothing to judge as a whole — and it returned
  a structural ordering fault (the violation and the norm it violates sitting in different
  sections) while the repair was still **twelve words**. The same fault found at 4,500 words
  costs a round, and by then the material around it has accumulated justification, which is
  reason 2 above operating on my own draft.
  - **Placement above is now a floor, not a schedule.** Step 1b says *between Draft and
    Critique*; that is where it is **owed**, not the only place it pays. A movement handed
    over one at a time is a free review point, and the notes arrive while the prose is still
    cheap to move.
  - **What it cannot do at movement scale, and this is what stops it being free:** it cannot
    assess an ending, a price, or whether the reader can answer *what was it* — the tests
    that matter most are tests on a whole story. Expect **ordering and legibility** notes
    here and structural ones later, and do not let an early clean review stand in for the
    one owed on the finished draft.
- **Authority is unchanged.** A review produces candidate notes. Aesthetic rulings remain
  Mark's, and nothing from a review enters this file on its own — *this* entry is here
  because Mark's process note is the source, not the review.
- **Third calibration, story 03 movements 1–2.** Six notes: one taken (adjacency, twelve
  words), two declined, one kept as a reading rather than an edit, one unplanted implication
  left alone, one process finding. **The pattern from draft 03 replicated exactly:** the
  single note the review was most confident about — make the footprint census land, since it
  is *"the hardest evidence in the story"* — is the one barred outright, because hard evidence
  that an avoidance works is L-016's mechanism arriving as physics. A review holds no standing
  decisions and cannot know which of its good ideas has already been ruled out. Its best note
  was its most modest one, and it said so itself: *"I wouldn't say more than that."*
**Consequences:** `revision-protocol.md` gains step 1b; `stories/01-the-game/critique-02.md`
records the first triage.
**Status:** active

### L-021 — Do not build the protagonist an alibi
**Rule:** Where the story's worst thought belongs to the point-of-view character, the plot
must not arrange her innocence. Remove whatever makes the harm nobody's fault, and let her
be implicated in the thing she noticed.
**Origin:** Story 01, Mark, 2026-09-09, on the Emmy Ledoux thread.
**Evidence:** *"That is the most damning thing in the story and I think the story then
protects her from it. As written, Alma repeatedly tries to send Emmy home and is refused,
so the child staying is nobody's fault."* And, on the pool he asked for: *"Do not assume
she must remain sympathetic. Assume she does not."*
**How to apply:**
- **The tell is a refusal she did not have to make.** When the world says no on her behalf
  — a letter that comes back agreeing, a relative with no room, a doctor who is away, an
  answer that is *"the only answer there was"* — check who is being protected. Foreclosure
  that arrives from outside is how L-019 keeps a character credible when she is **trying**;
  the same device applied to a thing she wants is an alibi, and the reader can feel the
  difference even when they cannot name it.
- **The two directions look identical on the page.** In story 01 the same instrument — a
  correspondence with a mother who cannot take her child back — ran twice: once so Alma
  could not be blamed, once so she could. Only the direction differed. So the question to
  ask of every closed option is not *is this plausible* but **whose interest does the
  closure serve.**
- **Sympathy for the POV character is a collapse vector, and it is invisible.** The draft
  had eleven rounds of critique on it and this went unnoticed because keeping her likeable
  was never a decision I made — it was the default I wrote from. Same family as L-014:
  a prior operating below the level the rules inspect. Ask directly, once per draft:
  **what does this story decline to let its protagonist be?**
- **Complicity is carried by omission and by deniable acts, not by villainy.** The
  strongest register is what a court would not touch: a letter shaped to get the answer
  she wants and every word of it true; the child's boots in a press she has no reason to
  open; the quick one sent out to the pot; a wagon with room in it and nobody, including
  her, saying the name. A protagonist who does something monstrous supplies a motive, and
  a motive is a mechanism (L-016). One who does nothing wrong and is guilty anyway
  supplies nothing to solve.
- **Do not let the complicity add up either.** The standing tendency in L-018 applies with
  full force here: a complete, consistent chain of guilt is a diagram, and it converts the
  horror into an argument the reader can check. Leave the thread lopsided — some real
  attempts, unrewarded; some omissions unremarked; no beat where the story totals them.
  **Never write the sentence in which she understands what she has done.**
- **It answers the visible-architecture note, and it is the only honest way to.**
  `critique-02.md` Tier 3a asked for the symmetry to be broken *somewhere on purpose*,
  which L-014 forbids as a scheduled deviation. A thread that runs long because it deserves
  to is asymmetry the story earned rather than owed.
- **Method note, from how the pool was requested.** Mark asked for *"at least fifteen,
  mildest to most damning, including ones you think go too far"* and for no
  recommendation — L-013 applied to a **revision** decision, not to invention. Thirty-six
  were generated (`stories/01-the-game/complicity-options.md`) and sorted by damage rather
  than by preference. Worth repeating: a moral question about a character is exactly the
  kind of choice the model will answer with the middle of the distribution, which in this
  case is *keep her sympathetic*.
**Consequences:** `ai-tells.md` §D gains *the protected protagonist*;
`craft-principles.md` §6 beat 3 gains the direction test; `stories/_template.md` gains a
line under `wound`. First rule in the repo extracted from a thread Mark identified rather
than a passage he objected to.
**Status:** active


### L-022 — Rules have a stage. Invention takes none of them.
**Rule:** No rule in this repo may be applied to an *idea*. Prohibitions, prescriptions and
tests all describe finished prose and come on at the **draft** stage. At invention,
generate as though the craft folder did not exist. At selection, repetition across the
collection may be **flagged** and never used to kill a candidate. See the stage index at
the top of this file.
**Origin:** Mark, 2026-09-09, reading `bible/plate-explorations/attitude-of-grief.md`.
**Evidence:** *"I'm realizing that there are lots of rules. We might be crippling ourselves
before we even start the race with these. We are assuming certain things won't work when we
probably don't know that yet. I think we should be much more open in the beginning, and then
whittle things down using the rules in later stages and prune what doesn't work or is cliche
or repetitive. But I see a lot of ideas that could be good being scrapped because of
incredibly strict rules."*
**The four instances that prompted it**, all mine and all in one day's output:
- **The wear-and-pressure burn.** I declared a whole family of ideas dead — pressed leaves,
  worn plinth, flattened grass, two-plate comparison, the ground reporting what the figure
  will not — on the strength of one 150-word vignette **that has never been scored.**
  Evidence for the ban: zero. That is the worst case in the repo and it is the model of the
  error: a spent *event* was generalised into a spent *register*.
- **The eighteen-item modal band.** Mark asked me to *exhaust* the obvious ones. I
  incinerated them. Several were premises with real potential killed by one line each — the
  fever ward, the coffins that come up, the double, the soldier at the crossroads — and the
  three that genuinely deserved refusing (plantation revenge, voodoo curse, swamp monster)
  did not need the other fifteen shot alongside them.
- **The wings.** A real observation — spread wings are story 01's arms-out silhouette —
  converted into a near-veto before a sentence existed. The observation was worth keeping;
  the veto was not mine to issue.
- **"She stood for the sculptor and never came out of the pose."** Ruled out on D-004. But
  D-004 is a test on a finished story, and I had collapsed *the premise is legible* into
  *the story will explain itself*, which does not follow: a story can be built on that rule
  and still be unassemblable, if nothing in the prose lets the reader put it together.
**How to apply:**
- **Flag, do not kill.** A note that names the risk and keeps the candidate is worth more
  than a verdict, because the note survives to be argued with at selection and the verdict
  destroys the evidence. Where a candidate file already carries `[near: …]` or `[flag: …]`,
  that is the correct form — **provided the candidate is still on the list.**
- **An event is spent; an idea is not.** Part 3 of `invention-protocol.md` is a repetition
  *register*, not a blacklist. The question at selection is not *is this a near neighbour*
  but **would the story it grows into be a different story** — which is a judgment about two
  stories, and it is Mark's.
- **The modal band is a list to notice, not a fence.** Its function is anti-collapse: named
  obviousness stops being the default answer. Naming it is the whole mechanism. Burning it
  adds nothing and costs the candidates.
- **Say the concern once, in a clause, and move on.** The failure mode this rule replaces is
  not caution, it is *volume* — three paragraphs of doctrine attached to an idea makes the
  doctrine the deliverable and buries the thing Mark is trying to read.
- **What stays absolute, and why it does not conflict.** The banned-phrase list, the tactile
  register, obliquity, the unasked question: all of them describe sentences, so they simply
  have nothing to bite on before a draft exists. Nothing is being loosened at the draft
  stage. This rule moves the fence; it does not remove it.
**Consequences:** the **stage index** added at the top of this file, above the beat index.
`invention-protocol.md` Part 2 gains *generate unfiltered* and Part 3 is rewritten from a
blacklist into a register. `ai-tells.md` §F's *banned by default* is qualified by stage.
`bible/story-seed-pool.md`'s discard band is un-burned. The `attitude-of-grief` exploration
is revised to restore what it scrapped.
**And this rule narrows rather than adds**, which STATUS names as the only kind of doctrine
worth writing: it puts a stage precondition on every other entry in this file, and the four
instances above are restored candidates rather than new prohibitions.
**Status:** active. Basis 1, and it governs process rather than prose, so it is a working
rule — obey it.

### L-023 — Propose the scare, not the specification
**Rule:** A candidate idea is delivered as a **rendered moment** — a short paragraph in
which something happens or is described, and which does something to a reader cold. Not a
one-line plot fact, not a mechanism, not a specification. If the frightening thing is not
present in the candidate itself, it was never in the idea; it was a plan for finding out
later whether there was an idea.
**Origin:** Mark, 2026-09-09, on `bible/plate-explorations/attitude-of-grief.md`.
**Evidence:** *"I'm seeing a lot of minutiae about type of screws, and details within the
story, but not a lot about the things that's scary. When I look at an image, I try to get my
mind to a place that sends a shiver down my spine and form an idea based on that. The details
can be ironed out later. I need to find a way for you to propose ideas that are about 'what
is the scary thing'. It can even be in the form of sample prose, a paragraph describing a
scene in which something happens or is described."*
**The tell, named exactly.** The candidate he quoted was *"the panel is refastened with the
wrong screws."* Inside a finished story that is good evidence — it is the sort of detail a
reader finds and cannot put down. In a candidate list it frightens nobody, because it only
works on a reader who is **already inside the world**, and at proposal stage there is no
world yet. I had been generating the artefacts a story leaves behind instead of the moments a
story is made of, and then grading them for compliance.
**How to apply:**
- **The generation-stage test, and it is the only one that belongs there:** read the
  candidate cold, as a stranger. Does anything happen to you? If the answer requires
  context, plot, or a second candidate to be understood, it is a specification.
- **Sixty to a hundred and forty words is the right size.** Long enough to have a run-up
  and an end-loaded sentence; short enough that twelve of them cost nothing and eleven can
  be thrown away.
- **The project already had the correct artefact and I substituted lists.**
  `craft/vignette-bank.md` exists precisely for this — *single-paragraph vignettes, one
  mechanism of dread each, critiqued cheaply* — and `CLAUDE.md` says test on vignettes before
  stories. A bulleted premise pool is not a cheaper vignette, it is a different and worse
  thing: it defers the only question that matters until a draft has been paid for.
- **Machinery is derived, never proposed.** Period, trade, paperwork, the wound, the rule
  off-page: all of it is worked out **after** something has landed, and in service of it. A
  period seam is a place to go looking for a scare, not a substitute for one.
- **Do not annotate the beat.** Flags, collisions and doctrine go in a short note at the end
  of the file, not attached to the paragraph. A caveat inside a rendered moment destroys the
  thing being tested — the same error as L-022's volume problem, one stage further in.
- **Contradiction between candidates is correct.** A set of beats may disagree about what the
  thing even is. Consistency is a property of a story and there is no story yet.
- **What this does not license.** Not purple, and not the tactile register (L-007). The beats
  that work are plain, and the scare sits in the fact rather than in the adjectives — a dirty
  sole, a wet hem in a dry week, a jaw the girl would have had. `style-guide.md` still
  governs the sentences; the change is what a candidate *is*.
**It extends to story shapes, not just to beats.** Added 2026-09-09, same day, after the
failure recurred one scale up: I obeyed this rule at the beat level and then wrote four story
shapes as specifications — arithmetic, a committee, a clerk — with no instant anywhere in them
where a reader's skin moves. Mark: *"I think we're bumping up against your wanting to satisfy
the constraints of the story, and losing the reason for the story, the fear, the horror."*
**A proposed shape comes with its worst scene written.** A shape described rather than
rendered gets assembled out of the constraints it has to satisfy, and an assembly always
passes the rules and never frightens anybody. The tell in that case: the ending I had built —
she buys the unowned plot — had the correct *form* (permanent expense, no explanation,
complicity) and was a **competent, protective act**, so the story closed on a woman solving
her problem. Horror endings expose; that one settled.

**Downstream half, added 2026-09-10, story 03 movement four: a scored beat is either placed
or declined by name, and the discard list must be exhaustive.** Mark asked whether the
children's lines about the figure were coming back — *"I liked those passages"* — and they
were not, because the skeleton had no slot for them and `source-material.md`'s superseded
table named only one of the three as declined. The other two died in the gap between
selection and drafting, silently, and nothing in the pipeline would ever have reported it.
- **The brief itself named the instrument** — *the horror arrives obliquely: a child's flat
  correction* — and the plan that was built from the brief contained no correction. A named
  instrument with no beat assigned to it is the failure, and it is invisible from inside the
  plan, because the plan reads as complete.
- **Procedure, and it is one pass:** after a skeleton is written, walk the scored beats and
  mark each one **placed (with its movement)** or **declined (with the reason)**. Anything
  unmarked is the bug. The same walk goes the other way for the brief's named instruments:
  each one gets a movement number or an argument.
- **Why this rule and not diligence.** L-023 exists because the frightening thing has to be
  present in the candidate; this is the same rule at the next stage — a beat that landed with
  Mark is the only *tested* material in the project, and losing one is strictly worse than
  never having written it, because it consumed a scoring round to earn its place.
**Consequences:** `invention-protocol.md` Part 2 gains a generation-format rule — candidates
are rendered, and the four tests and the off-page rule stay after selection (L-022).
`craft/vignette-bank.md` is named as the format of record for a candidate.
`bible/plate-explorations/attitude-of-grief-beats.md` is the first file written to it.
**Note on the shape of this critique.** It is L-002 pointed the other way: that rule says
Mark's feedback names an **experience** and it is my job to find the mechanism. This one says
my **proposals** must name an experience too — and that handing him mechanisms and asking
which he likes puts the work on the wrong side of the loop.
**Status:** active. Basis 1, working rule — obey it.

### L-024 — The exception needs the rule already faintly in place
**Rule:** When the horror is carried by one person violating an unspoken avoidance, the
avoidance must be on the page *before* the violation — and it may be laid in only in forms
too weak to be a mechanism: an absence, a trace in the ground, the point-of-view
character's own arrangement with an ordinary reason attached, or one other person doing one
small thing that goes unremarked. Never by anybody being seen to obey.
**Origin:** Story 03, movement one, Mark, 2026-09-10.
**Evidence:** *"Also, there hasn't been much setup of avoidance yet, so will him going
towards it do any work? something to think about."*
**How to apply:**
- **The mistake was reading a prohibition as a dispensation.** L-016 basis 4 supplies
  Mark's own fix for the crowd problem — *establish the rule by breaking it*, give the
  thing somebody who does not feel it and let him walk up and touch the face. That is a
  rule about **how not to prove** an avoidance. I took it as permission not to establish
  one at all, and wrote a movement in which a young man walks up a path in a burying ground
  where nothing has yet been conceded to anything. An exception with nothing to be an
  exception to is a man walking.
- **The collision with L-016 is real, and the resolution is register, not quantity.**
  What is barred is a scene in which people are *seen obeying* — that converts a feeling in
  the parish into a property of the object. What is permitted is everything that leaves the
  obedience unwitnessed:
  - **an absence** — the plot with no coping, nothing planted, no stone with a name. The
    observance not performed (L-016, third amendment) is the cheapest instrument in the
    repo and it does not spend D-003.
  - **a trace** — shell packed hard as far as the third row and loose past it. The ground
    reports the habit without anybody performing it. Attach no cause; a trace with an
    explanation attached is a fact with a lawyer (L-018).
  - **her own arrangement, with a deniable ordinary reason** — she works the north face of
    the coping because the wall's shade is on that side until ten. True, sufficient, and
    not a decision she has made.
  - **one other person, once, unremarked** — the aunt sends him to the cistern the long way
    round the wall. Nobody comments, and it reads as an errand.
- **Order within the movement:** absence → trace → her own arrangement → the break. By the
  time somebody goes up the path, it is the only thing in the scene that has not been
  quietly conceded to, and the break needs no emphasis whatever — which is what keeps it
  out of L-016's way.
- **The quantity ceiling.** Three light touches in a thousand words was the working number.
  Four or five people each performing an avoidance is the crowd L-016 bars, arriving one at
  a time, and it is the likelier failure now that this rule exists to be over-obeyed.
- **General form, beyond avoidance.** Any story whose engine is *one person is not affected*
  owes the reader the effect first. This is L-009 pointed at a social fact rather than at an
  activity: the wrongness needs a normal to be wrong against, and a norm nobody has been
  seen to hold is not yet a normal.
- **"Never by anybody being seen to obey" means SHOWN OBEYING IN A SCENE. It does not bar
  stating the norm. Added 2026-09-10, Mark, and it is a correction to this rule's own first
  draft.** I wrote the line above and then read it as *never state the avoidance at all*,
  which cost story 03 its best beat. Mark: *"I don't think I had a problem with the prose
  about people deliberately not laying tools on the plinth, and it kind of seems like that
  one line about her lying the rake down on the plinth needs this... When I read the line
  about her putting the rake down and then picking it up, it was just confusing."*
  - **What L-016 basis 4 actually bans is a demonstration**: a crowd shown obeying,
    simultaneously, in one scene, under test — *"forcing 40 people near it and them all still
    resisting."* That is an experiment with a 100% result, and a result is a property of the
    object.
  - **A single witness reporting a long-run absence is not a demonstration.** No scene, no
    simultaneity, no test, nobody's obedience on stage: *"She had never seen anybody put
    anything against the figure. Not a rake, not a coat, not a bucket."* That is one woman's
    negative observation, and it is the register Mark named as the strongest available —
    *"not laying tools on it could imply fear where a hat tip does not."*
  - **Two safeguards make it safe, and both belong to the passage rather than to the rule.**
    Say plainly that **there is no rule about it**, which denies the mechanism on the page;
    and let the violator **succeed** — the rake stands there perfectly well, so the object
    exerts nothing and every bit of the dread is in her taking it back. Deniable, overridable
    in principle, and overridden.
  - **The general failure, which is L-022 arriving at the draft stage.** L-022 records
    prohibitions killing *candidates*; this is a prohibition killing *a beat's setup*, by
    being widened from the specific staging it was extracted from into a ban on the whole
    subject. **Before obeying a prohibition, restate the exact thing that was objected to.**
    Mine was a scene of forty people; I acted as though it had been a sentence.
  - **The positive-habit substitute did not work and is worth recording as a failed fix.** I
    first tried establishing only where tools *do* go — the copings take the buckets, the
    rakes lean on the crosses — hoping the exemption would be inferred. Mark: *"the part you
    added about where things go doesn't mean that a rake couldn't lean on the figure."*
    Correct: a norm about where things go says nothing about where they are never put. The
    clause stays as scenery, and the omission is now stated.
- **Adjacency, not amplification — and this is the clause that keeps the rule out of L-016's
  way.** Added 2026-09-10 from the movement-scale fresh-context review of story 03. The four
  instruments above are deniable by design, which means they read as *terrain* on a first
  pass. The reflex when that is reported is to make the evidence harder: state the trace
  twice more, let a second person perform the swerve, put a number on it. **That reflex
  rebuilds the force field out of physics** — a review that says *"a footprint census, and
  the hardest evidence in the story because it can't be a mood"* is describing something
  that proves the avoidance works, and a thing that demonstrably works can be described.
  - **The fix is distance, not volume.** Put one already-planted physical fact of the
    avoidance in **the same paragraph as the violation**, so the break has something to be
    measured against at the instant it happens. Story 03: the compaction is planted in the
    ground description, and when Ti-Jean goes up the path three paragraphs later the whole
    addition is *"The shell went over his shoes."* Four words, no new evidence, verbatim
    echo, nothing explained.
  - **The general form:** an unstated contrast fails far more often from the two halves
    being *far apart* than from either half being too quiet. Before strengthening either
    half, check whether they are simply in different scenes — and if the trace is a physical
    one, the cheapest version of adjacency is to put the violator's own body on it.
**Consequences:** L-016 basis 4's fix gains a precondition; `craft-principles.md` §5
*repetition with variation* gains nothing, but §6 beat 1 (*notice*) is where these four
instruments live. Story 03 movement one revised: the figure moved to the head of the shell
path, three avoidance touches added ahead of the break.
**Status:** active. Basis 1, prohibition — near-absolute, and cheap to obey.

### L-025 — A beat is a unit with timing inside it; move it whole
**Rule:** Never merge two beats that share a mechanism, and never split one beat across two
places. A beat is not a fact plus a line — it is an approach, a **delay**, and an end-loaded
sentence, and the delay is the part that gets thrown away first because it looks like nothing
is happening in it. And before rebuilding any beat, read its **prose** version, including in
files marked superseded: a register can be retired while the construction stays canonical.
**Origin:** Story 03, movements two and four, Mark, 2026-09-10.
**Evidence:** *"I'm noticing that some ideas are getting fragmented or we're taking pieces of
them and putting them in which is making them less impactful... I see elements of this in the
story, but I think it's separated across two different lines in different places. The second
instance where the child simply says 'she wondered if grandma could see the angel back'
barely hits. The original prose is much better."*
**The original, in `bible/plate-explorations/attitude-of-grief-prose-01.md`:**
> At Easter I told her the angel was watching over her grandfather, because that is what you
> say to a child in a cemetery, and she said it wasn't watching over him, it was watching *at*
> him. I let it go, the way you let a child's grammar go. She was quiet all down the shell
> path and at the gate she wanted to know whether he could see it back.
**How to apply:**
- **Name the mechanism before touching the beat.** Here it is **delay**
  (`craft-principles.md` §5): the correction, the adult's dismissal, *then the length of a
  path with a child not talking*, then the question. The question is frightening because it
  is what a child arrives at after carrying the thought for a hundred yards. Cut the walk and
  the same words are a second cute line — which is exactly what Mark reported.
- **Fragmentation preserves the facts and destroys the timing**, which is why it is so easy to
  do while believing nothing was lost. My split kept both sentences and put them three
  movements apart; every word survived and the beat did not.
- **Merging is the same error inverted.** I welded the correction from one beat
  (*her hair's just in the way*) to the question from another (*could see it back*), losing the
  first beat's own logic and the second's internal chain — *watching **at** him* is what makes
  *can he see it back* inevitable, and the preposition **is** the correction. Two beats that
  perform the same move are alternatives, not components.
- **Read the retired prose, and distrust "content survives above."** `source-material.md`
  listed `-prose-01.md` as superseded with *"Content survives above"*, and the child beat was
  not above; it was nowhere. So I rebuilt from the **bullet** versions in the beats files,
  which record what happens and not the order it happens in. **A superseded-file table is a
  claim about register, never about completeness**, and a claim of completeness inside one is
  the thing to check rather than trust.
- **POV conversion is where units get broken, and it is predictable.** The original is first
  person and the adult telling the child the pious thing **is the narrator**. Converting to
  close third on a bystander demotes her to overhearing, the unit loses its owner, and it then
  looks divisible. The fix was to give the adult's line back to the POV character: Elmire tells
  her own son the angel is watching over his grandmother, and he corrects her. **When a beat
  resists conversion, check whether the POV character should be holding the other end of it.**
- **The cheap diagnostic:** if a beat can be described as *two good lines*, it has already been
  fragmented in the plan. A beat is one thing that happens.
**Consequences:** story 03's child beat restored whole in M2 and removed from M4;
`source-material.md`'s superseded table corrected to stop asserting completeness; beats.md #6
retired with a reason rather than merged. L-023's downstream clause covers whether a beat is
*placed*; this covers whether it arrives *intact*.
**Status:** active. Basis 1, prohibition.
