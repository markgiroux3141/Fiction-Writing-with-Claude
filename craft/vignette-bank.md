# Vignette Bank — Calibration Paragraphs

A bench test for the prose, run before any more full drafts. One paragraph per
idea, each isolating **a single mechanism of dread**, so that Mark's critique
identifies *which levers the model can actually pull* rather than whether one
particular story worked.

A failed 150-word vignette costs nothing. A failed 4,000-word draft costs a round.

---

## Rules for a vignette

- **100–200 words.** One paragraph, or two if the break is doing work.
- **Standalone.** No title, no name we need, no story around it. It starts in the
  middle and stops.
- **Past tense, close third or first** — the book's defaults apply.
- **One lever only.** If it works, we know why. If a vignette needs two mechanisms
  to land, that is a finding.
- **A stated Rule (off-page).** One sentence above the prose saying what the thing wants
  and what it does. It never appears in the vignette. If it cannot be written, the
  vignette is decoration and will read as such. (L-008.)
- **Zero or one impossible thing.** Several of these should have *none* — dread
  built entirely from the mundane is the hardest test and the most valuable pass.
- **`ai-tells.md` §A grep applies.** Zero hits, same as a draft.
- **One quotable sentence, or it failed.** (`ai-tells.md` §E.8, in miniature.)

## How Mark scores one

Three questions, ten seconds each. Nothing longer is needed.

1. **Did anything land physically?** Not "was it good" — did the body register it.
2. **Which sentence did the work?**
3. **Which sentence is a lie?** The limp one, the borrowed one, the one that sounds
   like a ghost story instead of like a person.

Answers convert to L-IDs in `lessons-learned.md` under the normal extraction rule.
Vignette critiques live in `craft/vignettes/critique-NN.md`.

## The bank

Grouped by lever. Plate references show where a vignette doubles as reconnaissance
for a story we already intend to write.

### A. Presence with nothing on stage
*Can dread be sustained with no manifestation at all?*

**A1 — The staircase.** Crossing the foot of the stairs on an ordinary errand, and
being certain someone is standing at the top, looking down. Nothing is there. The
errand still gets finished. *Lever: peripheral certainty. Zero impossible things.*

**A2 — The chair settles.** From the next room, the sound of the chair you got up
from taking someone's weight. *Lever: sound alone, and the beat of delay before it.*

**A3 — The dog will not go down the hall.** No growling, no bristling. It simply
finds something else to do, every time, for a week. *Lever: displaced witness — the
animal knows and the narrator has to decide what to do with that.*

### B. Arithmetic
*Dread delivered as a number that will not come out right.*

**B1 — The extra setting.** Laying the table and finding one place more than there
are people. The horror is not the plate; it is standing there deciding whether to
take it away. *Lever: the frightening thing is a decision.*

**B2 — The garment on the line.** A shirt that belongs to nobody in the house, worn
through at the elbows in exactly the places yours are. *Lever: the double, arriving
as laundry.*

**B3 — The third tread.** An empty house, ten years shut, and the stair treads
polished — but only the third one, and only in the middle. *Lever: wear as evidence
of patience.* (Plates I, II.)

### C. Delayed recognition
*The reader understands before the narrator does.*

**C1 — The day-book.** A photographer totting up the week's sittings, entirely
absorbed in the money. One entry is in the wrong hand. He notes it and moves on.
*Lever: professional composure as the delivery system.* (Plate V.)

**C2 — Describing the sitter.** A studio portrait described with complete technical
detachment — pose, exposure, the set of the collar — by someone who does not say
that the face is wrong. *Lever: omission. Everything is stated except the one thing.*
(Plate V.)

### D. Touch
*James's step 5: contact, brief, tactile, over before it is described.*

**D1 — The handover.** Reaching into flat brown water to retrieve something dropped,
and having it put into your hand. *Lever: the impossible arriving as a courtesy.*
(Plate III.)

**D2 — The bed.** Putting a hand out in the dark to the empty side, and finding the
sheet wet and warm. *Lever: texture and temperature, nothing seen.*

### E. Reflection and copy
**E1 — The basin.** Washing at dawn, and the reflection finishing the movement a
half-second after you do. Twice. Then correctly. *Lever: error, correction, doubt.*

**E2 — The lit window.** Standing at a window at night looking out at your own
reflection, and behind it, in the yard, the reflection of something that is in the
room with you. *Lever: geometry.* (Plate III.)

### F. Patience and wear
**F1 — The worn chair.** A seat worn through in a house where nobody sits, and the
boards in front of it worn too — in the shape of two feet that never moved.

**F2 — Bare feet in the leaf litter.** A cemetery angel photographed for a
commission; the leaves around the plinth are pressed flat, not undisturbed.
*Lever: the ground reports what the figure will not.* (Plate IV.)

### G. Vegetal physical, delivered flat
*D-003 register. The plainest sentence carries the worst image.*

**G1 — Trimming.** A man at a mirror with scissors, tidying a beard that is not
hair, in the same unhurried way he does it every Sunday. (Plate V.)

**G2 — The lifted board.** A housekeeper prises up a floorboard to deal with a smell
and finds the root beneath has grown around something soft, which moves. (Plate II.)

**G3 — The splinter.** Working a splinter out of a heel by lamplight, and it keeps
coming, and it is fibrous, and it is rooted. *Lever: duration — the horror is how
long the sentence goes on.*

### H. Document and object as vector
**H1 — Paid in advance.** A coffin-maker's receipt, correctly filled out, dated
eleven days ahead, in the buyer's own hand. *Lever: bureaucracy as prophecy.*

**H2 — The inventory.** An estate list of a dead woman's effects in which one line
describes an item still in use. *Lever: tense.*

### I. Indifferent weather, wrong tone
*Bright and hot is a better setting for the worst scene.* (`ai-tells.md` §C.)

**I1 — The game.** Children playing at ghosts on a clear hot morning. An adult in a
sheet joins in, and does not stop when they are called in to eat. *Lever: tonal
inversion — nothing dark, nothing at night.* (Plate I.)

**I2 — Six inches.** A drowning in six inches of standing water, in daylight, within
sight of the house. *Lever: the impossible as an accident report.*

### J. Speech
*Can the horror arrive in dialogue, from someone who is not frightened?* Untested
entirely — the collection has produced no dialogue at all.

**J1 — The laundress.** A woman collecting the wash mentions, in passing and with no
alarm, that she saw the mistress out on the road at four in the morning, and offers
neighbourly advice about the ditch. *Lever: the witness who is not afraid. The horror
is that someone else has seen it and finds it unremarkable.*

**J2 — The remote eye.** Clearing a dead relative's house on a video call; the person
on the other end keeps looking at a point above and behind the phone and will not say
why. *Lever: geometry at a distance — and the D-002 test, since this cannot be done
before about 2010.*

### K. Lost time
*Duration as the instrument, in the temporal register.*

**K1 — The wake.** Sitting up with a body, the two-till-four turn, told not to sleep.
She sleeps. Something in the room was attended to in the interval. *Lever: the gap.
Nothing is witnessed; only the result is.*

## Suggested first batch

Six, chosen for the widest spread of levers rather than the best images:

| # | Vignette | Lever tested |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A1 — The staircase | Presence with nothing shown; no impossible thing |
| 2 | B1 — The extra setting | Dread as arithmetic and decision |
| 3 | D1 — The handover | Touch; the James climax in one move |
| 4 | I1 — The game | Tonal inversion; daylight horror |
| 5 | G1 — Trimming | Explicit vegetal physical, flat delivery |
| 6 | F1 — The worn chair | Patience evidenced by wear |

If 1 and 4 land and 5 does not, the book's restraint budget (D-003) needs
rethinking before any story spends it. That is exactly the sort of thing worth
learning at 150 words rather than 4,000.
