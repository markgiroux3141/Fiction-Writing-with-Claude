# Timeline, 1850–1920 — Anchors for the Period Centre

Dated facts, so a story can be placed rather than vaguely olden. Gulf South in
**bold**; national and technological in plain text. Confidence is marked where it
matters: **[solid]** verified in more than one source; **[check]** believable but
single-sourced from a search snippet.

Rule of use: pick a year first, then read down the column. A story set in 1893 in a
Louisiana parish is a different world from one set in 1868 or 1915, and the
differences are mostly about light, ice, disease and who owns the land.

---

## 1850s

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1851 | Frederick Scott Archer publishes the wet collodion process. **[solid]** |
| **1853** | **Yellow fever's worst Louisiana year: c. 29,120 cases, c. 8,647 dead. [solid]** |
| 1854 | Ambrotype (collodion positive on glass) in commercial use. **[solid]** |
| 1855 | Safety match patented (Lundström). Friction "lucifers" already common since the 1830s. **[solid]** |
| 1856 | Tintype (ferrotype) patented; becomes the itinerant photographer's format. **[solid]** |
| **10 Aug 1856** | **The Last Island hurricane. Isle Dernière split in two; at least 198 dead. The subject of Hearn's *Chita*. [solid]** |
| 1858 | First transatlantic cable (fails within weeks). |

## 1860s

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1861 | **William Mumler's first "spirit photograph"**, Boston. **[solid]** |
| 1861–65 | Civil War. c. 620,000 dead — the demographic engine behind both Spiritualism and the professionalisation of embalming. **[solid]** |
| **1862** | **New Orleans falls to Union forces (April); occupied for the rest of the war.** |
| 1860s | Kerosene displaces whale and camphene oil as the standard lamp fuel. **[solid]** |
| **1865** | **New Orleans running three Carré ammonia refrigeration machines — the coastal ice trade from New England having been cut by the war. [check]** |
| **18 May 1868** | **Louisiana Ice Manufacturing Co. opens on the New Orleans riverfront at Tchoupitoulas and Delachaise — commonly called the first commercial ice plant in the world. [check — widely repeated, worth confirming]** |
| 1869 | **Mumler's fraud trial in New York.** National news; he is acquitted. **[solid]** |

## 1870s

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1870s | Cypress industry begins its boom in the Louisiana swamplands. **[solid]** |
| 1876 | Bell patents the telephone. Rural Louisiana will not feel this for decades. |
| **1877** | **End of Reconstruction; federal troops withdrawn. The land and labour arrangements that define the rest of this period harden from here.** |
| **1878** | **Lower Mississippi Valley yellow fever epidemic: >74,000 cases, c. 16,000 dead across five states. Originates in New Orleans, reaches Memphis. Upriver towns close trade with New Orleans at the first rumour. [solid]** |
| 1879 | Oliphant, *A Beleaguered City*. |
| 1879 | Edison's practical incandescent lamp. |

## 1880s — the hinge decade

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1880s | **Gelatin dry plates replace wet collodion.** Exposure times collapse; the portable darkroom becomes unnecessary. **[solid]** |
| 1880s | Germ theory achieves general acceptance in American medicine. **[solid]** |
| 1880 | Only about a third of Louisiana farmers are tenants. **[solid]** |
| **1880–1925** | **The Louisiana lumber boom.** |
| 1881 | Carlos Finlay proposes the mosquito as the yellow fever vector. Ridiculed for nineteen years. **[solid]** |
| 1883 | The steam skidder is introduced to swamp logging. **[solid]** |
| 1885 | The safety bicycle. |
| 1888 | The Kodak No. 1 — photography leaves the professional's hands. **[solid]** |
| 1888 | **Maggie Fox publicly confesses the Spiritualist rappings were made by cracking her joints.** The movement survives it. **[solid]** |
| 1889 | **Pullboats introduced to Louisiana cypress logging.** **[solid]** |
| 1889 | Hearn, *Chita*. Cable, *Strange True Stories of Louisiana*. |

## 1890s

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| early 1890s | **"Dummy" railroad lines pushed into the swamps.** Large-scale industrial logging becomes possible in previously unreachable ground like the Atchafalaya. **[solid]** |
| 1890s | Formaldehyde-based embalming spreads; the undertaker becomes a trade. **[check]** |
| **1–2 Oct 1893** | **The Chênière Caminada hurricane. Landfall at c. 130 mph; around 2,000 dead. Still the deadliest storm in Louisiana history. [solid]** |
| 1893 | Chopin, *Bayou Folk*. King, *Balcony Stories*. Bierce, *Can Such Things Be?* |
| 1896 | Rural Free Delivery begins — mail comes to the farm rather than the town. |
| 1896 | *Plessy v. Ferguson*, argued out of New Orleans. |
| 1899 | The dry-cell flashlight (Eveready). Note that in period photographic usage "flashlight" means **flash powder**, not a torch. **[check]** |

## 1900s

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1900 | Walter Reed confirms *Aedes aegypti* as the yellow fever vector. **[solid]** |
| 1900 | Galveston hurricane — c. 6,000–8,000 dead, the deadliest US natural disaster. |
| **1905** | **The last yellow fever epidemic in the United States: New Orleans, c. 450 dead. Controlled by covering cisterns, screening, and draining standing water. [solid]** |
| **1909** | **Ten sawmills operating on Bayou Teche alone. [solid]** |
| **1910** | **55% of Louisiana farmers are tenants, up from a third in 1880. [solid]** |

## 1910s

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1915** | **Another hurricane strikes the rebuilt Chênière Caminada, ending the recovery. [solid]** |
| 1914–18 | The First World War; American entry 1917. |
| 1918–19 | Influenza pandemic. |
| 1919–20 | **The Great Migration is underway; rural Louisiana parishes begin losing population northward.** |

## After our window, but worth knowing

- **1927** — the Great Mississippi Flood. The defining Louisiana catastrophe of the
  century, and the reason a modern-set story would sit on a different landscape.
- **1927** — Louisiana sells 1,200 carloads of Spanish moss. **[check]**
- **1935** — the Rural Electrification Administration. At the start of the 1930s more
  than 90% of rural American homes were still lit by kerosene. **[solid]**
  **This is the single most important fact in this file.** For the whole of our
  period, in the country, there is no electric light. None.

## Three quick period tests

Before drafting, answer these three and the year will mostly write itself.

1. **What is the light?** Candle and firelight, or kerosene lamp, or (town, late,
   wealthy) gas. Never electric.
2. **Who owns the ground the character is standing on, and does the character owe on
   it?** After 1877 this is the central social fact of the setting.
3. **Is anyone sick, and does anyone know why?** Before 1900, no — see
   `medicine-and-disease.md`.
