# Anachronism Traps

A negative checklist, in the manner of `craft/ai-tells.md` §A. Grep for these before
delivering a period draft.

**This is not a vocabulary list to spend from.** `style-guide.md` allows one or two
authentic period terms per page. The purpose of this file is to stop things getting
*out*, not to get more things *in*.

Two dating tools are cited where useful: the OED (paywalled — see
`00-ACCESS-GAPS.md`) and Google Ngram Viewer, which is free, crude, and good enough
to catch the obvious. **When in doubt, rewrite around the word rather than research
it.** A sentence that avoids the question is faster than a sentence that answers it.

---

## A. Words that will break it

Confidence marked: **[solid]** = confident; **[check]** = plausible, verify before
relying on a precise year.

### Social and personal
- **teenager** — 1940s. The category did not exist. **[solid]**
- **teenage / adolescent as a social identity** — the concept is 20th century.
- **date** (a romantic outing) — 1920s. They *courted*, or *walked out with*, or
  *kept company*. **[solid]**
- **boyfriend / girlfriend** — 1920s–30s in the romantic sense. **[solid]**
- **hello** — spreads with the telephone from about 1880. Before that: *good day*,
  *good evening*, *how do you do*. **[check on the exact decade; the direction is
  right]**
- **okay / OK** — American and attested from 1839, so it is *technically* available,
  but it reads modern on the page. Avoid.
- **weekend** — a British import that spreads late in the century; in a rural parish
  where Saturday is a work day, the concept barely applies. **[check]**
- **teenaged, sibling** (as a common word), **parenting**, **role**, **lifestyle**,
  **partner** (romantic), **relationship** (romantic) — all modern in this sense.
- **privacy** as an expectation, **childhood** as a protected state, **romantic love
  as the basis for marriage** — concept anachronisms, and worse than word ones,
  because they are invisible.

### Psychological — the largest single risk
Almost the entire modern vocabulary of the interior is 20th century, and it is exactly
the vocabulary an AI drafts in by default:

**trauma** (in the psychological sense), **traumatised**, **stress**, **stressed**,
**anxiety** (as a condition), **depression** (as a condition — the period says
*melancholy*, *low spirits*, *nerves*), **panic attack**, **triggered**, **coping**,
**processing**, **denial**, **repression**, **grieving process**, **closure**,
**boundaries**, **self-esteem**, **breakdown**, **obsessed**, **in shock**,
**dissociate**, **compartmentalise**, **projecting**.

> This overlaps almost exactly with `style-guide.md` → **Emotion: render, do not
> name.** Obeying the style guide will remove most of this list automatically. If a
> draft needs a psychological noun, the draft has already failed a different rule.

### Supernatural — the second largest risk
- **paranormal** — 20th century. **[solid]**
- **the supernatural** as a noun category is period-fine; **a supernatural entity** is
  not.
- **entity**, **energy** (spiritual sense), **vibes**, **aura** (popularised later),
  **psychic** (as a noun for a person — 20th century), **medium** (fine, 1850s
  onward), **haunting** as a countable noun ("a haunting"), **residual haunting**,
  **the veil**, **dark forces**, **cleansing**, **manifestation**, **poltergeist** (a
  German loan that arrives in English later in the century — **[check]**),
  **possession** (fine theologically, wrong casually), **portal**, **dimension**,
  **plane of existence**.

What they say instead: *a spirit*, *the dead*, *an apparition*, *something in the
house*, *she is not at rest*, *it walks* — or, most often and most usefully,
**nothing**. See `influences/the-19th-century-feel.md` §8.

### Technological and material
- **electric light** — see the timeline. Not in a rural parish in this whole period.
- **flashlight / torch** — the dry-cell hand light is 1899 and urban. In period
  photographic usage, "flashlight" means **flash powder**. **[check]**
- **telephone** — 1876, but rural service comes much later. A parish house does not
  have one.
- **radio**, **film**, **movie**, **camera** (handheld/casual), **snapshot** (Kodak,
  1888 onward — fine after that, wrong before).
- **plastic**, **stainless steel**, **zipper** (1910s+), **nylon**, **aspirin**
  (1899 — available late in the window only), **antibiotic**, **vitamin** (1912+),
  **X-ray** (1895, and not out here), **germ** (fine after the 1880s, anachronistic
  as a lay word much before).
- **nutria** — introduced to Louisiana in the 1930s. Use **muskrat**.
- **teabag**, **paper towel**, **screen door** (fine — wire screening spreads in this
  period and is part of the yellow fever story), **icebox** (fine).

### Idiom that carries its origin
- **like clockwork**, **the ball's in your court**, **touch base**, **on the same
  page**, **run interference**, **reach out** (contact), **check in**, **step up**,
  **push back**, **going forward**, **impact** as a verb, **prioritise**, **focus on**
  (fine literally, modern figuratively), **deal with it**, **big picture**,
  **red flag**, **wake-up call**, **game changer**, **bandwidth**, **unpack** (an
  idea).

These are the ones an AI reaches for hardest, because they are the connective tissue
of modern professional English. They are also the easiest to catch: **any metaphor
drawn from sport, business, machinery, aviation, or computing is suspect.**

## B. Concept traps

Harder than words, and worth a slower pass.

**Time.** Nobody has a wristwatch until the 1910s and the war. A pocket watch is a
possession worth mentioning. Time is told by the sun, the light, the church bell, and
the train. Nobody is punctual to the minute and nobody expects it. Standard time zones
arrive in the US in 1883, and before that local towns keep local noon.

**Distance and speed.** Six miles is an hour and a half on foot, longer in mud. A
neighbour is not somebody you can pop over to see. Getting help takes half a day, and
this is a gift to a ghost story — it is `craft-principles.md` §5's *constriction*,
supplied by the road.

**Communication.** A letter, days. A telegram, hours, from a town, expensive, and read
by the operator. There is no way to reach anyone quickly and no way to check anything.
**A character cannot verify a claim**, which means the reader cannot either.

**Photography.** See the `photography/` folder. Before roughly 1888, no ordinary
person takes a picture. A photograph is an event, made by a professional, paid for,
and probably the only image of that person that exists.

**Police and law.** A parish sheriff, distant, elected and political. No forensics, no
fingerprints (introduced from the 1900s), no crime scene. A body found in water is
looked at by whoever is there.

**Medicine.** See `medicine-and-disease.md`. And note the psychological corollary: no
character in this book has a therapeutic vocabulary or a therapeutic expectation.

**Death.** Ordinary, close, domestic, and handled by the family. See
`death-and-mourning.md`. **No character is squeamish about a body**, and a modern
squeamishness written into a period character is one of the most common tells there
is.

## C. The reverse trap — Wardour Street

M. R. James's own term, from the essay (`texts/craft-essays/`), for fake-antique
diction: *"the belted knight who meets the spectre in the vaulted chamber and has to
say 'By my halidom'... Wardour Street has no business, here."*

Banned as a class:
**hither, thither, whence, thence, betwixt, ere, oft, 'tis, 'twas, methinks,
perchance, forsooth, mayhap, verily, prithee, alas, nay, whilst** (in narration),
**shall** used decoratively, and inversions like *"Little did I know"* — which is
banned twice, being also in `ai-tells.md` §A.

Also banned: **ejaculated**, **exclaimed**, **cried he**, and every dialogue tag that
is not *said* or *asked* unless the verb is doing physical work.

And: **the omniscient moralising aside** ("Such, dear reader, is the way of the
world"). Real Victorians did it. It is unreadable now and it forfeits the flat,
scrupulous voice the whole book depends on.

## D. Pre-flight grep

Run alongside `ai-tells.md` §E. Case-insensitive:

```
trauma|stress|anxiet|closure|coping|processing|triggered|boundaries|self-esteem
paranormal|psychic|energy|entity|vibe|aura|the veil|dark forces|cleansing
teenager|boyfriend|girlfriend|okay\b|weekend|lifestyle|relationship|partner
flashlight|electric light|telephone|snapshot|plastic|nutria
reach out|touch base|on the same page|going forward|red flag|wake-up call|unpack
hither|thither|whence|betwixt|ere\b|methinks|perchance|forsooth|mayhap|prithee
ejaculated|exclaimed|dear reader
```

A hit is not automatically an error. It is a sentence that has to justify itself.
