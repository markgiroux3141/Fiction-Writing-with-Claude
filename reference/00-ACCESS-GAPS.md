# Access Gaps — Things Mark Can Get That Claude Can't

Sources that would improve this folder but that I could not retrieve from here.
Reasons vary: paywall, login, copyright refusal by the fetch tool, blocked host, or
material that only exists on paper. **Anything Mark saves into `reference/texts/`
becomes usable immediately.**

Format: what it is · why it's worth having · where to get it · status.

## Retrieval failures worth retrying in a browser

**M. R. James, "Stories I Have Tried to Write" (1929)**
His post-mortem on his own failures — arguably more useful than the successes. It is
the epilogue to *Collected Ghost Stories* (1931) and is not in any of the three
Gutenberg volumes downloaded here (checked). Look on thin-ghost.org or archive.org's
scan of *Collected Ghost Stories*.
· Status: **not retrieved.**

**M. R. James, introduction to *Ghosts and Marvels* (Oxford, 1924)**
His other craft statement, and where the "loophole for a natural explanation" line
actually lives. I have the 1929 *Bookman* essay in full (`texts/craft-essays/`) but
not this one; the phrase is quoted secondhand in `influences/james-rules.md` and is
flagged there as unverified.
· Status: **not retrieved.** archive.org likely has the volume.

**Charlotte Riddell, *Weird Stories* (1882)**
Gutenberg has *The Uninhabited House* (downloaded) but not *Weird Stories*, which
contains "The Open Door" and "Nut Bush Farm" — the two most useful Riddells for us,
because both are about property, money and a haunting that is a debt.
· Status: **not on Gutenberg.** Try archive.org or the Victorian Women Writers
Project (Indiana).

**Amelia B. Edwards, "The Phantom Coach" (1864)**
Her most anthologised ghost story, and the one worth studying: a man lost in snow,
a coach that should not be running. Both downloaded anthologies turn out to carry
"The Four-Fifteen Express" instead (checked their contents) — a good story, but the
railway one, not the coach one.
· Status: **not retrieved.** It is in *Miss Carew* (1865); try archive.org.

## Paywalled / subscription

**Oxford English Dictionary (first-attestation dates)**
The single most valuable anachronism tool there is. `period/anachronism-traps.md`
gets by on secondary sources and on Google Ngram, which is cruder. Many US public
libraries give free OED access with a card number.
· Status: **paywalled.**

**Oxford Bibliographies, "The Ghost Story" (Victorian Literature)**
A properly curated scholarly bibliography of the form.
oxfordbibliographies.com/display/document/obo-9780199799558/obo-9780199799558-0167.xml
· Status: **paywalled.**

**Michael Cox & R. A. Gilbert, eds., *The Oxford Book of Victorian Ghost Stories***
Still the best single anthology; the editorial introduction is a compressed history
of the form. In copyright as an anthology even where the stories are not.
· Status: **buy it.** The stories themselves are mostly in `texts/canon/`.

## Archives and image collections — browser only

**The Historic New Orleans Collection — wet plate virtual exhibition**
hnoc.org/virtual-exhibitions/from_daguerreotype_to_digital/wet-plate-process
Period Louisiana plates. Worth looking at rather than reading about.

**Library of Congress — Prints & Photographs Online Catalog**
loc.gov/pictures — search *Louisiana cypress logging*, *pullboat*, *Spanish moss*,
*New Orleans cemetery*. High-resolution, public domain, and full of the exact objects
we need names for.

**Louisiana Digital Library** — louisianadigitallibrary.org
**64 Parishes (LEH encyclopedia)** — 64parishes.org — reliable, browsable, and the
best single starting point for anything Louisiana. I have cited it repeatedly from
search snippets rather than reading entries in full.
**Louisiana Folklife Program** — louisianafolklife.org — occupational folklife essays,
including swamp trades and burial custom. Genuinely first-rate primary-adjacent
material.

**Southeastern Louisiana University, Mancil Cypress photo collection**
southeastern.edu/acad_research/programs/csls/historical_collections/photo_collections/l_m_photos/mancil_cypress/
Working photographs of the cypress industry. Faces, clothes, boots, tools.
