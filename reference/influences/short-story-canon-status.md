## The 15 still missing — and whether it matters

**Buy or borrow:**
- **#40 The Swords — Robert Aickman.** Of everything on the missing list, this is the
  one most worth having for *this* book. Aickman is the twentieth century's answer to
  the question the craft folder keeps asking — how to write a haunting whose rules the
  reader can half-infer and be wrong about in an interesting way
  (`craft-principles.md` §3). His stories refuse explanation more completely than
  James's do. *Cold Hand in Mine* or the NYRB *Compulsory Games*.
- **#36 The Summer People — Shirley Jackson.** A slow constriction with no monster in
  it. Directly relevant to the tone in `book-bible.md`. In *Come Along with Me* or
  *Dark Tales*.
- **#41 Where Are You Going, Where Have You Been? — Oates.** The best modern example of
  a menace whose rules are almost stated and never quite.

**Read for interest, not for method:**
Dahl, Ellison, Bixby, Barker, most of King. The Barkers and *The Midnight Meat Train*
in particular are the opposite of this book's programme — explicit, kinetic, and
resolved. #44 *The Autopsy* (Shea) is superb and equally not our register.

## What the five new arrivals are actually good for

- **#35 The Lottery.** The purest example in English of the technique in
  `the-19th-century-feel.md` §1 and §3: a whole community obeying a rule nobody
  explains, reported in a flat, sunny, almost cheerful register. Jackson never once
  tells you what the lottery is for. Read it beside `craft-principles.md` §3.
- **#34 The Small Assassin.** A haunting that is entirely a wound —
  `craft-principles.md` §4 — and a lesson in how far you can push an impossible
  premise if every scene around it is domestic and medical. Bradbury's prose is far
  more decorated than the house style; take the structure.
- **#33 Smoke Ghost.** Leiber's argument that a ghost should be made of the specific
  material conditions of its own time and place — soot, factories, coal yards. Ours
  would be made of standing water, cypress and debt. The single most transferable idea
  on the missing list, and now not missing.
- **#25 August Heat.** Nine pages, two men, one hot day, and an ending that simply
  stops. A very good model for `the-19th-century-feel.md` §9.
- **#31 Who Goes There?** The least relevant of the five to this book — its engine is
  "which of us is not human", a mode of paranoia that has nothing to do with a patient
  vegetal haunting. Worth reading; not worth studying here.

**#45–47 King.** *The Mist* is the useful one, for how long it sustains constriction
inside a single building. Its ending is the argument against itself.

## A note on what the list is missing

Given the shape of *Standing Water*, four absences stand out. All four are already
downloaded:

- **Mary E. Wilkins Freeman, "The Lost Ghost"** — `canon/freeman__the-wind-in-the-rose-bush`.
  Probably the single most relevant story in the whole reference folder to what this
  book is trying to do: a small, plain, domestic American ghost story where the
  haunting *is* the wound.
- **Le Fanu, "The Familiar"** — `canon/lefanu__ghostly-tales-v1`. M. R. James's own
  pick for the best ghost story in English.
- **Charlotte Riddell** — `canon/riddell__the-uninhabited-house`. Hauntings as
  property disputes.
- **Edith Wharton, "The Eyes"** — same volume as "Afterward", story VII. Nastier than
  "Afterward" and better.

See `reading-map.md` for what to take from each.
