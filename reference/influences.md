# Influences

What this book is reaching for, and the specific technique worth stealing from each.

## Primary

**M. R. James** — *Casting the Runes*, *Oh, Whistle, and I'll Come to You*.
The antiquarian method: a dry, credible narrator; a mundane setting rendered with
real expertise; the horror glimpsed at the edge of the frame and never explained.
James's own rule — the ghost should be malevolent, and the reader should be made to
feel "if I'm not careful, something of this kind may happen to me."
**Steal:** reticence, and the authority of specific professional detail.

**Robert Aickman** — *The Trains*, *The Hospice*, *Ringing the Changes*.
"Strange stories" rather than ghost stories: the wrongness is never accounted for,
and the ending refuses to close. Deeply unsettling because the logic is dream logic
held to with a straight face.
**Steal:** irresolution; the confidence to leave the mechanism dark.

**Shirley Jackson** — *The Lottery*, *The Summer People*, *Hill House*.
Domestic surfaces, social discomfort curdling into dread, and a house that is wrong
in ways that are architectural rather than spectral.
**Steal:** dread built from ordinary social interaction; the narrator whose grip is
slipping while the prose stays composed.

**Sarah Waters** — *The Little Stranger*.
Class, property, and resentment as the actual engine, with the haunting as their
expression. Period texture that never becomes costume.
**Steal:** the wound underneath the haunt.

## Secondary

**Susan Hill** — *The Woman in Black*. Pure classical technique; landscape and tide
as antagonists.
**Elizabeth Hand**, **Kelly Link** — the contemporary strange; permission to be
odd at the sentence level.
**Flannery O'Connor**, **Eudora Welty** — for the Gulf South register, and for
grotesquerie treated as ordinary.
**Cormac McCarthy** (*Suttree*, *Child of God*) — river-bottom squalor, vegetal
decay, sentence rhythm. Use the eye, not the punctuation habits.

## Visual

Wet-plate collodion and Victorian post-mortem/spirit photography: Hippolyte
Bayard's staged drowning, William Mumler's spirit portraits, the long exposure that
turns any movement into a ghost. Worth remembering that the technology *itself*
generated hauntings — a plate could not help but record what stood still longest.

## Deliberately not

Lovecraft (no cosmology, no adjective-stacking), splatterpunk, haunted-house
comedy, anything ironic about the genre.
